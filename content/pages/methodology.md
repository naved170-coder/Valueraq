answer: VALUERAQ estimates a digital business's value by multiplying a base metric (annual net profit, or annual recurring revenue for SaaS and AI) by a multiple. The multiple starts from a published default range for the business type and is adjusted up or down by fixed, published rules for age, growth and risk. The result is a low, midpoint and high value.

## Principles

1. **Transparent.** Every default, rule and formula is published here. Results show each step.
2. **Labelled.** Each figure is marked as *user-provided*, *platform assumption* or *calculated*. AI-generated commentary, where offered, is labelled separately and never changes the numbers.
3. **A range, not a point.** Valuation involves judgement, so results are ranges.
4. **No invented data.** We don't present assumptions as market statistics. See [data sources](/data-sources/).

## Step 1: Base metric

| Business type | Base metric | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Website, app, newsletter, ecommerce | Annual net profit (SDE) | Average monthly net profit × 12 |
| SaaS, AI business | Annual recurring revenue (ARR) | Current MRR × 12 |

[SDE](/glossary/sde/) is profit before the owner's own pay. For businesses valued on profit, zero or negative profit returns no estimate, because an earnings multiple can't value a loss-making business.

## Step 3: Adjustments

Each input the user provides is matched to a band with a fixed percentage effect on the multiple, listed below. Blank inputs are skipped. Effects multiply together: two adjustments of +8% and −6% combine to ×1.08 × 0.94 = ×1.015. The combined factor is limited to between ×0.5 and ×1.4, so no combination of answers can move the multiple beyond those bounds.

## Step 4: Value range

**Value = base metric × (base multiple × combined adjustment)**, for the low, midpoint and high multiples. Values are rounded to the nearest $100 below $20,000, the nearest $1,000 below $1 million, and the nearest $10,000 above that.

For ecommerce, inventory at cost is shown separately and added to a second range. For SaaS and AI, if profit is entered, the midpoint divided by annual profit is shown as a cross-check.

## Confidence rating

Each result carries a confidence rating (Low, Moderate or Reasonable) based on how many inputs were provided, whether the business has at least two years of history, and whether significant risk factors were found. It describes how much information the estimate rests on, not the probability that a sale will happen at that price.

## Limitations

- **Inputs are not verified.** Results are only as accurate as the figures entered.
- **Default multiples are assumptions.** Real prices vary with buyer type, deal structure, negotiation and market conditions.
- **Not modelled:** deal terms (earn-outs, seller financing), strategic value to a specific buyer, technical quality, legal issues, intangible assets such as brand strength, net revenue retention, and seasonality within the year.
- **Not suitable for:** loss-making or pre-revenue businesses, venture-scale startups valued on future growth, or businesses whose value lies mainly in assets rather than earnings.
- **Not advice.** Estimates are not appraisals, audits, or investment, tax or legal advice.
