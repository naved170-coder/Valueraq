## What {{BRAND}} does, and doesn't do

We review every listing before it goes live, show a [verification status](/verification/) where evidence has been checked, show a **Website ownership confirmed** badge where the seller has proved control of the site, and display automatic checks worked out from the seller's figures.

We aren't a broker or an escrow service, we don't hold money, and we aren't a party to the sale. The decision to buy, and the checks before it, are yours.

## Warning signs

Be careful if a seller:

- wants to be paid directly by bank transfer, cryptocurrency, gift cards or a payment app before anything is transferred;
- pushes you to decide quickly or says other buyers are about to pay;
- asks to move the conversation off {{BRAND}} straight away;
- sends screenshots only and won't give read-only access to the revenue and traffic sources;
- can't show that they control the domain and the main accounts;
- quotes a best month as if it were an average, or can't explain a sudden jump or fall;
- asks an unusually low price for the profit claimed, with no clear reason for selling;
- asks for your passwords, identity documents or card details.

## Before you pay

1. **See the numbers at the source.** Ask for read-only access to the payment processor, ad network or billing system and to analytics, for at least 12 months. Screenshots and spreadsheets can be edited.
2. **Confirm ownership.** Check the listing for the ownership badge, and ask the seller to prove control of the domain and accounts, for example by adding a line of text you choose to the website.
3. **Check what you are buying.** Get a written list of every asset: domain, code, content, accounts, customer lists, contracts and stock, and confirm each can be transferred.
4. **Talk to the seller on a video call** and ask how the business runs day to day.
5. **Use a written agreement.** A [letter of intent](/glossary/letter-of-intent/) and a purchase agreement set out the price, the assets, the handover and what happens if something is untrue. Take legal advice.
6. **Work through a full checklist.** See the [due diligence checklist](/guides/digital-business-due-diligence/).

## Paying safely

Use an independent, well-known [escrow](/glossary/escrow/) service. The buyer pays the escrow service, the seller transfers the assets, the buyer confirms everything works during an agreed inspection period, and only then is the money released. Check the escrow service's web address carefully, since fake escrow websites are a common scam; go to it yourself and don't follow a link the seller sends.

## Protecting your account

- Keep messages on {{BRAND}} until you are confident about the other person. Your email address is shown to a seller only when you send them a message.
- Open files from people you don't know with care, even inside a conversation.
- We never ask for your password by email or message.

## If something looks wrong

[Report the listing](/report-a-listing/) and include the listing number. If you have already paid and think you have been defrauded, contact your bank or card provider and the escrow service straight away, and report it to the police or the fraud-reporting service in your country.
