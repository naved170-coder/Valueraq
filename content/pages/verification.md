## Verification statuses

| Status | What we checked |
|---|---|
| Unverified | The listing passed moderation. Figures are as stated by the seller. |
| Revenue verified | Stated revenue matches read-only access to, or original statements from, the revenue source (payment processor, billing system, affiliate or ad-network dashboard) for at least the last 12 months. |
| Traffic verified | Stated traffic matches read-only analytics access for at least the last 12 months. |
| Fully verified | Revenue and traffic are verified, and the seller has shown control of the domain or main accounts. |

## How verification works

1. The seller requests verification from **My listings** in their account (it's free) and says what evidence they can share. A reviewer then emails them to arrange read-only access or original statements.
2. A reviewer compares the evidence with the listing's figures.
3. If they match within reasonable rounding, the status is applied. If not, the seller is asked to correct the listing.
4. The status applies to the period checked. If the seller later edits their figures, the listing is reviewed again.

## Website ownership

Separately from the statuses above, a listing can show a **Website ownership confirmed** badge. This check is automatic and free:

1. The seller adds the website address to the listing (it stays private).
2. {{BRAND}} gives the seller a unique code to place on that website: as a small text file, a meta tag on the home page, or a DNS record on the domain.
3. Our system looks for the code. If it is there, the badge appears on the listing straight away.

Only someone who controls the website or its domain can place the code, so the badge shows that the seller controlled that website on the date of the check. It says nothing about revenue, traffic or profit, and it is removed if the seller changes the website address on the listing.

## Automatic checks on listings

Each live listing also shows **automatic checks**: notes worked out from the seller's own figures and our published [methodology](/methodology/), such as how the asking price compares with our default multiple range, the profit margin, and the age of the business. They are prompts for a buyer's questions. They are not a verification, because they rely on the figures the seller entered.

## What verification doesn't cover

Verification confirms that specific figures match specific evidence at the time of checking. It doesn't confirm future performance, profit (unless stated), code quality, legal compliance, ownership of every asset, or the absence of undisclosed problems. It doesn't replace a buyer's own [due diligence](/guides/digital-business-due-diligence/).
