## What this tool does

Website valuation is the process of estimating the economic value of a website or online business based on factors such as revenue, profit, traffic, growth, risk and business model. This tool applies that process to one website using the figures you provide. It returns a low, midpoint and high value, the multiple behind each figure, and a line-by-line list of the adjustments that moved the multiple.

It is built for:

- **Owners** who want to know what their site could sell for before listing it or talking to a broker.
- **Buyers** who want to check whether a listing's asking price is supported by its numbers.
- **Advisers and investors** who need a quick, transparent first estimate before deeper due diligence.

## Inputs you need

| Input | Why it matters | Required |
|---|---|---|

| Average monthly net profit (SDE) | The base the multiple is applied to | Yes |
| Business age | Longer histories give buyers more evidence that earnings will last | No |
| Revenue change over the last 12 months | Growing sites are worth more than shrinking ones | No |

| Share of traffic from the largest channel | Heavy dependence on one channel, often Google search, adds risk | No |
| Owner hours per week | A buyer has to replace your time | No |
| Monthly pageviews | Shown in your report for context | No |

Use trailing twelve-month averages rather than your best month. Buyers and brokers normally ask for 12 to 24 months of profit and loss statements, so an estimate built on the same basis is closer to what you will be offered.

## How the valuation is calculated

1. **Annual profit.** Average monthly net profit × 12. Profit here means seller's discretionary earnings (SDE): revenue minus all business costs, before paying yourself.
2. **Base multiple.** Websites start from a default range of [[ex:base_low]] to [[ex:base_high]] annual profit, with a midpoint of [[ex:base_mid]]. This range is a {{BRAND}} platform assumption, not a figure measured from a dataset of sales. See [data sources](/data-sources/).
3. **Adjustments.** Each answer you give can move the multiple up or down by a fixed percentage. The effects multiply together, and the combined adjustment is capped between −50% and +40%.
4. **Value range.** Annual profit × adjusted low, midpoint and high multiples, rounded.

The complete rule table is published on the [methodology page](/methodology/#adjustments).

## What affects a website's value

**Profit and its quality.** Buyers pay for earnings they believe will continue. Profit that depends on one affiliate programme, one ad network or one large sponsor is discounted, as is profit inflated by cutting costs a buyer would need to restore, such as content production.

**Traffic sources.** Many content sites receive most of their visitors from organic search. That traffic can drop sharply after a search algorithm update, so a site with 90% of visits from Google is riskier than one with meaningful email, direct or social traffic. The tool reflects this through the channel-concentration input.

**Age and trend.** A site with several years of stable or rising profit gives a buyer more to rely on than a new site with one strong year.

**Owner workload.** If the site needs 30 hours of your time each week, the buyer must either do that work or pay someone to do it, which lowers what they can pay.

**Transferability.** Documented processes, content you own outright, contractors willing to stay on, and clean analytics all make a sale easier and support a higher price. The tool doesn't score these directly; they're covered in the [due diligence guide](/guides/digital-business-due-diligence/).

## Limitations

- The estimate is only as accurate as the figures entered. The tool does not connect to your analytics or accounts.
- The base multiple range is an editorial assumption. Real sale prices vary widely with deal terms, buyer type and market conditions.
- It does not value loss-making sites, sites sold mainly for their domain name, or strategic acquisitions where a buyer pays for synergy.
- It does not account for earn-outs, seller financing or other deal structures, which change the headline price.
- It is not an appraisal and not financial, tax or legal advice.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much is my website worth?

Most small profitable websites are valued as a multiple of their annual net profit. With {{BRAND}}'s default assumptions, that is roughly 2.5× to 4× annual profit before adjustments for age, growth and risk. Enter your own figures above to see the range for your site and the reasons behind it.

### Why does the tool use profit instead of traffic?

Buyers pay for the income a site produces. Traffic matters because it drives that income and because its sources carry risk, so traffic concentration adjusts the multiple, but pageviews alone don't determine value. Two sites with the same traffic can earn very different amounts.

### What counts as net profit?

Revenue minus every cost of running the site (hosting, content, software, contractors, fees), before you pay yourself. This is usually called seller's discretionary earnings, or SDE. Add back one-off costs only if you can document them.

### Can I value a website with no revenue?

Not with this tool. Pre-revenue sites are usually priced on the cost of rebuilding them, their content, domain and audience, or strategic fit, which an earnings-based model can't estimate reliably.

### Is my data saved?

No. Figures are used to calculate the result and are not stored unless you create a free account and save a report. Saved reports are private and are never shown to search engines.
