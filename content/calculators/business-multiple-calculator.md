## What the business multiple calculator shows

Enter an asking price and at least one of annual revenue or annual profit. The calculator returns:

| Result | Formula | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue multiple | Price ÷ annual revenue | How many years of revenue the price represents |
| Profit multiple | Price ÷ annual net profit | How many years of profit the price represents |
| Months of profit | Price ÷ monthly profit | The same as the profit multiple, in months |
| Simple payback | Price ÷ annual profit | Years for profit to repay the price, ignoring tax, growth and financing |
| Profit margin | Profit ÷ revenue | How much of each dollar of revenue is kept |

If you choose a business type, the calculator states whether the implied multiple is below, within or above {{BRAND}}'s default range for that type. The default ranges are editorial assumptions, not market data, and are listed on the [methodology page](/methodology/).

## When to use it

- **Buyers:** check a listing before contacting the seller. A profit multiple well above the typical range needs a reason, such as fast growth or strategic value.
- **Sellers:** test an asking price before listing, and prepare to explain it.
- **Anyone comparing listings:** multiples make businesses of different sizes comparable.

## Example

A website listed at $300,000 with $120,000 annual revenue and $90,000 annual net profit:

- Revenue multiple: $300,000 ÷ $120,000 = 2.5×
- Profit multiple: $300,000 ÷ $90,000 = 3.33×
- Months of profit: $300,000 ÷ $7,500 = 40 months
- Simple payback: about 3.3 years
- Profit margin: 75%

Compared with {{BRAND}}'s default website range of 2.5× to 4× annual profit, a 3.33× multiple sits within the range.

## Reading the payback period carefully

Simple payback ignores taxes, the cost of financing, the time you'll spend running the business, growth or decline, and the risk that profit falls after the sale. It is a quick sense-check, not a return-on-investment analysis. A buyer paying 3.3× profit is also buying the risk that the business doesn't hold its earnings.

**Worked example.** A content site is listed at $150,000 with $36,000 yearly profit. The calculator shows 4.2× profit and a payback of about 4.2 years. That is above the default high of 4.0×, so the seller needs strong reasons: long history, growth, several traffic sources, little owner time.

## If the multiple looks high

- Ask what justifies it, and check those claims in [due diligence](/guides/digital-business-due-diligence/).
- Run the full valuation tool for that business type to see which factors support or undermine the price.
- Propose a structure instead of a lower headline price: part paid later through an earn-out, or a holdback until figures are confirmed. See [deal structures](/guides/deal-structures-for-online-business-sales/).

## Limitations

The calculator does only arithmetic. It can't tell whether the seller's revenue and profit figures are accurate; that is the job of [due diligence](/guides/digital-business-due-diligence/). Deal terms such as earn-outs, seller financing and inventory also change what a price really means.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I compare profit or revenue multiples?

Profit multiples, for most small digital businesses, because they reflect what the owner actually keeps. Revenue multiples are more common for SaaS and for businesses investing heavily in growth. See [revenue vs profit multiples](/guides/revenue-vs-profit-multiples/).

### What if profit is zero or negative?

The profit multiple and payback can't be calculated. The revenue multiple is still shown, but loss-making businesses are usually priced on other grounds, such as assets, audience or strategic fit.

### Should I use an SDE multiple or an EBITDA multiple?

For small, owner-run online businesses, buyers usually use profit before the owner's pay (SDE). EBITDA is more common for larger businesses with paid managers. Use the same measure when you compare two listings.

### When is a revenue multiple the right one?

Mainly for subscription software, where revenue is recurring and predictable. For content sites, stores and newsletters, compare profit multiples.
