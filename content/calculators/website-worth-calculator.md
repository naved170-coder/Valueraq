## How the website worth calculator works

**Formula:** website value = average monthly net profit × months of profit.

The "months of profit" figure is the same thing as an annual multiple expressed differently: 36 months of profit equals 3× annual profit. Website marketplaces and brokers often quote monthly multiples, while accountants and investors usually quote annual ones. The table in the result shows both.

This calculator doesn't choose the multiple for you. It is useful when you already have a multiple in mind, for example from a broker's opinion or from an offer, and want to see what it means in dollars. To have a multiple estimated from your site's age, growth, traffic concentration and workload, use the [Website Valuation Tool](/tools/website-valuation/).

## What monthly profit to use

Use your average monthly **net profit over the last 12 months**, not your best recent month. Net profit here is revenue minus every cost of running the site (content, hosting, software, contractors, fees) before paying yourself. This is often called [seller's discretionary earnings](/glossary/sde/).

If the site is seasonal, such as a site about gifts or tax, a 12-month average is especially important, because a single month can be far above or below normal.

## Choosing a multiple

There's no universal correct multiple. As a starting point, {{BRAND}}'s [methodology](/methodology/) assumes 30 to 48 months (2.5× to 4× annual profit) for an established website before adjustments. Within any range, the factors that usually push a site higher are:

- a long, stable or growing earnings history;
- traffic from several sources rather than one;
- revenue from several monetization methods;
- little owner time needed each week;
- clean records that a buyer can verify.

Factors that usually push it lower include declining traffic, recent search-ranking losses, dependence on a single affiliate programme, and a short history.

## Example

A site with an average monthly net profit of $4,500 valued at 36 months:

$4,500 × 36 = $162,000, which is 3× its annual profit of $54,000.

At 30 months the same site would be $135,000; at 42 months, $189,000. The spread shows why the reasoning behind the multiple matters as much as the arithmetic.

## Limitations

The result is simple arithmetic on the numbers you enter. It doesn't check whether the multiple is realistic for your site, and it isn't an appraisal or financial advice. Sale prices also depend on deal terms, such as earn-outs or payment in instalments, which can make a higher headline price worth less in practice.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good multiple for a website?

It depends on the site's age, growth, traffic sources, monetization and how much work it needs. {{BRAND}}'s default range for established websites is 30 to 48 months of profit before adjustments. The [Website Valuation Tool](/tools/website-valuation/) estimates a range for your site specifically.

### Is a monthly multiple the same as an annual multiple?

They describe the same valuation. Divide a monthly multiple by 12 to get the annual multiple: 36 months equals 3× annual profit.

### Should I use revenue or profit?

Profit. Two sites with the same revenue can have very different costs. See [revenue vs profit multiples](/guides/revenue-vs-profit-multiples/) for when revenue multiples are used instead.
