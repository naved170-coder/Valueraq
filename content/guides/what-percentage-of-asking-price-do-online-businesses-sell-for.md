## The short answer

BizBuySell's benchmark page for website and ecommerce businesses covers 1,661 sold listings from 2021 to 2025. It reports a median sale price of $825,000 and a median asking price of $850,000. Divide one by the other and sales closed at about 97% of the asking price in that group: $825,000 / $850,000 = 0.9706.

Two cautions. These are medians of different listings, so they are not the same as the average gap on any one deal. And BizBuySell's listings skew to larger, broker-sold businesses, so a small site may sell differently. Treat 97% as one data point, not a rule.

## Judge the asking price, not just the gap

A seller can set an asking price at any level. What matters is whether the price fits the profit. Divide the asking price by yearly profit to get the multiple, then compare it with a range.

Example: a site is listed at $100,000 and earns $30,000 a year. $100,000 / $30,000 = 3.3 times profit, which sits inside {{BRAND}}'s default website range of 2.5 to 4.0 times. A listing at $150,000 would be 5.0 times, above that range, so you would need strong reasons to pay it.

| Asking price | Yearly profit | Multiple | Against the default range (2.5 to 4.0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| $75,000 | $30,000 | 2.5x | Bottom of the range |
| $100,000 | $30,000 | 3.3x | Inside the range |
| $150,000 | $30,000 | 5.0x | Above the range |

The default range is a VALUERAQ assumption, not market data. See the [methodology](/methodology/).

## How far below asking to offer

No percentage is right for every deal. Base your offer on three things: the multiple the asking price implies, what your checks find, and what is included. Problems you find in due diligence, such as lower real profit or a traffic risk, are the reason to offer less, and you should be ready to explain them.
