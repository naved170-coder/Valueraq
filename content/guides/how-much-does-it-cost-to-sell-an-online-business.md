## The short answer

There is no single price for selling an online business, because the cost is a list of items and your route decides which ones apply. A commission to a broker or marketplace is the biggest item for many sellers, but it is not the only one. Escrow, legal advice, accounting and tax all sit on top, and the tax figure depends on your own situation.

The useful question is not "what do sellers pay?" but "what will I keep?" Add up the items below for your route, then subtract the total from your asking price.

## The cost list

| Cost | When it applies | How to find the amount |
|---|---|---|
| Broker or marketplace commission | If you sell through one. Often a percentage of the price (a [success fee](/glossary/success-fee/)) | Ask for the rate in writing before you sign |
| Listing fee | Some routes charge to list | Check the route's published terms |
| Escrow fee | Almost every sale with a stranger | Ask the [escrow](/glossary/escrow/) provider; agree who pays |
| Legal | Contract and review by a lawyer | Get a quote |
| Accounting | Records, add-backs, tax planning | Get a quote |
| Payment and transfer charges | Bank or card fees; currency conversion | Ask your bank |
| Tax on the sale | Depends on your country and how you own the business | Ask your accountant for the figure |
| Your own time | Handover, buyer questions, a [transition period](/glossary/transition-period/) | Plan for it |

The U.S. Small Business Administration advises sellers to have an attorney review the sales agreement, and says to consider hiring an attorney and an accountant, so allow for both.

## Worked example: $120,000 sale, two routes

The costs below are invented to show the arithmetic. They are not market rates.

**Route A: a broker charging a 10% commission (invented rate).**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Sale price | $120,000 |
| Commission, 10% | −$12,000 |
| Escrow fee | −$1,200 |
| Legal | −$2,000 |
| Accounting | −$800 |
| **Left before tax** | **$104,000** |

**Route B: a direct sale or a free listing with no commission.** {{BRAND}} lists businesses for free and takes no commission when one sells, so the commission line is $0. The other costs are the same:

$120,000 − $1,200 − $2,000 − $800 = $116,000 before tax.

The gap is $116,000 − $104,000 = $12,000, which is the commission. A no-commission route still leaves the escrow, legal and accounting costs, and you do more of the selling yourself. [Broker vs marketplace vs selling directly](/guides/ways-to-sell-a-digital-business/) compares what the cost buys.
