## The short answer

You pay for what the site earns, not for what it cost to build. Buyers of small online businesses usually start from yearly net profit and apply a multiple. {{BRAND}}'s default range for a website is **2.5 to 4.0 times yearly profit**, with a midpoint of 3.2. These are default assumptions, not market data, and the real price depends on traffic, age, growth and how much work the owner does.

If you searched for the cost of a website and found pages about building one, this page is about the other case: paying a seller for a site that already has visitors and income.

## What the price looks like at three sizes

The table applies the default range to yearly profit. The examples are illustrative.

| Yearly net profit | Low (2.5x) | Midpoint (3.2x) | High (4.0x) |
|---|---|---|---|
| $12,000 | $30,000 | $38,400 | $48,000 |
| $30,000 | $75,000 | $96,000 | $120,000 |
| $90,000 | $225,000 | $288,000 | $360,000 |

Worked example for the middle row: $30,000 x 2.5 = $75,000, $30,000 x 3.2 = $96,000 and $30,000 x 4.0 = $120,000.

"Profit" here means profit after the real cost of running the site, including any work the owner does unpaid. See [how to calculate SDE](/guides/how-to-calculate-sde/) for how sellers arrive at the number, and [seven worked examples](/guides/website-worth-examples/) for different site types.

## Costs on top of the price

Budget for these before agreeing a price. Ask for a quote on each rather than assuming a figure.

- **Escrow or payment-holding service.** Money is held until the transfer is complete. See [how escrow works](/guides/how-escrow-works-for-website-sales/).
- **Legal review.** The U.S. Small Business Administration suggests you "consider hiring an attorney and an accountant" when buying a business.
- **Moving the site.** Hosting, domain, accounts and any one-off developer time.
- **Working capital.** Money to cover the first months of costs, such as writers, tools and ads, before income settles.
- **Fixes you find.** Outdated software, thin pages or missing backups.

## Why two similar sites can cost very different amounts

- **Growth.** A site with rising profit usually earns a higher multiple than one in decline.
- **Reliance on one source.** A site with most visits from one search engine or one affiliate program carries more risk.
- **Owner hours.** A site that needs 40 hours a week is worth less than one that runs in 5.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the asking price the price I pay?

No. It is where negotiation starts. Check it against the profit and the checks you complete. [What is included when you buy a website](/guides/what-is-included-when-you-buy-a-website/) lists what the price should cover.

### Can a website be bought for a few hundred dollars?

Very small or new sites may be listed cheaply, but with little or no profit there is nothing to apply a multiple to. Buyers treat them as a starting point to build on, not as an earning business.
