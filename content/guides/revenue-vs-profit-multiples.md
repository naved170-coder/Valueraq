## Key takeaway

Both multiples describe the same price from different angles. **Revenue multiple = price ÷ annual revenue. Profit multiple = price ÷ annual profit.** Which one is meaningful depends on the business model and on how much of its revenue turns into profit.

## Side-by-side comparison

| | Revenue multiple | Profit multiple |
|---|---|---|
| Formula | Price ÷ annual revenue (or ARR) | Price ÷ annual profit (SDE or EBITDA) |
| Typical use | SaaS, AI SaaS, subscription businesses | Websites, apps, newsletters, ecommerce, owner-run SaaS |
| Strength | Works when profit is low because of growth spending | Reflects what the buyer actually earns |
| Weakness | Ignores costs; high-revenue, low-margin businesses look better than they are | Can undervalue fast-growing businesses reinvesting profit |
| Sensitive to | Growth, churn, gross margin | Earnings quality, owner workload, risk |

## Why profit multiples dominate for small online businesses

Most small websites, stores, newsletters and apps are bought by individuals or small operators who will live on, or reinvest, the profit. For them, profit is the return on their purchase. A profit multiple shows directly how many years of current profit they're paying for.

## Why SaaS often uses revenue

Recurring subscription revenue is predictable, and growing SaaS businesses often spend heavily on product and marketing, which keeps profit low. A revenue multiple lets buyers value that recurring base and its growth. Many buyers of small SaaS still check the profit multiple, and {{BRAND}}'s [SaaS tool](/tools/saas-valuation/) shows both.

## Converting between them

Profit margin links the two:

**Profit multiple = revenue multiple ÷ profit margin.**

A business priced at 2× revenue with a 25% margin is priced at 2 ÷ 0.25 = 8× profit. The same 2× revenue for a business with an 80% margin is only 2.5× profit. This is why a revenue multiple means little without the margin next to it.

## Example

Two businesses are both listed at $400,000:

| | Business A: content site | Business B: online store |
|---|---|---|
| Annual revenue | $160,000 | $800,000 |
| Annual profit | $120,000 | $120,000 |
| Margin | 75% | 15% |
| Revenue multiple | 2.5× | 0.5× |
| Profit multiple | 3.3× | 3.3× |

On revenue, the store looks five times cheaper. On profit, which is what a buyer keeps, the prices are identical. The store also needs inventory and carries more operational risk, so a buyer might argue for a lower profit multiple.

## Common mistakes

- **Comparing revenue multiples across different business models.** Margins differ too much.
- **Quoting a SaaS revenue multiple for a content site**, or the other way round.
- **Mixing monthly and annual figures.** A "40× multiple" on a website listing is usually 40 months of profit, or 3.3× annual profit.
- **Using gross profit in place of net profit** without saying so.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which multiple should I use for my business?

Use a profit multiple for most websites, apps, newsletters and stores. Use an ARR multiple for SaaS and AI subscription businesses, and check the profit multiple it implies.

### What's a good profit multiple?

It depends on the business's age, growth and risk. {{BRAND}}'s default starting ranges are published in the [methodology](/methodology/).

### Is EBITDA a profit multiple?

Yes. An EBITDA multiple is a profit multiple based on EBITDA instead of SDE. It's more common for larger businesses with management teams. See [EBITDA](/glossary/ebitda/) and [SDE](/glossary/sde/).
