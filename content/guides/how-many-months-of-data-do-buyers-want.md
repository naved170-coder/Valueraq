## The short answer

Plan for 12 months at the least. A full year is the usual starting point because it shows every season once and gives a buyer a [trailing twelve months](/glossary/trailing-twelve-months/) figure to base an offer on. If you have 24 months, offer those too. {{BRAND}}'s guides on [what documents buyers ask for](/guides/what-documents-do-buyers-ask-for/) and the [due diligence checklist](/guides/digital-business-due-diligence/) both use 12 months as the minimum and 24 as the better case.

This is a general guide, not a rule. A buyer may ask for more, and a very young business will simply have less to show.

## Why 12 months is the usual minimum

- **Seasons.** Many businesses sell more at some times of year, and ad rates move too. Twelve months shows a full cycle, so one strong or weak month does not set the price.
- **A fair average.** {{BRAND}}'s tools ask for average monthly revenue and profit over the last 12 months, and compare the latest three months with the same three months a year earlier. That is the basis of the [methodology](/methodology/).
- **Trust.** A buyer cannot check a trend from three good months. A full year of figures that match the bank and the payment reports is far more convincing.

## When 24 months helps

Two years let a buyer compare each month with the same month a year earlier. That protects a seller whose latest quarter was quiet, and it protects a buyer from a one-off spike. If the business has changed a lot, such as a price rise, a new product or a drop in search traffic, the longer record shows the before and after.

## What to include for each month

1. Revenue by source, such as ads, affiliate links, product sales or subscriptions.
2. Costs, listed by type, with invoices for the large ones.
3. Net profit, worked out the way you will describe it in the listing. The steps are in [how to calculate SDE](/guides/how-to-calculate-sde/).
4. Traffic or customer counts from the same months.
5. Payment processor and bank records that match the figures. See [how to verify website revenue](/guides/how-to-verify-website-revenue/).

## What if you have less than 12 months?

Share what you have, say plainly that the history is short, and expect a lower price. In {{BRAND}}'s default rules, a business under one year old has 20% taken off its multiple, because a short record gives a buyer little evidence that earnings will last.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is 6 months enough to sell a website?

You can list it, but buyers have much less to go on and will usually offer less or ask for more proof. Twelve months is the safer plan.

### Should I include a bad month?

Yes. A buyer will find it in the records anyway, and a clear explanation builds more trust than a gap.

### Do I need tax returns as well?

Buyers often ask for them as a second check on profit. See [what documents buyers ask for](/guides/what-documents-do-buyers-ask-for/) for the full list.
