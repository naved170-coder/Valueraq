## Key takeaway

Buyers pay for earnings they can verify and believe will continue. Most of the work of selling a digital business is making that easy: clean numbers, documented operations, and fewer single points of failure. The earlier you start, the more you can improve the multiple you're offered.

## Step 1: Value the business honestly

Use your trailing 12-month average profit (or ARR for SaaS) and the [valuation tool](/tools/) for your business type. Look at which adjustments pull your multiple down. They're what buyers will focus on, and many can be improved before you sell.

## Step 2: Prepare your records

Buyers will ask for:

- **Monthly profit and loss statements** for 12 to 24 months, reconciled to bank and payment-processor deposits.
- **Revenue source reports**: affiliate, ad-network, billing system, app store or marketplace dashboards.
- **Traffic or usage data**: analytics access, search-console data, active users, email list statistics.
- **A list of every expense**, tool subscription and contractor.
- **Proof of ownership** for domains, trademarks, code and content.

If your personal and business finances are mixed, separate them now and keep a clean record from this point on.

## Step 3: Reduce the risks buyers discount for

| Risk | What to do before selling |
|---|---|
| One traffic or sales channel dominates | Build a second channel, such as an email list or another marketplace |
| One customer, sponsor or affiliate programme dominates | Add others; document long-term relationships |
| You do everything | Document processes; hand routine work to contractors |
| Revenue is declining | Stabilise it before listing, or price in the decline honestly |
| Messy code or content ownership | Clean up dependencies; confirm licences and authorship |

## Step 4: Decide how to sell

- **Marketplace listing**, such as the [{{BRAND}} marketplace](/businesses-for-sale/): you control the process and pay no commission to list here.
- **Broker**: handles marketing, buyer screening and negotiation for a commission. Often worthwhile for larger or complex sales.
- **Direct approach** to a strategic buyer, such as a competitor or a company in an adjacent market.

## Step 5: Write a listing that answers buyers' questions

A good listing states the business model, trailing 12-month revenue and profit, age, traffic or customer sources, owner hours, what's included, and why you're selling. Be specific and factual. Listings with complete figures get more serious enquiries and pass {{BRAND}}'s review faster. Keep your website address private until a buyer is qualified; our listing form does this by default.

## Step 6: Handle enquiries and due diligence

Qualify buyers before sharing sensitive details: ask about their budget, experience and plans, and consider a non-disclosure agreement. Offer read-only access to analytics and revenue dashboards rather than screenshots. Fast, complete answers keep buyers confident.

## Step 7: Negotiate terms, not just price

Consider payment structure (upfront, instalments, earn-out), your transition support, a non-compete period, what happens to inventory, and who pays escrow fees. An earn-out can raise the headline price but delays and conditions part of your payment. Take tax and legal advice on the structure before you sign.

## Step 8: Close and hand over

Use escrow. Transfer assets in an agreed order, keep a checklist, and stay available during the transition period you agreed. A smooth handover protects any earn-out and your reputation.

## Common mistakes sellers make

- Pricing on a best month or on revenue rather than profit.
- Listing while revenue is falling, without explaining why.
- Being slow or vague in due diligence.
- Changing the business significantly during the sale.
- Forgetting to agree, in writing, exactly what's included.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to sell an online business?

It varies with price, quality and demand. Preparation often takes longer than the sale itself. Small, well-documented businesses can sell in weeks; larger ones typically take a few months.

### What multiple can I expect?

It depends on the business's age, growth and risks. See [how digital businesses are valued](/guides/business-valuation/) and run the [tool](/tools/) for your type.

### Should I tell my customers I'm selling?

Usually not until the sale is agreed, and then in coordination with the buyer. Some B2B contracts require notice or consent, so check yours.

### Do I pay tax when I sell?

Most likely, but it depends on where you live, how the business is owned and how the sale is structured. Speak to a tax adviser before agreeing terms.
