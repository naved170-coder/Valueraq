## The short answer

A website changes hands in two parts: the agreement and the handover. Write down exactly what is being sold, pay through an independent [escrow](/glossary/escrow/) service, and move the assets in an agreed order so the site keeps working throughout. Most small website sales are an [asset sale](/glossary/asset-sale/): the buyer receives the site and its accounts, not the seller's company.

## What gets transferred

List every asset in the purchase agreement. Typical items are:

| Asset | What the buyer needs |
|---|---|
| Domain name | Control of the registrar account, or a transfer to the buyer's own account |
| Hosting | Access to the hosting account, or a backup restored on the buyer's hosting |
| Site files and database | A full copy, or the code repository |
| Content and images | The files, plus proof of who owns them and any image licences |
| Analytics and search tools | Access moved to the buyer's own account |
| Revenue accounts | Ad network, affiliate and payment accounts, where their rules allow a change of owner |
| Email list and social accounts | Exports or ownership of the accounts, if included |
| Documents | Instructions for the regular work, and contractor details |

Check each platform's rules. Some ad and affiliate programs do not allow accounts to be passed on, so the buyer may need to apply for their own and the seller should say so before the sale.

## The order of the handover

1. **Agree the list and the order** in writing before any money moves.
2. **Pay the escrow provider.** The buyer pays the independent provider, not the seller.
3. **Give access to hosting and the site files.** Take a full backup first.
4. **Transfer the domain.** Remove the transfer lock at the old registrar, send the authorization code, and the buyer accepts it. Domain transfers can take days, so start early.
5. **Move analytics, email and revenue accounts.**
6. **Share the documentation** and introduce the buyer to any contractors.
7. **The buyer checks everything.** Pages load, forms and links work, the revenue dashboards show the right data, and the domain is in their name.
8. **Escrow releases the money** once the buyer confirms.

A worked check: if the agreed price is $60,000 and the buyer has paid it into escrow, the seller should not transfer the domain until the provider confirms the full $60,000 is held. {{BRAND}} is not a broker or an escrow service and does not handle the purchase price.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I transfer the domain first?

No. Transfer it after the buyer's money is confirmed as held in escrow, and keep a backup of the site until the buyer has confirmed everything works.

### How do I avoid downtime?

Copy the site to its new hosting and test it before changing the domain's settings. Then point the domain across in one step.

### Do I need a lawyer?

A written purchase agreement should list every asset, and structure affects tax and liability. Take professional advice before you sign.
