## The short answer

A buyer reads a listing to decide one thing: is this worth a message? Give them what they need to say yes or no quickly. Put the numbers first, describe the business in plain words, be open about the weak spots, and say exactly what the buyer gets.

## What to include, in order

| Part of the listing | What to write | Why a buyer cares |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | The business type, what it sells and one key figure, such as yearly profit | They can sort it in seconds |
| Figures | Average monthly revenue and profit for the last 12 months, and the trend | Profit drives the price |
| How it earns | The sources of income and roughly how they split | Concentration is a risk |
| Time to run | The hours per week you spend and what you do in them | Owner hours change the value |
| What is included | Domain, accounts, code, content, stock, customer lists, social profiles | Avoids surprises later |
| Why you are selling | One honest sentence | A vague reason raises suspicion |
| Known risks | Reliance on one channel, one supplier or one big customer | Buyers find these anyway |
| Asking price | A number, ideally backed by a valuation range | See [asking price](/glossary/asking-price/) |

## Write facts, not adjectives

"Growing fast" says nothing. "Revenue rose from $3,000 to $4,200 a month over the last 12 months" can be checked. Use words like "passive" and "hands-off" only if the hours you list back them up.

Do not guess. If you do not know a figure, leave it out or say it is an estimate. A listing that claims a result it cannot prove will lose the buyer at the first question.

## Keep private details private

Leave out the website address, customer names and anything that identifies the business until a buyer has contacted you and you are comfortable sharing it. ## Frequently asked questions

### How long should a business listing be?

Long enough to answer a buyer's first questions, usually a few short paragraphs plus the key figures. Cut anything that is not a fact or a clear description.

### Should I put the price in the listing?

Yes. A stated price saves time for both sides. Work it out first with a free tool so you can explain it, and treat the result as an estimate, not a guarantee of what a buyer will pay.

### Should I mention the problems?

Yes. A buyer's due diligence will find them. Stating them first shows you are straightforward and keeps the later price talk shorter.
