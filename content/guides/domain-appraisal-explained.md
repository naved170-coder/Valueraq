## Key takeaway

A domain appraisal and a website valuation answer two different questions. An appraisal asks what a **name** might fetch from someone who wants it. A valuation asks what a **business** is worth to someone who wants its earnings. If your site makes money, the second question matters far more, and it is answered with profit and a multiple, not with the name.

{{BRAND}} values earning websites, newsletters, apps, stores, SaaS and AI businesses. It does not appraise bare domain names, and the tools will not give a figure for a site with no profit. This guide explains how appraisals work, where they fall short, and how to value the website that sits on the domain.

## What a domain appraisal is

A domain appraisal is an estimated price for a domain name. Free appraisal tools and paid appraisers both start from features of the name itself. The usual inputs are:

- **Length.** Short names are scarcer.
- **Extension.** A .com is generally the most sought after, with other extensions varying in demand.
- **Words.** A name made of common words that describe a product or an industry can be worth more than an invented word.
- **Readability.** Names that are easy to say, spell and remember are easier to resell.
- **Hyphens and numbers.** These usually count against a name.
- **Age and history.** A long-registered name with a clean history is preferred over one that has been penalized or used for spam.
- **Search interest.** Whether people search for the words in the name.

An automated tool turns those features into a number. A human appraiser may add comparisons with past sales of similar names. In both cases the output is an opinion about price.

## What the number means, and what it does not

The U.S. Internal Revenue Service defines fair market value in Publication 561 as "the price that property would sell for on the open market." An appraisal tries to estimate that price, but until a buyer and seller actually agree, nobody knows it. Treat a free appraisal as a rough guide. Three limits matter:

1. **It cannot see demand.** A name is worth what one particular buyer will pay. A tool cannot tell whether that buyer exists.
2. **Different tools give different answers.** The same name can receive widely different estimates, because each tool weights features its own way.
3. **Most names are not bought at the estimate.** An appraisal is not an offer. Many names never sell at all.

If you are asked to pay for an appraisal, ask what data it is based on and whether the figure comes from named, checkable sales.

## A bare domain versus a website with earnings

| | Bare domain | Website with earnings |
|---|---|---|
| What the buyer gets | A name and its registration | A name, content, audience, income and processes |
| What drives price | Features of the name and a buyer's plans | Yearly profit, trend, risk and owner effort |
| Method | Comparison and judgment | Profit multiple, cross-checked |
| Can it be checked? | Only against past sales of other names | Against bank, payout and analytics records |
| Handled by | Domain marketplaces and brokers | Business marketplaces such as {{BRAND}} |

The two are not exclusive. A strong name helps a website, for example through direct visits or easy recall. But once a site earns real money, buyers spend little time on the name and a great deal on the income. The U.S. Small Business Administration lists intangible assets such as brand presence, intellectual property, customer information and projected future revenue as things to value when selling a business. The name is one part of that brand.

## How to value the website instead

The usual working formula for a small website is **value = yearly profit × multiple**, where the profit is [SDE](/glossary/sde/): the profit left after all costs, before the owner is paid.

{{BRAND}}'s default range for a website is **2.5 to 4.0 times yearly profit**, with 3.2 as the midpoint. It is a published platform assumption, not measured market data. The reasoning and every adjustment are set out in the [methodology](/methodology/).

### A worked example

A website earns $2,000 a month after all costs, before the owner is paid. The domain is a short .com that an appraisal tool would value at a few thousand dollars.

- **Yearly profit:** $2,000 × 12 = $24,000.
- **At 2.5 times:** $24,000 × 2.5 = $60,000.
- **At 3.2 times:** $24,000 × 3.2 = $76,800.
- **At 4.0 times:** $24,000 × 4.0 = $96,000.

![Price on $24,000 of yearly profit: Low $60,000, Midpoint $76,800, High $96,000](/static/img/articles/domain-appraisal-explained-price-on-24k-profit.svg){: width="720" height="226" loading="lazy" }

The name may nudge the site toward the top of the range, but the profit supplies almost all of the value. A site that earns nothing has nothing for the multiple to act on, however good the name. In that case the value rests on the name, the content and the audience, which is an appraisal and negotiation question, not a multiple.

## Reading an appraisal sensibly

If you run a name through a free tool, do three things with the result. First, run it through two or three tools and note the spread, because a wide gap tells you the figure is soft. Second, search for what the words in the name mean to a buyer: does a company or a product category plainly need this name? Third, decide your own walk-away price before any conversation starts. An appraisal should help you set that price, not replace your judgment of it. If you are selling a name alone, expect the sale to take as long as it takes to find the one buyer who needs it.

## What moves a website's value besides the name

- **Trend.** Rising profit supports a higher multiple and falling profit lowers it.
- **Age.** A longer record is easier to believe.
- **Traffic sources.** A site that gets nearly all its visits from one search engine or one channel carries more risk than one with several sources.
- **Owner hours.** A business that needs the owner every day is harder to hand over.
- **Income sources.** Ads, affiliate links, products and leads each have different risks.

## When an appraisal is useful

An appraisal is still helpful in a few cases:

- **Buying or selling a bare name** with no site or income.
- **Deciding whether to renew** a name you hold but do not use.
- **Setting a starting point** for talks, with the understanding that it is only a starting point.
- **Checking an asking price** that seems far from any reason.

For a formal figure needed for tax, a loan, a legal dispute or a partner buyout, hire a qualified professional. The SBA also advises having an attorney review the sales agreement.

## How to sell a domain together with its website

When you sell the whole business, the domain is transferred as part of it. Before listing, confirm that you own the registration, that the account email is one you control, and that no transfer lock or renewal issue will delay the handover. ## Mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | A better approach |
|---|---|
| Treating a free appraisal as a price | Use it as a rough guide and check it against other tools |
| Valuing an earning site on its name | Value it on profit, then consider the name a small extra |
| Quoting one tool's figure as fact | Compare several, and ask what each is based on |
| Ignoring renewal and transfer details | Check registrar status before you list |
| Expecting a multiple to work on zero profit | Use an asset or audience approach instead |

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a free domain appraisal accurate?

It is an estimate from the features of the name. Free tools can differ from each other by a large margin, and none of them can tell you what a particular buyer will pay. Use the result as a guide only.

### Does {{BRAND}} appraise domain names?

No. {{BRAND}} values earning online businesses. The Website Valuation Tool needs a positive profit and will not give a figure for a bare domain.

### Does a good domain raise my website's value?

It can help, mainly by making the site easier to remember and trust. But the profit, the trend and the risks usually decide the multiple, so do not expect the name alone to move the price much.

### What if my site earns very little?

Then a profit multiple gives a small number. Buyers may value the content, audience and name instead, and the price depends on what the specific buyer plans to do with them. See [how to value a business with no profit](/guides/how-to-value-a-business-with-no-profit/).

### Do I need a professional appraiser?

Not to set expectations or check an asking price. For tax, lending or legal use, yes.
