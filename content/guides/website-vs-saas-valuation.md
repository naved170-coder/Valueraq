## Key takeaway

The difference comes down to how predictable the revenue is. SaaS subscription revenue repeats each month until a customer cancels, so buyers value the recurring base and its direction. Website revenue depends on traffic and third-party monetization, which can change quickly, so buyers value current profit and discount for traffic and platform risk.

## Comparison at a glance

| | Website | SaaS |
|---|---|---|
| Usual base metric | Annual net profit (SDE) | ARR, cross-checked against profit |
| {{BRAND}} default range | 2.5× to 4× annual profit | 2× to 5× ARR |
| Biggest value drivers | Profit stability, traffic sources, monetization mix | Growth, churn, gross margin |
| Typical risks | Search-engine updates, affiliate or ad-rate changes | Churn, competition, technical debt, key-person dependence |
| Evidence buyers want | Analytics access, search-console data, affiliate/ad dashboards | Billing-system exports, cohort data, code review |
| Owner workload | Content production and SEO | Development, support, sales |

The default ranges are published platform assumptions; see the [methodology](/methodology/).

## Why the base metric differs

A website's revenue comes from visitors who each arrive fresh; nothing obliges them to return. Profit is the clearest measure of what the site produces now. A SaaS customer, by contrast, has an ongoing subscription. As long as churn is low, today's MRR is a good guide to next month's, so revenue itself carries information about the future.

## Why the multiples don't compare directly

A 3× multiple means different things in each case. For a website, 3× annual profit means paying for three years of current profit. For SaaS, 3× ARR might be 10× profit or more if margins are modest, or less than 4× profit if margins are high. Always compare like with like, and use the conversion in [revenue vs profit multiples](/guides/revenue-vs-profit-multiples/).

## Example

Two hypothetical businesses, both earning $10,000 a month in revenue:

- **Website:** $7,500 monthly profit ($90,000 a year), four years old, 60% of traffic from search. At the website default midpoint of 3.2× profit, and before other adjustments, that's about $288,000.
- **SaaS:** $10,000 MRR ($120,000 ARR), $6,000 monthly profit, 20% annual growth, 2.5% monthly churn. At the SaaS default midpoint of 3.5× ARR, and before other adjustments, about $420,000, roughly 5.8× its $72,000 annual profit.

The SaaS business earns less profit but is valued higher, because buyers expect its recurring revenue to continue and grow. That premium only holds if churn and growth are verified.

## Hybrid businesses

Some websites sell subscriptions, and some SaaS products rely on content marketing for most of their sign-ups. Value the business on the model that produces most of its profit, and treat the other as a risk or an upside. A content site with a small paid membership is still mainly a website; a SaaS product whose sign-ups come almost entirely from its blog carries search-traffic risk as well as churn risk.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are SaaS businesses always worth more than websites?

No. A SaaS business with high churn, falling revenue or poor code can be worth less than a stable, low-maintenance website with the same profit.

### Which is easier to transfer to a new owner?

Websites usually transfer more simply: domain, content, hosting and monetization accounts. SaaS also involves code, infrastructure, customer contracts and often support obligations.
