## Short answer

If the name is free, you register it with a registrar and pay the yearly fee. If someone already owns it, you have to buy it from them. There are four common routes, and the right one depends on the price and how much help you want.

## The four routes compared

| Route | How it works | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Registrar marketplace | The name is listed with a price inside a registrar you may already use | Quick, fixed-price buys | The price is the seller's ask, and may be high |
| Aftermarket listing | The owner lists the name on a domain sales site and you offer or pay the asking price | Comparing many names | Fees and terms differ by site |
| Auction | Bidders compete until a set end time | Names the seller wants sold soon | It is easy to overbid in the last minutes |
| Broker | A [domain broker](/glossary/domain-broker/) contacts the owner for you | Names with no public price | Brokers earn a fee, so agree it in writing first |

A [premium domain](/glossary/premium-domain/) is one the registry or a seller prices above the normal registration fee. Read the difference before you pay.

## Step by step

1. **Search the exact name.** If it shows as available at the normal fee, register it. Check spelling, hyphens and the ending (.com, .net and others).
2. **Find the owner or the listing.** A "for sale" notice, a marketplace listing or a broker are the usual routes.
3. **Value the name.** Use a [domain appraisal](/guides/domain-appraisal-explained/) and what the site earns, if it is a live site, to set your limit before you offer.
4. **Check the history.** Ask whether the name was used for spam, has a penalty from a search engine or carries a trademark dispute. A lawyer can advise on trademarks.
5. **Agree in writing.** Record the price, the payment method and who pays any transfer fees.
6. **Pay through escrow.** An independent [escrow](/glossary/escrow/) service holds your money until the name is in your account. See [how escrow works](/guides/how-escrow-works-for-website-sales/).
7. **Take control.** Registrars handle transfers differently. Many apply a waiting period after a change of owner or registrar, so ask the registrar for its rules before you promise the seller a date.
8. **Secure it.** Turn on two-step login, renew automatically and note the renewal date.

## Buyer checklist

- The seller can prove they control the name, for example by changing a code you give them.
- The price includes any fees, with no surprises at the end.
- You have read the escrow service's terms.
- The name does not infringe a known brand.
- You can afford the yearly renewal fee.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a domain name cost?

A free, unregistered name costs the normal yearly registration fee. A name someone already owns is priced by the owner, and the range is wide, from a few hundred dollars to much more. Ask for the price in writing.

### Is it safe to buy a domain from a stranger?

It is safe when you use escrow and a written agreement. Never send money directly to an unknown seller before the name has moved.

### Can I haggle?

Yes. Make an offer based on your own valuation and be ready to walk away.
