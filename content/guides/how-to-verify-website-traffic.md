## The short answer

Traffic matters because it is usually where a site's income comes from. Ask the seller to add you with read-only access to the analytics account, so you see the data first-hand. Then test whether the numbers make sense.

## Five checks

1. **Get 12 months or more.** Look at the whole period, not the last good month. A site that has just jumped, or just dropped, needs an explanation.
2. **Compare two sources.** If the seller also has search-engine reports, a server log or an ad network dashboard, the visit counts should point the same way. Small differences are normal; large ones are not.
3. **Check where visits come from.** Note how much comes from search, direct visits, social media and other sites. If one source supplies most visits, that source is a risk. {{BRAND}}'s default methodology lowers the multiple when over 60% of traffic or sales comes from one channel, and lowers it further above 80%. See the [methodology](/methodology/).
4. **Look for traffic that does not behave like people.** Sudden spikes from one country, visits that stay a few seconds, or traffic with no matching sales are warning signs.
5. **Match traffic to revenue.** If visits doubled but income did not move, ask why. The [revenue check](/guides/how-to-verify-website-revenue/) covers the money side.

## A worked example

A seller says a site gets 60,000 visits a month and earns $3,000 a month.

- **Revenue per visit:** $3,000 ÷ 60,000 = $0.05.
- **In the analytics:** the last 3 months show 90,000 visits a month, but revenue is still $3,000, so revenue per visit is $3,000 ÷ 90,000 ≈ $0.033.

The extra 30,000 visits earned nothing. Ask where they came from before you accept the higher figure.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a screenshot of analytics enough?

No. It can be edited and shows only one view. Ask for read-only access, and ask your accountant or an experienced buyer to look if you are unsure.

### What if the seller will not share access?

Treat that as a reason to slow down. {{BRAND}} can mark a listing [traffic verified](/verification/) after its team compares stated traffic with read-only analytics access, but a buyer should still check the data before paying.

### Does high traffic mean a high price?

Not by itself. Small digital businesses are mostly priced on profit, so traffic matters through the income it produces.
