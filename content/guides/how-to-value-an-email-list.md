## The short answer

An email list is worth what it earns. A list of 50,000 addresses that nobody opens is worth little. A list of 5,000 readers who open, click and buy can be worth much more. So the first step is not counting subscribers. It is working out the yearly profit the list produces.

## Step 1: Find the yearly profit

Add up what the list earns over the last 12 months from sponsors, paid subscriptions, affiliate links and your own products sold through it. Subtract the costs of running it: the email service, writers, design and ads used to grow it. Leave your own pay out of the costs, which gives you [SDE](/glossary/sde/).

## Step 2: Apply a multiple

VALUERAQ's default range for a newsletter is 2.5 to 4.0 times yearly profit, with a midpoint of 3.2. These are editorial assumptions, not market data. The [methodology page](/methodology/) explains how the range is adjusted for age, growth and risk.

## Worked example

A list earns $1,500 a month in profit after costs.

- Yearly profit = $1,500 × 12 = $18,000.
- Low = $18,000 × 2.5 = $45,000.
- Midpoint = $18,000 × 3.2 = $57,600.
- High = $18,000 × 4.0 = $72,000.

Divided by a list of 10,000 subscribers, the midpoint is $5.76 per subscriber. That figure is an output of this example, not a rate to apply to other lists.

## What moves the value

- **Engagement.** Real readers open and click. The [open rate](/glossary/open-rate/) is one signal, though email apps can inflate it, so also look at clicks.
- **Where the money comes from.** Paid subscriptions renew. A single sponsor can leave.
- **Trend.** Growing profit raises the multiple. Falling profit lowers it.
- **Owner dependence.** If readers subscribe for you personally, a buyer will pay less.
- **List health.** Dead addresses, purchased lists and addresses collected without consent are a problem. Rules on consent differ by country, so ask a lawyer about yours.

## A sense check: cost to rebuild

Ask what it would cost a buyer to grow a similar list from zero in advertising and time. If that cost is far above your profit-based value, you may be undervaluing the list. If it is far below, the buyer has a cheap alternative. A list that earns nothing can still sell, as covered in [selling a website with no revenue](/guides/can-you-sell-a-website-with-no-revenue/), but it is priced on its parts rather than a profit multiple.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is an email list worth a fixed amount per subscriber?

No. The value depends on profit and engagement, so two lists of the same size can be worth very different amounts.

### Can I sell an email list on its own?

Often it is sold with the newsletter or site it belongs to. Check the terms you collected addresses under, and get legal advice on whether the list can be transferred.

### What proof will a buyer ask for?

Reports from your email service showing subscriber counts, opens and clicks over time, plus records of revenue. Have 12 months ready.
