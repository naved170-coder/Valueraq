## The short answer

Escrow solves a trust problem: the buyer does not want to pay before receiving the site, and the seller does not want to hand it over before being paid. An independent [escrow](/glossary/escrow/) provider holds the money in the middle and releases it once both sides have done what they agreed.

## The steps

1. **Agree the terms.** Price, what is included, and how long the buyer has to check the site (the inspection period).
2. **Open the escrow.** Buyer and seller record the terms with the provider.
3. **The buyer pays the provider.** The provider confirms to both sides that the full amount is held.
4. **The seller transfers the site.** Hosting, files, domain, analytics and revenue accounts, in an agreed order. See [how to transfer a website](/guides/how-to-transfer-a-website-to-a-new-owner/).
5. **The buyer checks.** Pages load, accounts work and the numbers match what was promised.
6. **The buyer accepts and the money is released.** If something is missing, the money stays held until the two sides settle it under the provider's dispute process.

A worked example: a buyer agrees to pay $60,000. The provider confirms it holds $60,000, the seller transfers the site, and the buyer accepts after five days. The provider then releases the money to the seller, less any fee the two sides agreed the seller would bear.

## Who does what

| Step | Buyer | Seller | Escrow provider |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agree terms | Confirms price and inspection period | Confirms what is included | Records the terms |
| Funding | Pays the provider | Waits | Confirms the money is held |
| Transfer | Prepares accounts to receive | Moves domain, hosting and accounts | Waits |
| Inspection | Tests everything | Answers questions | Keeps holding the money |
| Release | Accepts in writing | Waits | Pays the seller |

## Inspection period checklist

During the inspection period the buyer should confirm that:

- The domain is in the buyer's account and the name servers are correct.
- Hosting, files and the database load, and the site works end to end.
- Every payment, ad and affiliate account named in the listing is accessible.
- Analytics and email lists show the history the seller described.
- Recent revenue still matches the records the seller provided.

Accept only when every item is done. Accepting releases the money, and it is hard to recover afterwards.

## How to spot a fake escrow site

Fake escrow sites are a common scam: the "escrow" takes the money and never releases it. Before paying, check these things:

- You typed the provider's address yourself, or found it independently, instead of using a link from the other side.
- You can verify the provider's record and contact details elsewhere.
- The seller did not insist on one provider you cannot verify.
- The terms and fees are written down.

If any answer is "no", stop and choose a provider yourself.

## What escrow does not do

Escrow confirms that assets were delivered. It does not prove the seller's revenue or traffic claims were true, so check those first with [due diligence](/guides/digital-business-due-diligence/). ## Frequently asked questions

### Who pays the escrow fee?

Whoever the two sides agree on. It is common to split it or have one side pay. Settle it in writing before you start.

### How long does escrow take?

It depends on the inspection period and on how long the domain and account transfers take. Agree the period in advance.

### Is escrow needed for a small sale?

It is the safest way to pay for any digital business, whatever the price. Paying a seller directly leaves you with no recourse if the site is not transferred.
