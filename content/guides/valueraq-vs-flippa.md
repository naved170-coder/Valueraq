## Key takeaway

These are different kinds of service at very different stages. Flippa is an established marketplace that charges for listing and for a completed sale, and in return provides a large audience, payment handling and optional advisers. {{BRAND}} is new: it launched in 2026, has few listings and no track record of completed sales. It charges nothing to list and takes no commission, and it leaves the deal, the payment and the safeguards to the buyer and seller. If reaching the most buyers matters most, an established marketplace has a clear advantage.

{{BRAND}} is not affiliated with, endorsed by or partnered with Flippa. "Flippa" is a trademark of its owner and is used here only to identify the service.

## When Flippa is the better choice

- **You want the largest possible audience.** An established marketplace has many more active buyers than a marketplace that opened this year. More buyers usually means more enquiries and a better chance of competing offers.
- **You want payment handled inside the platform.** Flippa routes payment through FlippaPay or Escrow.com. On {{BRAND}} you have to choose and arrange an escrow service yourself.
- **You want help running the sale.** Flippa offers in-house M&A advisers and a network of third-party brokers. {{BRAND}} is not a broker and gives no representation in a deal.
- **You are selling an asset type {{BRAND}} doesn't focus on,** such as a domain name, a YouTube channel or a social media account.
- **A track record matters to you or your buyer.** Flippa has years of completed sales behind it. {{BRAND}} has none yet.

## When {{BRAND}} is the better choice

- **You want a free valuation and want to see how it was worked out.** The [valuation tools](/tools/) are free, and the formulas and default multiples are published in the [methodology](/methodology/). The multiples are {{BRAND}}'s own editorial assumptions, not figures derived from a transaction dataset; see [data sources](/data-sources/).
- **You want to list without paying.** Listing is free and there is no commission or success fee, so nothing is deducted from the price by {{BRAND}}. See [fees](/fees/).
- **You are happy to run the deal yourself.** You talk to buyers through private messaging with file attachments, agree terms directly and arrange your own escrow and sale agreement. [How it works](/how-it-works/) sets out the steps.
- **You are still preparing.** You can price the business and draft a listing with no cost or commitment before deciding where to sell.

The trade-off is plain: no fees, but a much smaller audience and more of the work and the safeguards left to you. Read [buyer safety](/buyer-safety/) before sending or accepting money.

## Can you use both?

Often yes, but check the terms first. A seller can generally list a business in more than one place unless they have signed an exclusivity agreement. Flippa's terms of service, as checked on 4 October 2026, include an exclusivity clause for listings with an asking or reserve price of US$25,000 or more, which also runs for a period after the listing is removed. Read the current terms, and any broker agreement you have signed, before listing the same business elsewhere.

A practical way to combine them without any conflict is to use the {{BRAND}} tools to work out a realistic price range, then list wherever suits the size and type of your business.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is VALUERAQ a replacement for Flippa?

No. They overlap but are not equivalent. Flippa is an established marketplace with a large audience, payment handling and assisted sales. {{BRAND}} is a new marketplace with few listings, built around free valuation tools and free listings, and it does not handle payment or act as a broker.

### Which costs less to sell on?

{{BRAND}} charges nothing to list and no commission. Flippa charges a listing fee and a success fee on a completed sale. Cost is only one factor, though: a larger audience can produce more offers, and with a free listing you pay separately for an escrow service and any advice you need.

### Does VALUERAQ offer escrow like Flippa?

No. {{BRAND}} is not an escrow service and never holds the purchase price. Buyers and sellers deal directly and should use an independent escrow service and a written sale agreement.
