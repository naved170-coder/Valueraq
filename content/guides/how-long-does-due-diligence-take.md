## The short answer

Plan on a few weeks for a small online business, such as a website, store, newsletter or app. It is shorter when the seller has organized records and gives access quickly, and longer when numbers do not match or accounts sit in someone else's name. This page does not quote an average, because no reliable one exists for small online deals. The plan below is an illustrative guide you can adapt.

[Due diligence](/glossary/due-diligence/) is the buyer's checking period between agreeing a price and signing the contract. The U.S. Small Business Administration describes it as a "thorough, objective investigation" of the business you plan to buy.

## A week-by-week plan

This example assumes a buyer who starts the day the offer is accepted and a seller who responds within a day or two.

| Week | What happens | What slows it down |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Seller shares records. Buyer checks profit and loss against payout reports and bank lines | Records not ready, figures that disagree |
| 2 | Buyer checks traffic or customer data, accounts and ownership of the domain and platforms | Access given late, accounts in a third party's name |
| 3 | Buyer reviews contracts, costs and risks, and raises questions. Price or terms may change | Long answer times, new findings |
| 4 | Agreed changes are put in the contract. Escrow is set up | Legal edits, buyer funding |

Small, clean deals can move faster, and complex ones slower. A sale with an [exclusivity period](/glossary/exclusivity-period/) usually sets a deadline, so ask for a length that fits the work.

## How to keep it short

1. **Seller:** gather records before listing. The [data room checklist](/guides/data-room-checklist/) shows the folders.
2. **Buyer:** send one complete list of requests on day one, not new requests each week. The [financial due diligence checklist](/guides/financial-due-diligence-checklist/) is a starting list.
3. **Both:** agree who answers questions and by when.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can due diligence be done in a week?

It can for a very simple site with tidy records, but rushing is how problems get missed. Match the time to the size of the deal.

### What happens if the buyer finds a problem?

The buyer can ask for a lower price, change the terms, ask for a fix or walk away, depending on what the offer allows. See [digital business due diligence](/guides/digital-business-due-diligence/).

### How does this fit into the whole sale?

Due diligence is one of six stages. See [how long it takes to sell an online business](/guides/how-long-to-sell-an-online-business/).
