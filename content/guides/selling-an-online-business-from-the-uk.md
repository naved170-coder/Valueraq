## Key takeaway

Selling an online business from the United Kingdom is the same job as selling one anywhere: you prove what the business earns, agree a price, move the money through a neutral third party and hand the assets over. The UK part is the layer on top. Many of your buyers will pay in dollars, the legal and tax treatment depends on how the business is owned, and a UK accountant and solicitor need to see the deal before you sign.

This guide covers the process in plain terms, a UK-specific checklist of questions to take to your advisers, and a worked example of what a $192,000 sale leaves you with once costs come out. It does not state UK tax rates or legal rules, because those change and depend on your circumstances. It tells you what to ask, and who to ask.

*This guide is general information, not legal or tax advice. Take advice from a qualified UK accountant and solicitor before you agree a sale.*

## The sale process at a glance

| Step | What you do | UK point to watch |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Value | Get a price range you can explain | Work in one currency throughout |
| 2. Prepare records | 12 to 24 months of profit and loss, traffic and account data | Tie figures to bank and payment statements |
| 3. Check ownership | Domain, hosting, store, app and payment accounts in your name | Decide whether you or a company owns them |
| 4. List | Describe the business, price and what is included | Say which currency the price is in |
| 5. Screen buyers | Ask for proof of funds before sharing private data | Overseas buyers: agree how and when funds arrive |
| 6. Letter of intent | Price, structure, exclusivity | Ask your adviser how the structure affects tax |
| 7. Due diligence and contract | Buyer checks the figures; solicitor reviews the agreement | Ask who the contract's governing law is |
| 8. Escrow and handover | Buyer pays into escrow; you transfer the assets | Plan for bank transfer time and currency conversion |

## Step 1: Value the business in one currency

Start with a number you can defend. The U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA) advises owners to "Use business valuation to set a monetary value." A free estimate from a [valuation tool](/tools/) gives a low, midpoint and high figure, and the [methodology](/methodology/) shows how each was reached.

Pick one currency for the whole sale. Many buyers of online businesses think and pay in US dollars, so price the listing in dollars if you expect overseas interest, and keep your own records in pounds only if you need them for your accounts. Mixing the two in one conversation causes confusion over what each figure means. {{BRAND}}'s tools use US dollars, so a UK owner should convert monthly profit at a recent rate and note the rate used.

## Step 2: Prepare records a buyer can check

Buyers abroad cannot visit you, so your records do the talking. The SBA's guide for buyers lists financial statements and tax returns among the documents to evaluate, and says to ask about contracts, leases, existing cash flow and inventory. Prepare the same set before you list.

For a UK seller, the practical points are these:

- **Match every figure to a statement.** Revenue in the listing, in the payment processor and in your accounts must agree, or you must be able to explain the difference.
- **Keep personal and business spending apart.** If you have run costs through a personal account, document each add-back. BizBuySell warns that "some add-backs can unrealistically overvalue the company," so expect a buyer to challenge every one.
- **Write down how the business runs.** A short operations document makes the handover faster. The [data room checklist](/guides/data-room-checklist/) and [how to document your business before selling](/guides/how-to-document-your-business-before-selling/) set out what to gather.

## Step 3: Check who owns what

Before anything else, list every asset the buyer expects to receive and the name each one is registered in: the domain, hosting, store or app accounts, payment processor, email platform and social profiles. If a freelancer holds one, move it to you now. Transfers take as long as the slowest account.

Then settle the legal owner. A business can belong to you personally or to a company you run, and which it is changes who signs the contract, who receives the money and which tax questions apply. Ask your accountant before you list, not after you have an offer.

## Step 4: List and screen buyers

A good listing states how the business makes money, how many hours it needs each week, where traffic or sales come from, and what is included in the price. See [how to write a business-for-sale listing](/guides/how-to-write-a-business-for-sale-listing/) for the details. Listing on {{BRAND}} is free, and the [fees page](/fees/) sets out what is and is not charged.

Overseas buyers are normal for online businesses, so screen them before sharing private data. Ask for [proof of funds](/glossary/proof-of-funds/), and confirm how and when the money will arrive. Share the website address and analytics only once you are comfortable the buyer is serious. The [ways to sell a digital business](/guides/ways-to-sell-a-digital-business/) guide compares a marketplace, a broker and a direct sale.

## Step 5: Agree the terms in a letter of intent

A [letter of intent](/glossary/letter-of-intent/) records the main terms before the lawyers draft the contract: the price, how it is paid, what is included and an [exclusivity period](/glossary/exclusivity-period/). Keep exclusivity short.

Look closely at the structure. Whether part of the price is delayed through an [earnout](/glossary/earnout/) or a [holdback](/glossary/holdback/), and whether the deal is an [asset sale](/glossary/asset-sale/) or a [stock sale](/glossary/stock-sale/), can affect your tax. The [deal structures guide](/guides/deal-structures-for-online-business-sales/) compares them. Ask your UK accountant which structure suits you before you accept one.

## Step 6: Due diligence and the contract

[Due diligence](/glossary/due-diligence/) is the buyer's check of what you have told them. The SBA calls it a "thorough, objective investigation." Answer questions quickly and consistently, because each open question adds days.

For the contract, the SBA recommends that an attorney review the sales agreement, and says to consider hiring an attorney and an accountant. In the UK that means a solicitor. Book one while due diligence is running. Two points to raise in the first call: which country's law governs the contract, and what you are promising in the [representations and warranties](/glossary/representations-and-warranties/). A statement you cannot support can come back as a claim after closing.

## Step 7: Escrow, payment and handover

With an overseas buyer, an independent escrow service solves the trust problem for both sides. The buyer pays into [escrow](/glossary/escrow/), you transfer the assets, the buyer confirms everything works, and the funds are released. [How escrow works for website sales](/guides/how-escrow-works-for-website-sales/) walks through it. Check any service independently before you use it, and read the [buyer safety](/buyer-safety/) page, because fake escrow sites exist.

Plan for two timing points. First, bank transfers between countries can take days, so agree the payment method in the contract. Second, if the buyer pays in dollars and your account is in pounds, the exchange rate on the day affects what you receive, and the conversion has a cost. The worked example below includes an invented conversion cost for this reason.

Finish with a written [transition period](/glossary/transition-period/): the days or weeks you will answer the buyer's questions after the money has moved.

## UK checklist: questions to take to your advisers

This is a list of topics, not a statement of UK rules. Rates, thresholds and deadlines change, so get the current position from your accountant and solicitor.

| Topic | Question to ask |
|---|---|
| Ownership | Do I or my company own the business and its accounts, and who must sign the sale? |
| Deal structure | Is this an asset sale or a share sale for my situation, and how does that change what I keep? |
| Tax on the gain | What tax applies to the profit I make on the sale, and when is it due? |
| Sales tax registration | Is the business registered for VAT or similar taxes, and what happens to that registration after the sale? |
| Staff and contractors | Does anyone work for the business, and what do I owe them or need to tell them? |
| Customer data | What customer data transfers to the buyer, and what must be said to those customers? |
| Contract terms | Which country's law governs the contract, and what am I promising to the buyer? |
| Currency | If the buyer pays in dollars, who bears the exchange-rate risk and the conversion cost? |
| Records | How long must I keep the business records after the sale? |

## Worked example: what a $192,000 sale leaves you

The numbers are invented to show the arithmetic. They are not averages and not a forecast.

A UK-run content website earns $5,000 a month in net profit, so $5,000 × 12 = $60,000 a year. {{BRAND}}'s default range for a website is 2.5x to 4.0x annual profit, before adjustments:

- Low: $60,000 × 2.5 = $150,000
- Midpoint: $60,000 × 3.2 = $192,000
- High: $60,000 × 4.0 = $240,000

These are {{BRAND}}'s own default assumptions, not market data; see the [methodology](/methodology/).

![Default valuation range for the example website: Low $150,000, Midpoint $192,000, High $240,000](/static/img/articles/selling-an-online-business-from-the-uk-example-range.svg){: width="720" height="226" loading="lazy" }

Suppose the site sells at the midpoint, $192,000. Costs paid to other people come out of that. These costs are invented:

| Cost | Amount |
|---|---|
| Escrow service fee | $1,900 |
| Solicitor | $2,500 |
| Accountant | $1,200 |
| Currency conversion and transfer | $1,000 |
| **Total costs** | **$6,600** |

$1,900 + $2,500 + $1,200 + $1,000 = $6,600. So $192,000 − $6,600 = $185,400 before any tax on the sale.

![Invented example costs deducted from the sale price: Escrow $1,900, Solicitor $2,500, Accountant $1,200, Currency and transfer $1,000](/static/img/articles/selling-an-online-business-from-the-uk-example-costs.svg){: width="720" height="226" loading="lazy" }

The tax on the sale is deliberately missing, because it depends on your situation. Ask your accountant to produce that figure before you accept an offer, so you know what you will actually keep. If part of the price is an earnout or holdback, the cash you receive at closing is lower than $185,400, and the rest arrives later only if the conditions are met.

## Mistakes UK sellers make

1. **Starting with the buyer, not the adviser.** Talk to an accountant before you sign a letter of intent, not after.
2. **Leaving accounts in someone else's name.** A freelancer holding the domain delays the whole handover.
3. **Not fixing the currency.** A price quoted in one currency and paid in another causes disputes.
4. **Skipping proof of funds.** An overseas buyer who cannot show funds wastes weeks.
5. **Over-promising in the contract.** Warranties you cannot support create risk after closing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I sell my online business to a buyer outside the UK?

Yes, online businesses are commonly sold across borders. Use escrow, agree the currency and payment method in writing, and ask your solicitor which country's law governs the contract.

### Do I need a broker to sell from the UK?

No. A broker, a marketplace and a direct sale are all options, and [how to sell your online business without a broker](/guides/how-to-sell-your-online-business-without-a-broker/) covers the direct route. The right choice depends on how much of the work you want to do yourself.

### Which currency should I price the business in?

Pick one and use it throughout. If you expect overseas buyers, dollars are a common choice. Ask your accountant how the conversion should be recorded for your own accounts.

### Do I pay tax when I sell?

Often there is tax on a gain from selling a business, but the rules depend on how you own it and your circumstances, and this guide does not state them. Ask a UK accountant for the figure before you agree a price.

### How long does a sale take?

It depends on your records, your price and the buyer's funding. [How long it takes to sell an online business](/guides/how-long-to-sell-an-online-business/) breaks the sale into stages.
