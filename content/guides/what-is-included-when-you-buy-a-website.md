## The short answer

This page is about buying an existing, running website, not paying an agency to design a new one. The only things you can count on are the ones written into the sale agreement. As a starting point, most website sales include:

- **The domain name** and the right to move it to your registrar account.
- **The site itself**: files, database, theme or code, and all content.
- **Hosting and platform accounts**, or a clean move of the site to yours.
- **Business accounts tied to the site**, such as analytics, ad or affiliate accounts, where the program's rules allow a transfer.

The U.S. Small Business Administration advises asking about "contracts, leases, existing cash flow and inventory" when you buy a business, and lists financial statements and tax returns among the documents to evaluate ([SBA](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/plan-your-business/buy-existing-business-or-franchise)).

## Included by default or negotiate it

Use this table as a draft checklist. "Usually" means common, not guaranteed. Only the written agreement decides.

| Asset | Usually included? | What to confirm |
|---|---|---|
| Domain name | Yes | Registrar account, no transfer lock, owned by the seller |
| Website files, database and theme or code | Yes | A full copy, and that you can run it |
| Content and images | Yes | Who wrote it, and that rights transfer to you |
| Hosting or platform account | Often | Move the site, or take over the account |
| Analytics and search reports | Often | Historical data comes with it |
| Affiliate and ad accounts | Depends on program rules | Ask each program whether the account can move |
| Email list | Negotiate | The list's rules and what subscribers were promised |
| Social media accounts | Negotiate | Handles, logins and recovery email |
| Brand name, logo and trademarks | Negotiate | Files and any registration in the seller's name |
| Supplier or writer contracts | Negotiate | Whether they can be assigned to you |
| Seller's help after the sale | Negotiate | How many weeks and what it covers |
| Non-compete | Negotiate | How long, and what it blocks |

## How to write it into the agreement

Ask that the agreement lists each item above under a heading such as "Assets included in the sale," and that anything not listed is stated to be excluded. Add the seller's promise that they own the assets and are free to sell them. Include the transition period and the non-compete as separate clauses. A lawyer should review the final text.

List everything you need to run the site on day one without the seller and tick it off against the draft agreement. Moving the assets is covered in [how to transfer a website](/guides/how-to-transfer-a-website-to-a-new-owner/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the domain always included?

Usually, but confirm it. Check the registrar account and that the domain is not locked or held in another name.

### Do I get the traffic and rankings?

You get the site that earns them. Rankings can move after a sale, so look at [how to verify website traffic](/guides/how-to-verify-website-traffic/) and ask for historical reports.

### What if something is missing after the sale?

If it was not in the contract, your options are limited. Have a lawyer review the agreement before you sign.

### Is the email list included?

Not automatically. Name it in the agreement and ask what subscribers were told when they joined, because that affects how you may use it.

### Does the seller have to help me after the sale?

Only if the contract says so. Agree the number of weeks and the topics, such as handover calls or training, before you sign.
