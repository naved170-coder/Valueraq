## Short answer

**Book value = total assets − total liabilities.**

Both numbers come from the company's balance sheet. Assets are what the business owns, such as cash, stock, equipment and money customers owe it. Liabilities are what it owes, such as supplier bills, loans and taxes due. The U.S. Small Business Administration describes the assets approach to valuation as one that "subtracts total business liabilities from the total value of all assets."

## How to find it, step by step

1. **Get the balance sheet.** Ask for the latest one, dated as close to today as possible. A balance sheet is a snapshot of one day, not a year.
2. **Add up the assets.** Use the total assets line, or add cash, inventory, equipment, software and receivables yourself.
3. **Add up the liabilities.** Include supplier bills, loans, credit cards, taxes due and money owed to customers, such as unfilled prepaid orders.
4. **Subtract.** Total assets minus total liabilities is the book value.
5. **Check the date and the basis.** Ask whether equipment is shown at cost less wear, and whether every bill is recorded. An unrecorded debt makes the book value look better than it is.

## Worked example

All figures are invented to show the arithmetic.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Cash | $15,000 |
| Inventory | $25,000 |
| Equipment and software | $8,000 |
| **Total assets** | **$48,000** |
| Supplier bills due | −$9,000 |
| Loan outstanding | −$6,000 |
| **Total liabilities** | **−$15,000** |
| **Book value** | **$33,000** |

If the same business earns $60,000 a year and a buyer pays 3 times that, the price is $180,000. The $147,000 gap between price and book value is [goodwill](/glossary/goodwill/): the value of earnings, customers and a brand that the balance sheet does not show.

## Book value, market value and fair market value

| Measure | What it tells you | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Book value | What the accounts say is left after debts | The balance sheet |
| Market value | What buyers are paying for similar businesses | Comparable sales and offers |
| Fair market value | The price a willing buyer and seller would agree. The IRS puts it as "the price that property would sell for on the open market." | An appraisal or negotiation |

## Why online businesses sell above book value

A website, app, newsletter or store holds little on its balance sheet. Its value comes from the profit it is expected to keep earning, its audience and its brand, and none of those are recorded as assets when the owner built them. That is why buyers use earnings methods such as a profit multiple. Book value still helps in three cases: a business with a lot of stock or equipment, one that is closing, and one that makes little or no profit. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can book value be negative?

Yes. If a business owes more than it owns, its book value is below zero.

### Is book value the same as the asking price?

No. The asking price is the seller's number. It usually follows earnings, not the balance sheet.

### Where does a small business find its balance sheet?

In its accounting software or from its accountant. A business that does not keep one can build a simple list of what it owns and owes on a given date.
