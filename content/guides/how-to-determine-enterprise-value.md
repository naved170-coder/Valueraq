## Short answer

**Enterprise value = equity value + debt − cash.**

Corporate Finance Institute defines it this way: "Enterprise Value (EV) is the measure of a company's total value." Debt is added because a buyer who takes the business also takes its debts. Cash is subtracted because it comes with the business and can pay those debts down.

## Worked example

All figures are invented to show the arithmetic.

| Step | Figure |
|---|---|
| Equity value, the price for the owner's stake | $200,000 |
| Add debt | + $30,000 |
| Subtract cash | − $10,000 |
| **Enterprise value** | **$220,000** |

If the business earns $66,000 a year, the multiple on enterprise value is $220,000 ÷ $66,000, about 3.3x. See [enterprise value](/glossary/enterprise-value/) and [profit multiple](/glossary/profit-multiple/).

## Enterprise value, equity value and asking price

| Term | What it counts |
|---|---|
| Equity value | What the owner's stake is worth after debts |
| Enterprise value | The whole business, ignoring how it is financed |
| Asking price | The number the seller names, which may follow either |

## How it works for a small online business

Many small websites and apps are sold as an [asset sale](/glossary/asset-sale/): the buyer gets the site, domain and accounts, and the seller keeps the cash and pays off any debts. With no debt and no cash left in the deal, equity value, enterprise value and price are the same number. Check the sale agreement to see what is included.

Enterprise value matters most when a business holds real cash or carries a loan, or when you compare it with others through multiples of [EBITDA](/glossary/ebitda/). For the full method see the [business valuation formula](/guides/business-valuation-formula/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can enterprise value be negative?

Yes. If cash is greater than equity value plus debt, the result is below zero. It is uncommon in small deals.

### Why subtract cash?

Cash comes with the business, so the buyer's net cost of the operating business is lower by that amount.

### Is enterprise value the same as the price I pay?

Not always. The price you pay is the equity value, unless the deal is cash-free and debt-free.
