## The short answer

*This page is general information, not tax or legal advice. {{BRAND}} cannot tell you what you owe.*

Often, yes. Selling a website at a profit usually creates a tax bill somewhere, but how much and when depends on three things: the country you are taxed in, whether you or a company owns the site, and how the price is split between the assets you sell. This page does not state any rates, because they change and depend on your situation. It shows what to ask.

## Why the price split matters

A website sale is usually a sale of several things at once: the domain and site, customer or email lists, content, brand, and perhaps equipment. In an [asset sale](/glossary/asset-sale/), the contract divides the price between them, and that division is called [purchase price allocation](/glossary/purchase-price-allocation/). Different assets can be treated differently for tax, so the split can change what you keep.

An invented example of a $100,000 sale:

| Asset | Share of price |
|---|---|
| Domain, site and content | $70,000 |
| Customer and email lists | $20,000 |
| Equipment and tools | $10,000 |
| **Total** | **$100,000** |

$70,000 + $20,000 + $10,000 = $100,000. A different split of the same $100,000 could produce a different tax result, so agree the split with your accountant before it goes in the contract.

## What to ask in each country

The table lists topics for a tax adviser, not rules. Named agencies are the usual tax authorities.

| Country | Tax authority | Ask your accountant |
|---|---|---|
| United States | IRS | How is the gain taxed for my business structure? Does the allocation get reported? |
| United Kingdom | HMRC | How is the gain taxed for me or my company? Is there any relief for my case? |
| Canada | CRA | How is the gain taxed, and does the structure of the sale change it? |
| Australia | ATO | How is the gain taxed, and do any concessions apply to my sale? |

In every country, also ask about sales-tax or VAT registration for the business, and what happens to it after the sale.

## Questions that apply everywhere

1. Who owns the website: me or a company?
2. Is this an asset sale or a [stock sale](/glossary/stock-sale/)?
3. How will the price be split between assets?
4. Is any of the price delayed through an [earnout](/glossary/earnout/) or [seller financing](/glossary/seller-financing/), and when is that taxed?
5. What tax will I owe, and by when?

The U.S. Small Business Administration recommends hiring an attorney and an accountant when a business changes hands. Do this before the [letter of intent](/glossary/letter-of-intent/), because the structure is agreed there.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I pay tax if I sell at a loss?

It depends on your country and circumstances, and a loss may have its own treatment. Ask your accountant.

### Does the buyer's payment method change the tax?

Not directly, but timing can: a payment spread over time may be taxed over time. Ask your accountant how a delayed payment is treated.
