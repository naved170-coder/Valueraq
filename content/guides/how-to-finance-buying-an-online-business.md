## The short answer

There are four common ways to pay for an online business, and many deals mix them:

1. **Your own cash.** The simplest route, and the one sellers like best.
2. **Seller financing.** The seller lets you pay part of the price over time. See [seller financing](/glossary/seller-financing/).
3. **An earnout.** Part of the price is paid later, only if the business hits agreed targets. See [earnout](/glossary/earnout/).
4. **A loan from a lender.** The U.S. Small Business Administration says buying an existing business calls for a "thorough, objective investigation" ([SBA](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/plan-your-business/buy-existing-business-or-franchise)). Lenders will want the same records.

## Options compared

| | Own cash | Seller financing | Earnout | Lender loan |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Who pays | You | Seller, over time | Seller, if targets are met | Bank or lender |
| Cost to you | None beyond the price | Often interest or a higher price | Usually a higher headline price | Interest, fees, often security |
| Speed | Fastest | Fast | Fast, needs careful wording | Slowest |
| If profit falls | You carry it | You still owe the note | Payments may shrink | You still owe the loan |
| Availability | Anyone with funds | Depends on seller | Depends on seller | Depends on country and records |

SBA programs are for buyers in the United States. Elsewhere, ask local banks and credit unions whether they lend against a digital business, since many want physical assets as security.

## Worked example: can the profit cover the payments?

A buyer pays $120,000 for a website: $72,000 cash and a $48,000 seller note over 24 months.

Without interest the payment is $48,000 ÷ 24 = **$2,000** a month. If the seller asks for 8% interest (a what-if rate, not a typical one), the standard loan formula gives about **$2,171** a month, about $52,100 in total and about $4,100 in interest.

The site earns $4,000 a month in profit. Test it:

| Monthly profit | Profit ÷ $2,171 payment | Share of profit used |
|---|---|---|
| $4,000 | 1.84 | about 54% |
| $3,000 (down 25%) | 1.38 | about 72% |
| $2,171 | 1.00 | 100% |

A buyer who works in the business also needs pay for their time, so a ratio near 1.0 leaves no room for a bad month. Ask for a smaller note or a longer term if the numbers look tight.

## What to ask before you choose

- **How much can the business carry?** Repayments come from verified profit. See [due diligence](/guides/digital-business-due-diligence/).
- **How much is certain?** A seller note is owed whatever happens. An earnout may never be paid.
- **How will the money be held?** Use an independent [escrow](/glossary/escrow/) service. {{BRAND}} is not part of the sale and takes no commission.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I buy an online business with no money down?

It is rare. Sellers want you to have something at stake.

### Does seller financing cost more?

It can. Sellers often ask for interest or a higher price. Compare the total you will pay.

### Do I need a lawyer?

Yes, for the agreement. Loan terms and tax treatment differ by country, so also ask an accountant.
