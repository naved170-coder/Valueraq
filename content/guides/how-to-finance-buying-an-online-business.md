## The short answer

There are four common ways to pay for an online business, and many deals mix them:

1. **Your own cash.** The simplest route and the one sellers like best.
2. **Seller financing.** The seller lets you pay part of the price over time. See [seller financing](/glossary/seller-financing/).
3. **An earnout.** Part of the price is paid later, and only if the business hits agreed targets. See [earnout](/glossary/earnout/).
4. **A loan from a lender.** The U.S. Small Business Administration describes buying an existing business as something that calls for a "thorough, objective investigation", and suggests you "Consider hiring an attorney and an accountant" ([SBA](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/plan-your-business/buy-existing-business-or-franchise)). Lenders will want the same records, so have them ready.

## A worked example

A buyer agrees to pay $120,000 for a website. The numbers are an illustration.

- Own cash at closing: $72,000 (60%).
- Seller note: $48,000 (40%), repaid over 24 months.
- Monthly instalment before interest: $48,000 ÷ 24 = **$2,000**.

If the site earns $4,000 a month in profit, the instalments take half of it for two years. Check that you can live with that before you sign.

## What to ask before you choose

- **How much can the business carry?** Repayments come out of its profit, so use verified figures. See [due diligence](/guides/digital-business-due-diligence/).
- **How much is certain?** A seller note is owed whatever happens. An earnout may never be paid. Compare offers on what is paid at closing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I buy an online business with no money down?

It is rare. Sellers want to see that you have something at stake, so expect to pay a meaningful share at closing.

### Does seller financing cost more?

It can. Sellers often ask for interest or a higher price in return for waiting. Compare the total you will pay, not only the headline price.

### Do I need a lawyer?

Loan terms, security and tax treatment differ by country and by deal, so have a lawyer draft the agreement and an accountant advise on tax.
