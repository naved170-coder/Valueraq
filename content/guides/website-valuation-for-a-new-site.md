## The short answer

If the site earns a profit, value it as a multiple of annual profit, then lower the multiple for its age. {{BRAND}}'s [website valuation tool](/tools/website-valuation/) does this with fixed rules: a site with under one year of history has its multiple cut by 20%, and one with one to two years has it cut by 10%. The reason given in the rules is that a short history gives buyers little evidence that earnings will last.

If the site earns nothing yet, the tool returns no estimate, because an earnings multiple cannot value a loss. Read [can you sell a website with no revenue](/guides/can-you-sell-a-website-with-no-revenue/) for how those sites are priced.

## How to work it out

1. **Find average monthly net profit** over the months the site has been earning. Leave out your own pay. This is the same measure as [SDE](/glossary/sde/).
2. **Multiply by 12** to get annual profit.
3. **Apply the default range** for a website: 2.5x low, 3.2x midpoint, 4.0x high.
4. **Apply the age adjustment**: ×0.80 for under one year.

Other adjustments, such as growth and traffic concentration, also apply in the tool. The example below uses age only.

## Example

A site is 8 months old and has earned an average of $1,500 a month in profit. These figures are invented.

Annual profit = $1,500 × 12 = $18,000.

| | Multiple | Before age adjustment | After ×0.80 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 2.5x | $45,000 | $36,000 |
| Midpoint | 3.2x | $57,600 | $46,080, shown as about $46,000 |
| High | 4.0x | $72,000 | $57,600, shown as about $58,000 |

Those multiples are {{BRAND}}'s own default assumptions, not market data. The method is in the [methodology](/methodology/). The tool also shows a lower confidence rating for sites with under two years of history, since there is less to base the estimate on.

## What helps a buyer trust the number

- **Proof of earnings.** Payment processor and ad network statements for every month. See [how to verify website revenue](/guides/how-to-verify-website-revenue/).
- **A rising trend.** Month-by-month profit that climbs is better evidence than one strong month.
- **Documented work.** Written processes that show the site does not depend on you alone.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a new website be worth more than the formula says?

Yes. A buyer may pay extra for a domain, content or an audience they would otherwise take months to build. The formula gives a starting range, and the final price is agreed between the two sides.

### Should I wait until the site is a year old before selling?

It can help, since the age adjustment is smaller after one year and a longer record makes verification easier. But waiting has its own risks, so decide on the numbers rather than a date.

### What if my profit changes a lot month to month?

Use the average over all months you have, and say so in the listing. Large swings make the estimate less certain, so show the monthly figures.
