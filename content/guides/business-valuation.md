## Key takeaway

A digital business is any business that earns most of its revenue online: websites, SaaS, AI products, apps, newsletters and ecommerce stores. Small digital businesses are usually valued as **earnings × multiple**, where the multiple reflects how confident a buyer can be that earnings will continue and grow.

## The three valuation methods

### 1. Market multiples

The most common method for small online businesses. You take a financial metric and multiply it by a figure that reflects risk and growth.

- **Profit multiples** apply to websites, apps, newsletters and ecommerce stores, using [seller's discretionary earnings (SDE)](/glossary/sde/) for owner-run businesses or [EBITDA](/glossary/ebitda/) for businesses with a management team.
- **Revenue multiples**, usually on [ARR](/glossary/arr/), apply to SaaS and AI businesses whose recurring revenue is predictable, or which are investing their profit into growth.

Multiples are quick to apply and easy to compare between listings. Their weakness is that the multiple itself involves judgement.

### 2. Discounted cash flow (DCF)

DCF estimates future cash flows year by year and discounts them back to today at a rate reflecting risk. It's rigorous in theory but sensitive to assumptions: small changes to the growth or discount rate change the result a lot. It's used more for larger companies with predictable cash flows than for small online businesses.

### 3. Asset-based valuation

This values the business at what its assets would cost to replace or would fetch if sold separately: domain, content, code, inventory, customer lists. It sets a floor for loss-making or very young businesses but usually undervalues profitable ones, because it ignores their earning power.

## SDE or EBITDA?

**SDE** is profit before the owner's own pay and personal or one-off expenses. It suits businesses where one owner does most of the work, which describes most small online businesses. **EBITDA** is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation, after paying market-rate salaries for everyone, including management. It suits businesses where the owner doesn't need to work in the business.

The difference matters. A business with $100,000 SDE whose owner works full time might have much lower EBITDA once you pay someone to do the owner's job. Buyers apply multiples to the figure that matches how they'll run the business.

## How the multiple is chosen

Whatever the business type, the same questions move the multiple:

| Question | Higher multiple when… |
|---|---|
| How long has it been earning? | Several years of stable or growing earnings |
| Which way is it heading? | Revenue and profit rising |
| How concentrated is it? | No single channel, customer, supplier or platform dominates |
| How much work does it need? | Few owner hours, documented processes |
| How transferable is it? | Clean records, owned assets, contracts that can be assigned |
| How durable is the revenue? | Recurring, contracted or repeat revenue |

## How different business types compare

| Type | Usual base | What buyers focus on |
|---|---|---|
| [Websites](/tools/website-valuation/) | Annual profit | Traffic sources, search-engine risk, monetization mix |
| [SaaS](/tools/saas-valuation/) | ARR or profit | Growth, churn, gross margin, code quality |
| [AI businesses](/tools/ai-business-valuation/) | ARR or profit | Model dependence, inference costs, retention |
| [Apps](/tools/app-valuation/) | Annual profit | App-store platform risk, retention by cohort, acquisition costs |
| [Newsletters](/tools/newsletter-valuation/) | Annual profit | Writer dependence, engagement, sponsor concentration |
| [Ecommerce](/tools/ecommerce-valuation/) | Annual profit + inventory | Margins after ads, suppliers, channel concentration |

## Example: the same profit, different values

Imagine three businesses each earning $60,000 a year in SDE:

- A five-year-old newsletter with a strong brand and several repeat sponsors, needing 10 hours a week.
- A one-year-old app whose downloads come almost entirely from paid ads.
- A three-year-old ecommerce store selling mostly through one marketplace, with $30,000 of inventory.

The newsletter's long history and low workload support a higher multiple. The app's short history and channel dependence push its multiple down. The store sits between them on earnings, but the buyer also pays for inventory at cost. Same profit, three different prices, which is why the multiple matters as much as the earnings figure.

## Limitations of any valuation method

Every method depends on accurate figures and reasonable judgement about the future. Real sale prices also depend on deal structure (earn-outs, seller financing, transition support), buyer type (individual, portfolio operator or strategic acquirer) and market conditions. An estimate is a starting point for negotiation and [due diligence](/guides/digital-business-due-diligence/), not a promise of a price.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the most common way to value an online business?

A multiple of annual profit (SDE) for most owner-run online businesses, and a multiple of ARR for many SaaS and AI businesses.

### Why do similar businesses sell for different multiples?

Because multiples price risk and growth. Age, trend, concentration, workload and transferability all differ between businesses that look similar on revenue.
