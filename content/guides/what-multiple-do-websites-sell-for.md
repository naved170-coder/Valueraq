## The range we use

| | Low | Midpoint | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Times yearly profit | 2.5 | 3.2 | 4.0 |
| Times monthly profit | 30 | 38 | 48 |

These are {{BRAND}}'s default assumptions for a website with two to four years of history, flat revenue, a moderate workload for the owner and a reasonable spread of traffic. They are published in the [methodology](/methodology/). They are editorial assumptions and not an average taken from a sales database.

## Why there is no single market figure

Most website sales are private. The price, the profit figure behind it and the terms are rarely published, and the deals that are reported are the ones a seller or marketplace chose to share. A quoted "average" depends on whose deals were counted and how profit was measured. Use any such figure as a rough guide.

## Monthly and yearly multiples

People quote the same idea in two units. To convert:

- **Monthly to yearly:** divide by 12. A multiple of 36 times monthly profit is 3.0 times yearly profit.
- **Yearly to monthly:** multiply by 12. A multiple of 3.5 times yearly profit is 42 times monthly profit.

Always check which one a listing means, and whether "profit" is before or after the owner's pay. See [SDE](/glossary/sde/).

## What moves a site inside the range, or outside it

The website tool applies fixed rules. The largest effects are:

| Factor | Effect on the multiple |
|---|---|
| Under one year old | −20% |
| Revenue down more than 20% in a year | −25% |
| Over 80% of traffic from one source | −12% |
| Owner works over 40 hours a week | −10% |
| Revenue up more than 75% in a year | +20% |
| Four or more years old | +5% |
| Owner works under 10 hours a week | +5% |

The combined adjustment is limited to between 0.5 and 1.4 times the default. That gives a floor of 1.25 (2.5 × 0.5) and a ceiling of 5.6 (4.0 × 1.4).

## A quick example

A site keeps $3,000 a month, so $36,000 a year. At the default range:

- Low: $36,000 × 2.5 = $90,000
- Midpoint: $36,000 × 3.2 = $115,200
- High: $36,000 × 4.0 = $144,000

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a higher multiple always a better deal for the seller?

Not always. A higher multiple with part of the price paid later, or dependent on future results, can be worth less than a lower multiple paid in full at closing.

### Do websites sell on a revenue multiple?

Rarely. Costs differ too much from site to site, so profit is the usual basis. Revenue multiples are mainly used for subscription software.

### How do I work out the multiple in a listing?

Divide the asking price by yearly profit. The [business multiple calculator](/calculators/business-multiple-calculator/) does it for you.
