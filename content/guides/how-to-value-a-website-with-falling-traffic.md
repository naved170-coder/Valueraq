## The short answer

Price the profit the site earns now, then lower the multiple for the decline. Falling traffic does not set the price by itself. Buyers pay for profit, and they discount it when they doubt it will last.

## How to do it

1. **Use recent profit.** Work out [SDE](/glossary/sde/) for the last 12 months, and then for the last 3 months. If the recent months are lower, a buyer will notice, so show both.
2. **Find the cause.** A drop caused by one search update, a lost page or a seasonal dip is read differently from a long steady slide. Check [organic traffic](/glossary/organic-traffic/) by page and by month, and write down what you know.
3. **Apply the decline to the multiple.** {{BRAND}}'s default range for a website is 2.5 to 4.0 times annual profit. If revenue is down 5% to 20% over 12 months, the tool lowers the multiple by 12%. A drop of more than 20% lowers it by 25%. These are editorial assumptions in the [methodology](/methodology/), not market data.
4. **Add the other risks.** Most visits from one channel (over 60% to 80% lowers the multiple 6%, over 80% lowers it 12%) matter more on a falling site.

## Example

A site earns $3,000 a month, or $36,000 a year. Revenue is down 15% over 12 months, 75% of visits come from one search engine, the site is three years old and the owner spends 12 hours a week on it.

- Decline: −12%. Single channel: −6%. Other factors: no change.
- Factor = 0.88 × 0.94 = 0.827.
- Multiple range = 2.5 × 0.827 to 4.0 × 0.827 = 2.07 to 3.31, midpoint 2.65.
- Value = $36,000 × those multiples = **$74,000 to $119,000, midpoint $95,000**.

A steady site with the same profit and a 3.2 midpoint is worth about $115,000. The decline costs about $20,000 at the midpoint (17%). If the last 3 months averaged $2,400 a month, a buyer using that figure would value the site nearer $76,000 at the midpoint. These are illustrations, not market prices.

## What can help the price

Show the cause and what you did about it. Document recovery steps, new revenue sources and a clean traffic record. Buyers can check your figures against [verified traffic data](/guides/how-to-verify-website-traffic/), so use real numbers. For the full method, see [website valuation](/guides/website-valuation/). ## Frequently asked questions

### Is a site with falling traffic worth anything?

If it still earns a profit, yes. Buyers price the profit and take a lower multiple. A site with no profit cannot be priced on earnings by the tool.

### Should I wait for traffic to recover before selling?

It depends on the cause and on how long you can wait. If the drop looks seasonal, a full year of data shows it. If it is a steady slide, waiting can lower the price further.

### Do buyers use traffic or profit?

Profit sets the price. Traffic helps buyers judge whether the profit will last. The SBA describes the income approach as one that "looks at projected revenue and accounts for potential risks", which is why a decline matters: it raises the risk. Source: [U.S. Small Business Administration](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/manage-your-business/close-or-sell-your-business).
