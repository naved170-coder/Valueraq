## The short answer

A listing is suspect when its numbers cannot be checked, when the seller cannot prove they control the site, or when the seller wants to hurry you or be paid outside escrow. A misleading listing can be honest but sloppy: a best month shown as an average, costs left out, or a drop in traffic that goes unexplained. In both cases the fix is the same. Check everything at the source, not from screenshots.

## Seven checks to run

1. **Ask for read-only access to revenue.** The payment processor, ad network or affiliate dashboard should show the same twelve months as the listing. A screenshot or spreadsheet can be edited. See [how to verify website revenue](/guides/how-to-verify-website-revenue/).
2. **Ask for read-only access to traffic.** Analytics should match the visits in the listing. See [how to verify website traffic](/guides/how-to-verify-website-traffic/).
3. **Confirm ownership.** The seller should be able to show control of the domain and the main accounts, for example by adding a line of text you choose to the site. On VALUERAQ, look for the **Website ownership confirmed** badge and the [verification status](/verification/).
4. **Compare the average with the best month.** Add up twelve months and divide by twelve. If the listing quotes a single strong month, the average is the figure that counts.
5. **Look for an explanation of every jump or fall.** A sudden rise before listing, or a sharp fall, needs a reason you can check.
6. **Test the price against the profit.** An unusually low price for the profit claimed, with no clear reason for selling, deserves questions before excitement.
7. **Watch how the seller handles the money.** Be wary of requests to pay directly by bank transfer, cryptocurrency, gift cards or a payment app, or to move the conversation away from the platform. Use a recognized [escrow](/glossary/escrow/) service.

## What to do if something is wrong

Stop, keep the messages, and do not pay. On VALUERAQ you can [report a listing](/report-a-listing/) with the listing number and a note on what is wrong. If you have already sent money, contact your bank or payment provider and consider legal advice.

A verification status helps but is not a guarantee: it covers the specific evidence checked, for the period checked. It does not replace your own [due diligence](/guides/digital-business-due-diligence/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a verified listing mean the business is safe to buy?

No. Verification means named evidence was checked against the listing's figures. You should still review the records, the assets being sold and the contract.

### Is a seller who refuses to share read-only access hiding something?

Not always, but a seller who will only send screenshots gives you nothing you can check. You can ask for original statements instead, or walk away.

### Can I use a free estimate to spot an unrealistic price?

It can help. Put the seller's profit into the [Website Valuation Tool](/tools/website-valuation/) and compare the range with the asking price. The range is VALUERAQ's default assumption, not market data.
