## Buying a website: what you're actually buying

When you buy a website you normally buy the domain, the content and design, the traffic it attracts, its revenue relationships (affiliate accounts, ad-network placements, sponsor contacts, email lists) and any documentation or contractors that keep it running. Most website sales are **asset sales**: you buy these assets, not the seller's company.

Websites on {{BRAND}} fall into a few common business models:

| Model | How it earns | Main risk to check |
|---|---|---|
| Affiliate content | Commissions on products readers buy | Commission cuts or programme closures; search traffic changes |
| Display advertising | Ad revenue per thousand visits | Traffic volatility and ad-rate seasonality |
| Lead generation | Fees for enquiries passed to businesses | Dependence on a few buyers of leads |
| Digital products | Courses, templates, downloads | Whether sales depend on the founder's personal brand |

## Metrics to compare on every listing

- **Average monthly net profit** over 12 months, and the trend within it.
- **Implied profit multiple**: shown on each listing, calculated from the asking price and the seller's figures.
- **Traffic by channel**: how much comes from organic search, direct visits, email, social and referrals.
- **Age**: time since the site started earning, not the domain registration date.
- **Owner hours**: what you'd need to do, or pay for, each week.

## How websites are valued

Most small websites sell for a multiple of annual net profit. {{BRAND}}'s default assumption is 2.5× to 4× annual profit before adjustments for age, growth, traffic concentration and workload. The full method is on the [methodology page](/methodology/). You can check any listing by entering its figures into the [Website Valuation Tool](/tools/website-valuation/).

## Buying tips specific to websites

1. **Ask for analytics access**, not screenshots, covering at least 12 months. Compare traffic trends with the dates of known search-engine updates.
2. **Match revenue to payouts.** Affiliate and ad-network dashboards should agree with bank or payment-processor deposits.
3. **Check content ownership.** Confirm articles and images were written or licensed for the site, not copied.
4. **Review backlinks.** Large numbers of paid or low-quality links can become a liability.
5. **Plan the migration.** Domain, hosting, affiliate and ad accounts each have their own transfer process.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much do websites sell for?

It depends on profit, age, growth and risk. Using {{BRAND}}'s default assumptions, a site earning $3,000 a month in net profit would start from roughly $90,000 to $144,000 before adjustments. Real prices also depend on deal terms.

### Is it better to buy a website or build one?

Buying gives you existing traffic and income immediately but costs more upfront and carries the risk that earnings fall after the sale. Building is cheaper but can take a long time to reach similar income, with no guarantee it will.

### Do I need a broker to buy a website?

No, but you need a careful process: verified figures, a written purchase agreement, an escrow service for the payment and a planned handover.
