## Buying an ecommerce business

An ecommerce acquisition typically covers the store and domain, the brand and any trademarks, product listings and photography, supplier relationships, customer and email lists, marketplace seller accounts where they can be transferred, and inventory, which is normally bought separately at cost.

| Model | What to check |
|---|---|
| DTC brand on its own site | Repeat purchase rate, ad dependence, brand assets |
| Marketplace seller (for example Amazon FBA) | Account health, fees, reviews, share of sales on one marketplace |
| Dropshipping | Supplier reliability, shipping times, margin after refunds |
| Print on demand | Design ownership, production partner terms |
| Wholesale / B2B | Customer concentration, payment terms, contracts |

## Metrics to compare on every listing

- **Monthly net profit** after cost of goods, shipping, fees, returns and advertising.
- **Revenue by channel**: own site, each marketplace, wholesale.
- **Advertising spend** as a share of revenue, and how it has changed.
- **Inventory** at cost, and how quickly it sells.
- **Implied profit multiple** shown on each listing.

## How ecommerce businesses are valued

Profitable online stores are usually valued as a multiple of annual profit, with inventory added at cost. {{BRAND}}'s default assumption is 2× to 3.5× annual profit before adjustments for fulfillment model, channel concentration, growth, age and workload. See the [methodology](/methodology/) or check a listing in the [Ecommerce Valuation Tool](/tools/ecommerce-valuation/).

## Buying tips specific to ecommerce

1. **Reconcile revenue** with payment-processor and marketplace payout reports, net of refunds and chargebacks.
2. **Check each marketplace account's health and transfer rules.** Some seller accounts can't be transferred, which affects how the deal is structured.
3. **Meet the suppliers** and confirm pricing, minimum orders and lead times will continue for a new owner.
4. **Count the stock** before closing and exclude obsolete items from the inventory price.
5. **Test unit economics** without the current ad strategy, in case ad costs rise.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is inventory included in the asking price?

Check each listing. Most ecommerce deals price the business on earnings and add inventory at cost, confirmed by a stock count at closing.

### What makes an online store worth more?

Diverse sales channels, a recognisable brand, repeat customers, reliable suppliers, healthy margins after advertising, and low owner time all support a higher multiple.
