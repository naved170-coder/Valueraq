## Buying a newsletter

A newsletter sale usually includes the subscriber list, the publication's name and domain, the archive of past issues, sponsor relationships, the newsletter platform account or an export from it, and social accounts tied to the brand. The most valuable part, readers' trust, doesn't appear on a balance sheet and depends on how the handover is managed.

| Revenue model | What to check |
|---|---|
| Sponsorships | Number of sponsors, repeat bookings, rates and booked-ahead calendar |
| Paid subscriptions | Paid subscriber count, churn, whether payments move to your platform |
| Mixed | Which source is growing and which is shrinking |

## Metrics to compare on every listing

- **Monthly net profit** and its trend.
- **Subscribers**, and how they were acquired (organic sign-ups, referrals, paid growth, giveaways).
- **Open and click rates** over recent months. Open rates can be inflated by email privacy features, so clicks help confirm engagement.
- **Largest sponsor's share** of revenue.
- **Writer dependence**: who writes each issue and how readers relate to them.

## How newsletters are valued

Profitable newsletters are generally valued as a multiple of annual profit. {{BRAND}}'s default assumption is 2.5× to 4× annual profit before adjustments for engagement, revenue model, sponsor concentration, growth, age and workload. See the [methodology](/methodology/) or check a listing in the [Newsletter Valuation Tool](/tools/newsletter-valuation/).

## Buying tips specific to newsletters

1. **Ask for read-only platform access** to see list growth, unsubscribes and engagement over at least 12 months.
2. **Check consent records.** Subscribers should have opted in; lists built from scraped or purchased addresses are a legal and deliverability risk.
3. **Talk to sponsors** (with the seller's agreement) about whether they'll continue after the sale.
4. **Plan the voice transition.** Agree whether the seller will introduce you, keep writing for a period, or stay on as a contributor.
5. **Confirm paid-subscription portability** on the platform the newsletter uses.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much is a newsletter with 50,000 subscribers worth?

Subscriber count alone doesn't determine value; profit and engagement do. Two newsletters of the same size can earn very different amounts. Use the tool with the newsletter's actual profit.

### Can a newsletter be sold if the founder writes it?

Yes, but buyers usually ask for a transition period or an earn-out, because some readers may leave when the writer changes.
