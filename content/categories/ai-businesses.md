## Buying an AI business

An AI business for sale might be an AI writing or image tool, a chatbot product, an AI agent that automates a workflow, or an API that wraps one or more machine-learning models. The sale usually includes the application code, prompts and configuration, any proprietary data or fine-tuned models, the customer base and the brand.

What makes AI businesses different from other software is that a large part of the product often comes from a model the business doesn't own. That shapes the risks you're buying.

| Model | What to check |
|---|---|
| AI SaaS on a third-party model | Provider terms, cost per user, ability to switch providers |
| AI API or wrapper | Margin after inference costs, rate limits, differentiation |
| Fine-tuned or own models | Ownership of weights and training data, compute costs |
| AI agent or automation | Reliability, error handling, customer trust and support load |

## Metrics to compare on every listing

- **MRR, growth and churn**, with retention by monthly cohort if available.
- **Gross margin** after model/API and hosting costs, tracked over time.
- **Model providers used** and whether the product can switch between them.
- **Age**: many AI products are young, so look at how revenue has behaved month by month.
- **Implied multiples** shown on each listing.

## How AI businesses are valued

Small AI businesses with recurring revenue are usually valued like SaaS, on a multiple of ARR or profit. {{BRAND}}'s default assumption starts slightly lower, at 1.5× to 4.5× ARR, then adjusts for growth, churn, gross margin and provider dependence. The reasons are set out in the [methodology](/methodology/). Check a listing in the [AI Business Valuation Tool](/tools/ai-business-valuation/).

## Buying tips specific to AI

1. **Test what the product does beyond the model.** Would a general-purpose AI assistant do the same job? If so, revenue may not last.
2. **Model the costs.** Ask for cost per active user and per request over several months.
3. **Read the provider's terms** for commercial use, usage restrictions and data handling.
4. **Check data rights.** Confirm the business has the right to any training data and customer data it uses, and that customer data is handled under its privacy policy.
5. **Look at cohort retention**, not just sign-ups. Early interest in AI tools often fades.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are AI businesses worth more than normal SaaS?

Not automatically. Fast growth can support a high price, but thinner margins, short histories and provider dependence often offset it. Each business needs to be judged on its own figures.

### What happens if the model provider changes its prices?

Margins change. That is why the AI tool asks for gross margin and provider dependence, and why buyers ask how the product would cope with a price change.
