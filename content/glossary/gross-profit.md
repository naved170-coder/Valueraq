## How it works

**Gross profit = revenue − direct costs.** Direct costs are the ones that rise and fall with each sale: the cost of the goods sold, hosting that serves customers, payment fees, and any contractor paid per order or per piece of content.

Gross profit stops early on purpose. It does not subtract advertising, salaries, software used to run the business or taxes. Those come off later and leave [net profit](/glossary/net-profit/). Gross profit shows whether the product itself earns money before the rest of the business is counted.

## Example

An online store sells $200,000 of products in a year.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Revenue | $200,000 |
| Cost of the products sold | −$90,000 |
| Payment fees | −$6,000 |
| Shipping paid by the store | −$14,000 |
| **Gross profit** | **$90,000** |

Gross profit is $200,000 − $110,000 = $90,000. As a share of revenue, that is 45%, which is the [gross margin](/glossary/gross-margin/).

Advertising, software and the owner's time are still to come. If those cost $50,000, net profit is $40,000.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Buyers of small digital businesses price mainly on profit after running costs, such as [SDE](/glossary/sde/), and then apply a [multiple](/glossary/profit-multiple/). Gross profit is where they look first to understand that figure:

- **Is the product healthy?** A low gross profit means little is left to cover everything else.
- **Can it grow?** If gross profit is strong, extra sales can turn into profit. If it is thin, more sales may add work and not much profit.
- **Did costs change?** A fall in gross profit with steady revenue usually means supplier, hosting or shipping costs rose.

Be consistent about what counts as a direct cost. Moving a cost between "direct" and "overhead" changes gross profit without changing the real profit, and a careful buyer will notice.

## Gross profit vs net profit

Gross profit comes after the direct costs only. Net profit comes after all costs. The first measures the product; the second measures the whole business.
