## How it works

**ARPU = revenue in the period ÷ number of users in the period.** Pick a period (a month is common), use the same period for both figures, and decide whether free users count. Including them lowers ARPU, so say which version you use. Many businesses report ARPU for paying customers only.

## Example

A subscription app earns $9,000 in a month from 300 paying customers.

**ARPU = $9,000 ÷ 300 = $30 per customer per month.**

If the app also has 700 free users and they are counted, the average falls to $9,000 ÷ 1,000 = $9.

## Why ARPU matters in a valuation

ARPU shows whether growth comes from more customers or from each customer paying more. A buyer will compare it over time and across plans. Rising ARPU with steady [churn](/glossary/churn/) suggests pricing power. Falling ARPU while customer numbers rise can mean heavy discounting or a shift to cheaper plans.

It also feeds other measures: dividing ARPU by the churn rate gives a rough estimate of [customer lifetime value](/glossary/ltv/), which is then compared with the cost of winning a customer ([CAC](/glossary/cac/)).

ARPU is an average, so a few large accounts can hide many small ones. Ask for the spread of customers by plan, not only the average.

## Ways to calculate it

- **By plan.** Divide each plan's revenue by its customers to see which plans carry the business.
- **By cohort.** Compare customers who joined in different months to see whether newer customers pay more or less.
- **Paying users only, or all users.** Pick one and label it, since the two give very different numbers.

ARPU is not the same as profit per user: it ignores what it costs to serve each customer.

Whatever the method, use revenue after refunds and discounts, so the figure reflects what the business actually kept.

## ARPU vs ARPPU

ARPPU is average revenue per paying user. It leaves free users out. The two answer different questions.

| Measure | Divides revenue by | Use it to see |
|---|---|---|
| ARPU | All users, free and paid | How well the whole audience earns |
| ARPPU | Paying users only | What a paying customer is worth |

For the app above, ARPU is $9 and ARPPU is $30. Always state which one a listing shows.

## From ARPU to a customer lifetime estimate

Buyers combine ARPU with [churn](/glossary/churn/). Suppose paying customers pay $30 a month and 4% leave each month.

- Average lifetime ≈ 1 ÷ 4% = 25 months.
- Revenue per customer over that time ≈ $30 × 25 = $750.

Multiply by the gross margin to see what is kept: at 80%, about $600. This is a rough guide, since real customers do not leave at an even pace. Compare it with the cost of winning a customer ([CAC](/glossary/cac/)).

## What buyers ask about ARPU

- Does it include free users, trials or refunds?
- Is it rising because of price changes or plan upgrades?
- How does it differ by plan and by sign-up month?

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good ARPU?

There is no single figure. It depends on the product, the plan mix and the market, so compare with your own history and with similar businesses whose records you can see.

### Is ARPU the same as MRR?

No. [MRR](/glossary/mrr/) is total monthly recurring revenue. ARPU divides revenue by users, so MRR ÷ customers gives ARPU for paying customers.

### Can ARPU go down while revenue goes up?

Yes. If many new customers join on a cheaper plan, revenue rises while the average per user falls.

## Related terms

[MRR](/glossary/mrr/) is the total recurring revenue that ARPU divides. [SaaS](/glossary/saas/) businesses report it most often.
