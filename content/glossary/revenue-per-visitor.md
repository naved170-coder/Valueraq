## How it works

**Revenue per visitor = total revenue ÷ number of visitors**

Use the same period and the same source for both numbers, for example last month's revenue and last month's visitors from your analytics tool. Say whether "visitors" means unique people or sessions, because the two give different answers. A store that counts sessions will show a lower figure than one that counts unique people.

RPV rolls three things into one number: how many visitors buy ([conversion rate](/glossary/conversion-rate/)), how much each order is worth ([average order value](/glossary/average-order-value/)), and, for content sites, how well the page earns from ads or affiliate links. For a store, **RPV = conversion rate × average order value.**

### RPV vs RPM

[RPM](/glossary/rpm/) is revenue per 1,000 visits. It is RPV multiplied by 1,000. Content sites usually quote RPM because the amounts per visit are small; stores usually quote RPV. They carry the same information.

## Example

A hypothetical online shop had 20,000 visitors last month and $30,000 in revenue.

**RPV = $30,000 ÷ 20,000 = $1.50.** Its RPM is $1.50 × 1,000 = $1,500.

Check it the other way: the shop converts 2.5% of visitors (500 orders) at an average order value of $60. 2.5% × $60 = $1.50. Both routes agree.

If a promotion brings 10,000 extra visitors who buy less often, RPV can fall even while revenue rises. That is why a buyer reads RPV next to total traffic.

## Why it matters in a valuation

VALUERAQ's profit-based tools value a business on its profit, not on visitors or RPV. But buyers use RPV and RPM to sense-check the income a seller claims:

- **Does the revenue match the traffic?** Divide the stated revenue by the stated visitors. If the result is far from what similar pages earn, ask why, and compare with the [traffic](/glossary/traffic/) records. See [how to verify website traffic](/guides/how-to-verify-website-traffic/).
- **Is growth coming from more visitors or better earning?** A higher RPV suggests the site is monetizing better; a flat RPV with more visitors suggests the growth depends on traffic.
- **Is it steady?** A sharp fall in RPV after a traffic spike can mean low-quality traffic.

Avoid quoting a "good" RPV. Without a published source, any single figure is a guess, because it depends on the product, the traffic source and the monetization model.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is revenue per visitor the same as RPM?

No, but they are linked. RPM is revenue per 1,000 visits, so RPM = RPV × 1,000. A site with an RPV of $0.05 has an RPM of $50.

### How do I raise revenue per visitor?

Raise the share of visitors who buy, raise the average order value, or improve how each page earns. Change one thing at a time so you can see which worked.
