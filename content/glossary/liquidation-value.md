## How it works

Liquidation value answers a hard question: what would the owner receive if the business had to be shut down and its assets sold soon? Because the sale is rushed, buyers pay less than they would in an ordinary sale. The U.S. Small Business Administration describes the assets approach as one that "subtracts total business liabilities from the total value of all assets." Liquidation value uses the same idea, but values each asset at a quick-sale price.

**Liquidation value = quick-sale value of assets − selling costs − liabilities**

It differs from [fair market value](/glossary/fair-market-value/), which assumes a willing buyer and a willing seller with time to find each other, and from [book value](/glossary/book-value/), which is the figure in the accounts.

## Example

A small online store is closing. The figures are made up.

| Item | Book value | Quick-sale value |
|---|---|---|
| Inventory | $30,000 | $15,000 |
| Equipment | $8,000 | $3,000 |
| Domain name and store | $12,000 | $6,000 |
| Cash | $5,000 | $5,000 |
| **Assets** | **$55,000** | **$29,000** |

Selling costs are $2,000. The business owes $9,000 to a supplier and $16,000 on a loan.

**Liquidation value = $29,000 − $2,000 − ($9,000 + $16,000) = $2,000**

On its books the store looks like it is worth $55,000 − $25,000 = $30,000. In a forced sale, the owner would keep about $2,000.

## Why liquidation value matters in a valuation

- **It is a floor.** A business that earns well is worth more running than closed. Liquidation value shows the least a buyer could be asked to accept.
- **It matters most when earnings are weak.** A loss-making business is often priced on its assets, because an earnings multiple cannot value a loss. See [asset-based valuation](/glossary/asset-based-valuation/).
- **It leaves out [goodwill](/glossary/goodwill/).** The reputation, audience and customer relationships that make a business worth buying are hard to sell in a rush.
- **It is not a price to pay.** A buyer who pays liquidation value for a working business is asking for a deep discount, and a seller should expect a higher number from an [asset sale](/glossary/asset-sale/) of a business that is still earning.
