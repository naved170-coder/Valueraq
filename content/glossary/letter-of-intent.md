## How it works

An LOI comes after the first conversations and before the detailed work. It records what the two sides think they have agreed, so that neither spends weeks on checks and legal drafting only to find they differ on a basic point.

A typical order of events:

1. The buyer reviews the listing and asks initial questions.
2. The buyer sends an LOI. The seller accepts it, rejects it or negotiates.
3. Once it is signed, the buyer carries out [due diligence](/glossary/due-diligence/).
4. The final contract is drafted and signed, and the sale closes.

## What an LOI usually covers

- **Price** and how it will be paid: cash at closing, [seller financing](/glossary/seller-financing/) or an [earnout](/glossary/earnout/).
- **Structure**: an [asset sale](/glossary/asset-sale/) or a sale of the company, and what is included.
- **Due diligence period**: how long the buyer has and what access the seller will give.
- **Exclusivity**: a period during which the seller agrees not to negotiate with other buyers.
- **Confidentiality**: how shared information may be used.
- **Transition**: how long the seller will help after closing, and any agreement not to compete.
- **Target closing date** and conditions that must be met first.

## Binding and non-binding parts

An LOI is usually written so that the price and deal terms are not binding. Either side can still walk away or renegotiate after due diligence. Certain clauses, most often exclusivity and confidentiality, are usually written to be binding.

Whether a clause actually binds depends on how the document is worded and on local law, not on its title. A document headed "non-binding" can still create obligations.

## Example

A buyer offers $150,000 for a SaaS business in an LOI: $120,000 at closing and $30,000 paid over 12 months, as an asset sale, with 30 days of exclusive due diligence and 60 days of handover support from the seller. The seller signs. During due diligence the buyer finds that churn is higher than stated and proposes a lower price. Because the price terms were not binding, the two sides renegotiate before the final contract.

## Why it matters when buying or selling

- **Sellers** give up the ability to talk to other buyers during exclusivity, so the period should be no longer than the checks reasonably need.
- **Buyers** get time to verify the numbers without being outbid.
- Terms left vague in the LOI tend to become arguments later. It is easier to settle them before either side has spent money.

## Take advice

Have a lawyer review an LOI before you sign it, particularly the exclusivity, confidentiality and any clause described as binding.
