## How it works

A site places ad units on its pages. An ad network or exchange fills them with ads from advertisers and pays the site. Payment is usually one of two kinds:

- **Per thousand views (CPM).** The site is paid a set amount for every 1,000 times an ad is shown.
- **Per click.** The site is paid only when a visitor clicks the ad.

Some owners join an ad network directly. Others use a management company that handles the ad setup for a share of the revenue. The network, not the site, sets the rates, and rates move with the season, the advertiser demand and the visitor's country.

## Example

A site shows 150,000 ads in a month and earns $6 for every 1,000 views. The figures are an illustration, not a market figure.

**Ad revenue = 150,000 ÷ 1,000 × $6 = $900 a month.**

If the site has 50,000 visits that month, its [RPM](/glossary/rpm/) from ads is $900 ÷ 50,000 × 1,000 = **$18**. If the rate falls to $4 per 1,000 views on the same traffic, revenue drops to 150,000 ÷ 1,000 × $4 = $600, a third lower, though nothing changed on the site.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Ad income rises and falls with traffic and with rates the owner does not control. Buyers therefore look at:

- **Twelve months of ad reports**, so that a seasonal peak is not mistaken for the normal level. Compare each month with the same month a year earlier.
- **The traffic behind it.** Ad revenue moves with visits, so buyers check the figures in analytics. See [how to verify website traffic](/guides/how-to-verify-website-traffic/).
- **Where the traffic comes from.** A site that relies on one search ranking can lose both traffic and ad income at once. See [organic traffic](/glossary/organic-traffic/).
- **Whether the ad account can change hands.** Ad networks have their own rules for account ownership, so read them before a sale.
