## Main types of digital business

| Type | How it earns | Usually valued on |
|---|---|---|
| [Website](/tools/website-valuation/) | Affiliate commissions, ads, leads, digital products | Annual profit |
| [SaaS](/tools/saas-valuation/) | Software subscriptions | ARR or profit |
| [AI business](/tools/ai-business-valuation/) | Subscriptions or usage fees for AI products | ARR or profit |
| [App](/tools/app-valuation/) | Subscriptions, in-app purchases, ads | Annual profit |
| [Newsletter](/tools/newsletter-valuation/) | Sponsorships, paid subscriptions | Annual profit |
| [Ecommerce](/tools/ecommerce-valuation/) | Product sales | Annual profit plus inventory |

## What they have in common

Digital businesses usually need little physical infrastructure, can be run remotely, and can often be transferred to a new owner by moving domains, code, accounts and data rather than buildings or equipment. That makes them comparatively easy to buy and sell.

They also share risks: reliance on platforms they don't control (search engines, app stores, marketplaces, model providers), fast-changing competition, and often heavy dependence on the founder.

## Example

A person who owns a cooking website, runs a recipe newsletter and sells a meal-planning app owns three digital businesses. Each would be valued differently: the website and newsletter on profit, and the app on profit adjusted for its store and retention risks.

## How digital businesses are valued

Most small digital businesses are valued as a multiple of annual profit or recurring revenue, adjusted for age, growth and risk. See [how digital businesses are valued](/guides/business-valuation/).
