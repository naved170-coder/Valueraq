## How it works

The formula is:

**EV to EBITDA multiple = enterprise value ÷ EBITDA.**

Turned around, it is how a price is built: **enterprise value = EBITDA × multiple.**

[Enterprise value](/glossary/enterprise-value/) is the value of the whole business. Corporate Finance Institute gives the formula as market capitalization plus the market value of debt, minus cash and equivalents. [EBITDA](/glossary/ebitda/) is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. Both sit on the "whole business" side, before debt payments, which is why they pair well.

## Example

A small online business earns EBITDA of $200,000 a year. A buyer and seller agree on a multiple of 4.0 times, which is an illustration, not market data.

- **Enterprise value:** $200,000 × 4.0 = $800,000.
- The business owes $50,000 in loans and holds $30,000 in cash.
- **Value to the owner:** $800,000 − $50,000 + $30,000 = $780,000.

Many small deals are priced "cash-free and debt-free", meaning the seller settles debts and keeps cash, so the headline price is close to the enterprise value. The contract should say which applies.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Multiples let a buyer compare one business with another and sense-check an asking price quickly. Corporate Finance Institute notes that a multiples approach is [quick but only considers one period](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/valuation/multiples-analysis) and depends on comparable companies using similar accounting.

Three cautions apply to small online businesses:

- **Owner pay.** EBITDA is normally measured after paying the owner a salary. Most very small businesses are priced on [SDE](/glossary/sde/) instead, which adds the owner's pay back. Using an EBITDA multiple on an SDE number overstates the price. See [SDE vs EBITDA](/guides/sde-vs-ebitda/).
- **Adjustments.** Check every add-back that was used to reach the EBITDA figure.
- **Source.** A multiple only means something if it comes from comparable deals. {{BRAND}}'s default profit multiples are platform assumptions, explained in the [methodology](/methodology/).
