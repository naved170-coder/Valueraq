## Formula

**CAC = total sales and marketing costs in a period ÷ new customers acquired in that period**

Include advertising spend, marketing tools, content and creative costs, sales staff or contractors, and affiliate or referral payouts. Be consistent about the period and about whether founder time is counted.

## Example

An app spends $6,000 on ads and $1,500 on creative and tools in a month, and gains 300 new paying users.

**CAC = ($6,000 + $1,500) ÷ 300 = $25.**

## Blended vs paid CAC

**Blended CAC** divides all acquisition spend by all new customers, including those who arrived organically. **Paid CAC** divides paid spend by customers from paid channels only. Blended CAC looks lower when a business gets many free customers; paid CAC shows whether advertising itself pays back.

## Why CAC matters for valuation

Buyers compare CAC with customer lifetime value ([LTV](/glossary/ltv/)). If it costs more to win a customer than that customer is worth, growth destroys value. A business that grows mainly through paid ads is also exposed to rising ad costs, which buyers treat as channel concentration.

## Worked example: a small online store

An online store spent these amounts last quarter.

| Cost | Amount |
|---|---|
| Paid ads | $9,000 |
| Email tool and creative | $1,000 |
| Contractor for ad management | $2,000 |
| **Total acquisition cost** | **$12,000** |

It gained 400 new customers, of whom 250 came from paid ads and 150 found it free.

- **Blended CAC** = $12,000 ÷ 400 = **$30**
- **Paid CAC** = ($9,000 + $2,000) ÷ 250 = **$44**, counting the ad spend and the contractor who manages it

If each customer brings $50 of gross profit over their time with the store, blended CAC leaves $20, but paid CAC leaves only $6. A buyer who sees only the blended figure sees a healthier business than the one that depends on paid ads.

## How buyers read CAC

Buyers rarely look at CAC alone. They compare it with:

1. **Lifetime value.** See [LTV](/glossary/ltv/). If CAC is higher than what a customer returns, growth loses money.
2. **Payback time.** How many months until a customer repays CAC. Longer payback needs more cash to grow.
3. **Trend.** Rising CAC often means a channel is getting more crowded or the audience is running out.
4. **Channel mix.** If most customers come from one paid channel, the business depends on its prices and rules.

Ask the seller how CAC was calculated, which costs were included, and for monthly figures, not a single average.

## CAC payback

**CAC payback (months) = CAC ÷ monthly gross profit per customer.** It shows how long it takes to recover the cost of acquiring a customer. Shorter is better, especially if churn is high.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good CAC?

There is no single number. It depends on what a customer returns. A $30 CAC is cheap for a customer worth $500 and expensive for one worth $35.

### Should CAC include the founder's time?

It can. Whatever you choose, say so and use the same rule every period, so a buyer can compare months fairly.

### Is CAC only for SaaS?

No. Stores, apps and newsletters with paid growth all have one. For a SaaS business, see the [SaaS valuation tool](/tools/saas-valuation/).
