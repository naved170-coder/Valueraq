## Formula

**Open rate = unique opens ÷ emails delivered**

"Delivered" means sent minus bounced. "Unique" means each recipient is counted once, however many times they open the email. Email platforms differ slightly in how they count, so compare figures from the same platform.

## Example

A newsletter issue is sent to 20,400 addresses and 400 bounce, so 20,000 are delivered. The platform records 9,000 unique opens.

**Open rate = 9,000 ÷ 20,000 = 45%.**

If 600 recipients click a link, the click rate is 600 ÷ 20,000 = 3%.

## Why reported open rates are inflated

Email platforms detect an open by placing a tiny invisible image in each email and recording when it is loaded. Privacy features in some mail apps now load those images automatically, whether or not the person reads the email. Apple Mail Privacy Protection, introduced in 2021, is the best-known example.

The result is that reported open rates are higher than real readership, sometimes by a wide margin, and the size of the effect depends on how many subscribers use those mail apps. Other apps block images by default, which hides real opens. Open rate is therefore a rough signal, not a precise count.

More reliable evidence of engagement:

- **Click rate**, since a click requires a person to act.
- **Replies** to the newsletter.
- **Unsubscribe and spam-complaint rates.**
- **Sales or sponsor results** that can be traced to an issue.

## Why open rate matters when buying or selling

Sponsors pay for attention, and paid subscribers renew only if they read. A buyer uses open rate, with the caveats above, to judge whether a list is active or padded with addresses that never engage.

When comparing newsletters, check that the figures cover the same period, come from the platform's own reports and state whether automatic opens were filtered out. A list that is cleaned regularly will show a higher open rate than one that keeps every inactive address.

## How {{BRAND}} uses it

The Newsletter Valuation Tool asks for the average open rate over the last 90 days. As a default assumption, an open rate under 25% reduces the multiple by 8%, 25% to 45% is the baseline, and above 45% adds 5%, with a note to check for inflation from privacy features.
