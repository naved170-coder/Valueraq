## How it works

Costs fall into two groups. **Fixed costs** stay the same whatever you sell, such as software subscriptions, hosting plans and salaries. **Variable costs** rise with each sale, such as payment processing, shipping or the store's cut of an app sale.

Each sale leaves a **contribution margin**: the price minus the variable cost of that sale. The break-even point is how many sales it takes for those contributions to cover the fixed costs.

**Break-even units = fixed costs ÷ (price per unit − variable cost per unit)**

**Break-even revenue = fixed costs ÷ contribution margin ratio**, where the ratio is (price − variable cost) ÷ price.

## Example

A small subscription app has $3,000 of fixed costs a month. Each subscriber pays $30 a month and costs $6 a month in variable costs.

- Contribution per subscriber: $30 − $6 = $24
- **Break-even subscribers = $3,000 ÷ $24 = 125**
- Contribution margin ratio: $24 ÷ $30 = 0.80
- **Break-even revenue = $3,000 ÷ 0.80 = $3,750 a month** (check: 125 × $30 = $3,750)

With 200 subscribers, profit is 200 × $24 − $3,000 = **$1,800 a month**.

### How fixed costs move the break-even

Same price and variable cost as above:

| Fixed costs per month | Break-even subscribers | Break-even revenue |
|---|---|---|
| $2,000 | 84 | $2,500 |
| $3,000 | 125 | $3,750 |
| $4,500 | 188 | $5,625 |

Subscribers are rounded up, because you cannot sell part of a subscription.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Buyers use the break-even point to see how much cushion a business has. If current sales are far above break-even, a revenue dip still leaves a profit. If they are just above it, a small loss of customers can turn profit into a loss.

For a business that is not yet profitable, break-even shows how far it has to grow, and how much cash it needs to get there (see [burn rate](/glossary/burn-rate/)). Sellers can help buyers by showing fixed and variable costs separately. Owner pay should be listed apart, since a buyer may run the business differently.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is break-even the same as profit?

No. At break-even, profit is zero. Every sale above it adds its contribution margin to profit.

### Does break-even include the owner's pay?

It can, and the answer changes the result. State whether it is included so a buyer can compare fairly.
