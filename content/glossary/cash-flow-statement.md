## How it works

A profit and loss statement shows profit. A cash flow statement shows cash. The two differ because profit counts sales when they are earned and costs when they are incurred, while cash only moves when money arrives or leaves.

The statement has three parts:

- **Operating activities:** cash from running the business, such as customer payments, supplier payments and staff pay.
- **Investing activities:** cash spent on or received from long-term items, such as equipment, software purchases or selling a domain.
- **Financing activities:** cash from loans, repaying loans, or money the owner puts in or takes out.

**Net change in cash = operating + investing + financing cash flows**

The result matches the change in the cash line on the [balance sheet](/glossary/balance-sheet/) between the start and end of the period.

## Example

A small online store reports $30,000 of [net income](/glossary/net-income/) for the year. Its cash flow statement tells a different story. The figures are made up.

| Section | Item | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Operating | Net income | $30,000 |
| Operating | Add back depreciation (no cash paid) | +$2,000 |
| Operating | Stock bought but not yet sold | −$14,000 |
| **Operating total** | | **$18,000** |
| Investing | New camera and packing equipment | −$5,000 |
| Financing | Loan repayment | −$4,000 |
| **Net change in cash** | 18,000 − 5,000 − 4,000 | **$9,000** |

Profit was $30,000, but cash rose by only $9,000, because the store spent money on stock, equipment and its loan.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Buyers pay for cash they can take out, not for profit on paper. A cash flow statement helps check three things:

- **Do profits turn into cash?** If operating cash flow is much lower than profit year after year, ask where the money goes. Growing stock or slow-paying customers are common reasons. See [working capital](/glossary/working-capital/).
- **How much must be reinvested?** Large investing outflows reduce the cash a new owner can keep.
- **How is the business funded?** Heavy loan repayments affect what a buyer inherits.

Valuation methods such as discounted cash flow start from [cash flow](/glossary/cash-flow/) rather than profit, and [free cash flow to firm](/glossary/free-cash-flow-to-firm/) is built from the same operating and investing figures.
