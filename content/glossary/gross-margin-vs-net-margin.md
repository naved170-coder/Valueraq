## How it works

Both numbers are percentages of revenue. They differ in how many costs they subtract.

- **Gross margin** = (revenue − [cost of goods sold](/glossary/cost-of-goods-sold/)) ÷ revenue × 100. It leaves out everything that is not a direct cost of the product.
- **Net margin** = [net profit](/glossary/net-profit/) ÷ revenue × 100. It subtracts every cost, including [operating expenses](/glossary/operating-expenses/), interest and taxes.

Net margin is always the same as or lower than gross margin, because it subtracts more.

## Example

A store has $100,000 of revenue in a year. The figures are made up.

| Line | Amount | Margin |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $100,000 | 100% |
| Product cost, shipping and payment fees (direct) | −$55,000 | |
| **Gross profit** | **$45,000** | **45% gross margin** |
| Ads, software, contractors and other running costs | −$25,000 | |
| Interest and taxes | −$5,000 | |
| **Net profit** | **$15,000** | **15% net margin** |

The same business is "45%" on one measure and "15%" on the other. Neither is wrong. They answer different questions: gross margin asks how much each sale earns before overhead, and net margin asks how much the owner keeps.

## Which one to look at

Use gross margin to judge the product: pricing, product cost, shipping and payment fees. Use net margin to judge the whole business: whether the owner's work, ads and overhead leave a healthy profit. Track both over the same months, and compare each with your own past results before comparing with anyone else's.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Buyers of small online businesses mostly apply a multiple to profit, so net profit sits at the centre of the price. Gross margin still matters for two reasons:

- **Room to grow.** A high gross margin means each extra dollar of revenue leaves more to cover costs, so profit can rise faster as sales rise.
- **Where the gap is.** A good gross margin with a thin net margin points at overhead or ad spend. A thin gross margin points at product cost, pricing or shipping.

When a seller quotes only one of the two, ask for the other. See VALUERAQ's [methodology](/methodology/) for how profit is used to build a default range.
