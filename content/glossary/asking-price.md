## How it works

The asking price is the seller's opening number. It is usually set as yearly profit multiplied by a [valuation multiple](/glossary/valuation-multiple/), then adjusted for anything the seller believes adds value, such as growth or low owner workload. A seller may also leave room to negotiate.

An asking price is a statement of what the seller hopes to get. It is not a measure of worth. The U.S. Small Business Administration says to "use business valuation to set a monetary value," which is a different step from choosing a price to list at.

## Example

A newsletter earns $50,000 a year in profit. The seller lists it at 4 times profit.

- **Asking price = $50,000 × 4 = $200,000.**
- A buyer who values it at 3 times profit offers $150,000.
- They settle at $170,000, which is 3.4 times profit.

The sale price, $170,000, is lower than the asking price by $30,000, or 15%. The asking price is the starting point, and the sale price is where the two sides ended.

## Asking price vs sale price

The sale price is the amount actually agreed. It can be lower than the asking price after negotiation, or after a buyer's checks find that revenue or profit was lower than stated. Buyers should compare the asking price with proven profit, and sellers should expect to be asked to prove it.

## Why it matters in a valuation

- **For buyers.** Divide the asking price by yearly profit to see the multiple the seller is asking. Compare it with {{BRAND}}'s [default ranges](/methodology/), which are assumptions and not market data.
- **For sellers.** A price set far above what buyers can justify tends to draw few offers. A realistic price based on verified profit is easier to defend.
- **For both.** The gap between asking price and [fair market value](/glossary/fair-market-value/) is where most negotiation happens.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the asking price negotiable?

Usually, yes. It is an opening number, and buyers often offer below it, especially if checks find lower profit than stated.

### What is the difference between asking price and value?

Value is an estimate of what the business is worth from its earnings and risks. The asking price is what the seller chooses to ask. They can differ in either direction.

### Does a lower asking price mean a better deal?

Not on its own. Divide the price by proven profit. A low price on weak or unverifiable profit can cost more than a higher price on clean records.
