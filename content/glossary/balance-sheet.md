## How it works

A balance sheet is a snapshot. Where a profit and loss statement covers a period, the balance sheet shows one day, such as December 31. It rests on one equation:

**Assets = Liabilities + Equity**

- **Assets** are what the business owns: cash, money customers owe it (receivables), inventory, equipment, domains and software.
- **Liabilities** are what it owes: unpaid bills, loans, taxes due and refunds owed to customers.
- **Equity** is the difference, the owner's share after the debts are paid.

## Example

A small online store has these figures on one date.

| Assets | Amount | Liabilities and equity | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash | $40,000 | Unpaid supplier bills | $6,000 |
| Customer payments due | $5,000 | Business loan | $14,000 |
| Inventory | $10,000 | **Total liabilities** | **$20,000** |
| | | **Equity** | **$35,000** |
| **Total assets** | **$55,000** | **Total liabilities + equity** | **$55,000** |

Equity = $55,000 − $20,000 = **$35,000**. Both sides equal $55,000, so the sheet balances. Equity is also the business's [book value](/glossary/book-value/).

## Why it matters in a valuation

Small online businesses are usually priced on profit (see [SDE](/glossary/sde/) and [profit multiples](/glossary/profit-multiple/)), not on the balance sheet. A website or app may own little beyond a domain and code, so most of its value is earning power, which does not appear as an asset.

The balance sheet still matters in a purchase. Buyers use it to see how much debt comes with the business, how much cash and [working capital](/glossary/working-capital/) is in it, and whether inventory is worth what the books say. In an [asset sale](/glossary/asset-sale/), the buyer picks the assets and debts it takes. Ask for balance sheets at several dates, not just one, and match them to bank records.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a balance sheet the same as a profit and loss statement?

No. The balance sheet is a position on one date; the profit and loss statement shows income and costs over a period. Buyers want both.

### Do small online businesses have a balance sheet?

Many do not keep one. A buyer can ask for one, or build it from bank statements, loan records and the inventory count with an accountant's help.
