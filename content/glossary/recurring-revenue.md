## How it works

A customer agrees to pay on a repeating schedule, and the payment continues until they cancel. Stripe describes monthly recurring revenue as the amount a company can expect to receive from its existing customers each month. Common forms are:

- **Subscriptions**: monthly or yearly software plans, memberships and paid newsletters.
- **Retainers and service contracts**: a fixed fee for ongoing work.
- **Usage plans with a minimum**: a base fee that repeats whatever the usage.

One-off sales, setup fees and single purchases are not recurring, even if the same customer buys again later.

## Example

A software business has 200 customers paying $30 a month, plus $4,000 of one-off setup fees this month.

**Monthly recurring revenue = 200 × $30 = $6,000.** The $4,000 is left out. Annual recurring revenue is $6,000 × 12 = $72,000.

## Why recurring revenue matters in a valuation

Income that repeats is easier to forecast, so buyers see less risk in it. That is why VALUERAQ values SaaS and AI businesses on annual recurring revenue (default range of 2.0 to 5.0 times for SaaS and 1.5 to 4.5 times for AI), while websites, stores, apps and newsletters are valued on profit. These are VALUERAQ's published assumptions, not market data; see the [methodology](/methodology/).

Recurring does not mean permanent. Customers leave, so a buyer will check the [churn](/glossary/churn/) rate and [net revenue retention](/glossary/net-revenue-retention/) before trusting the figure.

## What buyers check

A buyer will usually ask how much of total revenue is truly recurring, how long customers stay, whether contracts are monthly or yearly, and whether a few large customers make up most of it. Yearly plans paid up front are good for cash, but the buyer will want to know how many renew. Keep the recurring and one-off parts of revenue in separate lines in your reports, so the split is easy to see.

## Related terms

[MRR](/glossary/mrr/) is recurring revenue per month and [ARR](/glossary/arr/) is the yearly equivalent. A [SaaS](/glossary/saas/) business is the most common example.
