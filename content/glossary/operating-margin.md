## How it works

The formula is:

**Operating margin = operating income ÷ revenue × 100.**

[Operating income](/glossary/operating-income/) is revenue minus the cost of goods and all [operating expenses](/glossary/operating-expenses/), such as ads, software, staff and rent. It is measured before interest and income taxes, which is why operating margin is also called the [EBIT](/glossary/ebit/) margin.

Three margins are often confused:

| Margin | What it leaves out | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| [Gross margin](/glossary/gross-margin/) | Only direct costs are taken off | Pricing and product cost |
| Operating margin | Direct and running costs taken off, interest and tax left out | Efficiency of day-to-day running |
| [Net profit](/glossary/net-profit/) margin | Everything taken off | What the owners keep |

[EBITDA margin](/glossary/ebitda-margin/) is close to operating margin, but it also adds back depreciation and amortization, so it is the higher of the two when a business has those costs.

## Example

A store has $400,000 in yearly revenue. The figures are made up to show the arithmetic.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Revenue | $400,000 |
| Cost of goods | −$180,000 |
| Operating expenses | −$140,000 |
| **Operating income** | **$80,000** |
| **Operating margin** | $80,000 ÷ $400,000 = **20%** |

The business keeps 20 cents of each sales dollar after the cost of goods and running costs. A second store with the same $400,000 in revenue and $40,000 of operating income has a 10% margin, which is half as efficient even though sales are equal.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Buyers of online businesses pay for earnings, so margin explains why two businesses with the same revenue are priced differently. A margin that is steady or rising suggests costs are controlled. A falling margin with flat sales raises a question a seller should be ready to answer.

Small businesses often show the owner's pay inside operating expenses. If a buyer will not pay that salary, the true earnings are higher, which moves the figure toward [SDE](/guides/sde-vs-ebitda/). Know which measure a listing is quoting before you compare margins. Check the figures against the [revenue multiple](/glossary/revenue-multiple/) too: a price based on revenue should still be supported by a margin that turns sales into profit.
