## How it works

The formula is:

**EBITDA margin = EBITDA ÷ revenue × 100.**

[EBITDA](/glossary/ebitda/) measures operating earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. Dividing it by revenue puts the figure on a common scale, so a small business and a larger one, or this year and last year, can be compared.

It sits between two other margins. [Gross margin](/glossary/gross-margin/) looks only at the direct cost of sales. [Net profit](/glossary/net-profit/) is what remains after everything, including interest and tax. EBITDA margin looks at the day-to-day running of the business, after [operating expenses](/glossary/operating-expenses/) but before financing and accounting choices.

## Example

Two online businesses each have $500,000 in yearly revenue.

| | Business A | Business B |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $500,000 | $500,000 |
| EBITDA | $150,000 | $75,000 |
| **EBITDA margin** | $150,000 ÷ $500,000 = **30%** | $75,000 ÷ $500,000 = **15%** |

Same sales, but A keeps 30 cents of every dollar as operating earnings and B keeps 15. If a buyer pays the same multiple of EBITDA for each, A is worth twice as much, even though its revenue is no larger. The figures are made up to show the arithmetic.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Buyers of online businesses value earnings, not sales, so a margin explains why two businesses with the same revenue can sell for different prices. A rising margin suggests costs are under control. A falling margin with flat revenue is a question the seller should be ready to answer.

Margins also expose problems in the numbers. A margin far above what similar businesses show deserves a check of the records and of every add-back used. For very small businesses the owner's pay is often added back, which makes the figure closer to [SDE](/guides/sde-vs-ebitda/) than to true EBITDA, so make sure you know which one is being quoted.

Use margin together with the [revenue multiple](/glossary/revenue-multiple/): a business valued on revenue should still show it can turn sales into earnings.
