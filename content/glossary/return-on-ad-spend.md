## How it works

The formula is:

**ROAS = revenue from ads ÷ ad spend**

It is usually written as a ratio such as 4:1 or "4x". A ROAS of 4 means every $1 of ads brought in $4 of sales. It counts revenue, not profit, so it says nothing about product cost, shipping, fees or refunds.

Ad platforms report ROAS using their own method of crediting sales, which can differ from what a shop's own records show. For a decision that matters, compare the platform figure with orders in the store.

## Example

A store spends $5,000 on ads in a month and the ads are credited with $20,000 of sales. ROAS is $20,000 ÷ $5,000 = 4.

Now add the product margin. If [gross margin](/glossary/gross-margin/) is 40%, the $20,000 of sales leaves $8,000 of gross profit. After $5,000 of ad cost, $3,000 is left to cover everything else. The break-even ROAS is 1 ÷ 0.40 = 2.5: below that, each ad dollar loses money before other costs. At a ROAS of 4 the store is above break-even, but the margin of safety depends on the other costs. The figures are an illustration, not a benchmark.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Buyers of ecommerce businesses care about how the sales are produced. A store whose growth comes from paid ads depends on those ads staying affordable. If costs rise or an ad account is suspended, the profit can fall quickly.

When reading a seller's numbers, buyers usually check:

- **Ad spend inside the profit figure.** Profit that leaves out ad costs is overstated.
- **ROAS over time.** A falling ratio can mean the best audiences are used up.
- **Break-even ROAS.** How far the current figure sits above it.
- **The share of sales from paid ads.** A high share is a [channel concentration](/guides/ecommerce-valuation/) risk.
