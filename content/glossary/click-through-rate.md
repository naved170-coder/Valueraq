## How it works

Click-through rate compares how many people saw something with how many acted on it.

**CTR = clicks ÷ impressions (or emails delivered) × 100**

The denominator depends on where you measure. For an ad it is the number of times the ad was shown. For a link in an email it is usually the number of emails delivered, though some tools divide by the number opened instead. Those two methods give different answers, so always say which one you used.

## Example

A newsletter delivers an issue to 4,000 subscribers. The sponsor's link receives 120 clicks. The figures are an illustration, not a market figure.

**CTR = 120 ÷ 4,000 × 100 = 3%.**

If the same issue was opened by 1,600 people, the clicks as a share of opens are 120 ÷ 1,600 = 7.5%. Both numbers are correct; they answer different questions. The first measures the whole list, and the second measures only readers who opened the email.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Buyers of newsletters and content sites want proof that the audience pays attention, not only that it exists. A steady click-through rate over many issues suggests readers act on what they are sent. A falling one can warn of a tired list, even while the subscriber count looks healthy.

Sponsors may be paid per click, so CTR also ties directly to income. Combined with the [conversion rate](/glossary/conversion-rate/) of the page that follows, it shows how a click becomes revenue.
