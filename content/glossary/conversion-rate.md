## Formula

**Conversion rate = conversions ÷ visitors (or sessions) × 100**

Always state what counts as a conversion and whether the base is sessions or users, because the choice changes the result.

## Example

An online store has 40,000 sessions in a month and 800 orders.

**Conversion rate = 800 ÷ 40,000 × 100 = 2%.**

If the average order is $60, revenue per session is 2% × $60 = $1.20.

**Quick answer:** a conversion rate of 2% means 2 of every 100 visitors completed the action. Divide the actions by the visitors and multiply by 100.

### Why the base matters

The same store gives different answers depending on what you divide by. The figures below are made up.

| Base | Count | Orders | Conversion rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sessions | 40,000 | 800 | 2.0% |
| Unique visitors | 25,000 | 800 | 3.2% |
| Product page views | 16,000 | 800 | 5.0% |

Each figure is correct. They answer different questions, so a buyer should confirm which one the seller reports before comparing it with anything.

### Sense-check: what a change is worth

A buyer can use conversion rate to test a revenue claim. Using the store above, 40,000 sessions at $60 an order:

| Conversion rate | Orders | Monthly revenue |
|---|---|---|
| 1.5% | 600 | $36,000 |
| 2.0% | 800 | $48,000 |
| 2.5% | 1,000 | $60,000 |

A half-point change in conversion rate moves revenue by $12,000 a month when traffic stays the same. If a seller's revenue rose that much in the months before a sale, ask whether traffic, conversion or order size caused it.

## Where conversion rate is used

- **Ecommerce:** sessions to orders.
- **SaaS:** trial sign-ups to paying customers.
- **Lead generation sites:** visits to submitted enquiries.
- **Newsletters:** visitors to subscribers.
- **Affiliate sites:** clicks on affiliate links to commissions.

## Why conversion rate matters for valuation

Conversion rate links [traffic](/glossary/traffic/) to revenue. A buyer who understands it can see how much revenue depends on keeping traffic, and where they might improve results. A sharp change in conversion rate before a sale deserves a question: it can come from a genuine improvement, a change in how conversions are tracked, or a change in traffic mix.

Conversion rates vary widely by industry, price, device and traffic source, so compare a business with its own history rather than with general benchmarks.

## What a buyer checks

- **Definition.** What counts as a conversion, and is the base sessions, users or page views?
- **Trend by month.** Is the rate steady, or did it jump just before the sale?
- **Split by source.** Paid traffic, search, email and direct visitors usually convert differently.
- **Matching records.** Orders in the analytics tool should match orders in the store or payment records.
- **Tracking changes.** A new analytics setup can change the rate without any real change in sales.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good conversion rate?

It depends on the product, the price and where visitors come from, so a single figure would mislead. Compare a business with its own past months and its own traffic sources.

### Is conversion rate the same as click-through rate?

No. [Click-through rate](/glossary/click-through-rate/) is the share of people who click a link or ad. Conversion rate is the share who then complete the action, such as buying.

### How do I raise a conversion rate?

Change one thing at a time, such as page speed, price clarity or checkout steps, and compare the months before and after. Changing many things at once hides which one worked.
