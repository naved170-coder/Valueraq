## Formula

**MRR = sum of each active customer's monthly subscription amount**

For annual or multi-month plans, divide the contract value by the number of months it covers. For a plan billed at $600 a year, that customer contributes $50 of MRR.

## MRR movements

Buyers usually ask to see how MRR changed each month, split into:

| Movement | Meaning |
|---|---|
| New MRR | From customers who started paying this month |
| Expansion MRR | Upgrades and added seats from existing customers |
| Contraction MRR | Downgrades from existing customers |
| Churned MRR | Lost from customers who cancelled |

**Net new MRR = new + expansion − contraction − churned.**

## Example

A business starts the month with $15,000 MRR. It gains $1,500 from new customers and $400 from upgrades, loses $200 to downgrades and $600 to cancellations.

Net new MRR = $1,500 + $400 − $200 − $600 = $1,100. **Closing MRR = $16,100.**

## Common MRR mistakes

- Counting an annual payment as MRR in the month it's received.
- Including one-off fees or services.
- Counting discounted customers at full price.
- Keeping cancelled customers in MRR until the end of a period they've already left.

## Why MRR matters for valuation

MRR is the input to [ARR](/glossary/arr/) (MRR × 12), which is the base of most SaaS valuations. Its month-by-month trend shows growth and [churn](/glossary/churn/) more clearly than annual figures. The [SaaS Valuation Tool](/tools/saas-valuation/) asks for current MRR.
