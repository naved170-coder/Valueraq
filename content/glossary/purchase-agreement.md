## How it works

The purchase agreement is signed after [due diligence](/glossary/due-diligence/) and replaces the earlier [letter of intent](/glossary/letter-of-intent/), whose price terms were usually not binding. For a small online business it is often an [asset purchase agreement](/glossary/asset-sale/). It typically covers:

- **The assets sold:** domain, website, code, content, accounts, customer or subscriber lists, and any inventory.
- **The price and payment:** the amount, when it is due, and any [seller financing](/glossary/seller-financing/) or [earnout](/glossary/earnout/). See [deal structures](/guides/deal-structures-for-online-business-sales/).
- **How the money is held:** often through [escrow](/glossary/escrow/), with an inspection period.
- **Statements and promises:** what the seller says is true about the business, such as the figures and ownership of the assets.
- **Handover:** what the seller will do after closing and for how long.
- **Restrictions:** such as a [non-compete](/glossary/non-compete-agreement/) or confidentiality terms.
- **What happens if something is untrue,** and how disagreements are settled.

## Example

A buyer agrees to pay $80,000 for a content website. The agreement says $72,000 is paid into escrow at signing, $8,000 is held back for 90 days, the seller transfers the domain, files and analytics accounts within five days, and gives four weeks of handover help.

**Paid at closing = $80,000 − $8,000 = $72,000.** If no problem is raised in 90 days, the $8,000 is released.

## Why it matters in a valuation

The price on a listing becomes real only through this document. The agreement decides how much is certain on closing day, what is excluded, and who bears the risk if a figure turns out wrong. Two deals with the same price can be worth different amounts to each side depending on the terms.

Vague wording is the usual source of later disputes. A list of assets should be specific, and any adjustment to the price should state how it is calculated.

## Take advice

The U.S. Small Business Administration recommends that an attorney review the sales agreement ([SBA](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/manage-your-business/close-or-sell-your-business)). Contract rules differ by country, so have a lawyer draft or review the agreement before you sign.
