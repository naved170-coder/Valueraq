## Formula

**Profit multiple = price ÷ annual profit**

Website marketplaces often quote a **monthly multiple** instead: price ÷ monthly profit. Divide a monthly multiple by 12 to get the annual one. A "36× multiple" on a website listing means 36 months of profit, or 3× annual profit.

## Example

A newsletter earns $5,000 a month in net profit, or $60,000 a year, and sells for $210,000.

- Annual profit multiple: $210,000 ÷ $60,000 = **3.5×**
- Monthly multiple: $210,000 ÷ $5,000 = **42 months**

## What moves a profit multiple

| Higher multiple | Lower multiple |
|---|---|
| Several years of stable or growing profit | Short history or declining profit |
| Diverse traffic, customers and revenue sources | Dependence on one channel, customer or platform |
| Few owner hours, documented processes | Heavy owner involvement |
| Verified, clean records | Unverifiable or mixed personal and business finances |

## Profit multiples on {{BRAND}}

The website, app, newsletter and ecommerce [tools](/tools/) value businesses as a multiple of annual profit (SDE), starting from default ranges published in the [methodology](/methodology/). Every marketplace listing shows its implied profit multiple.

## Profit vs revenue multiples

Profit multiples reflect what the owner keeps, which is why they're standard for owner-run online businesses. [Revenue multiples](/glossary/revenue-multiple/) are more common for SaaS. See [revenue vs profit multiples](/guides/revenue-vs-profit-multiples/).
