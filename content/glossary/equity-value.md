## How it works

[Enterprise value](/glossary/enterprise-value/) is the value of the whole business to everyone with a claim on it. Equity value is the part that belongs to the owners once lenders are paid. The Corporate Finance Institute gives enterprise value as market capitalization plus debt minus cash. Rearranged, that gives equity value:

**Equity value = enterprise value − debt + cash**

If a business has no debt and no spare cash, the two values are the same. Most small online businesses are valued on earnings with a multiple, which produces an enterprise-style figure first, and then debt and cash are dealt with in the deal terms.

## Example

A software business has $120,000 of annual earnings, and a buyer and seller agree on a multiple of 3.0 for the whole business. The figures are made up.

| Step | Amount |
|---|---|
| Enterprise value ($120,000 × 3.0) | $360,000 |
| Less: bank loan | −$40,000 |
| Plus: cash in the business | +$25,000 |
| **Equity value** | **$345,000** |

Check: 360,000 − 40,000 + 25,000 = 345,000.

If the seller repays the loan from the sale and keeps the cash, the buyer is paying for the business, and the seller's net depends on those two lines. Say clearly in the contract whether the price assumes the business comes with its cash and debt, or free of both.

## Why equity value matters in a valuation

- **It is the number the owner receives.** Enterprise value is the headline; equity value is closer to what the owner takes home before costs and tax.
- **Two offers can look equal and differ.** An offer of $360,000 for a business with $40,000 of debt is not the same as $360,000 for one with none.
- **It follows the deal structure.** A sale of the company's shares carries its debts. An [asset sale](/glossary/asset-sale/) usually does not. See [stock sale](/glossary/stock-sale/).
- **It is not book value.** [Book value](/glossary/book-value/) comes from the balance sheet at cost. Equity value comes from what a buyer will pay.

For how to reach the earnings number and a range of multiples, see [how to value a tech company](/guides/how-to-value-a-tech-company/).
