## How it works

When someone buys a business, they are paying for its customers, audience and know-how. If the seller could open a copy the next week, much of that value would walk away. So the purchase agreement often includes a non-compete: the seller agrees not to compete for a fixed period.

A clear non-compete states:

- **The time**, for example two years from closing.
- **The scope**, meaning what counts as competing, such as a similar website on the same topic or a similar software product.
- **The area or market**, which for an online business may be a topic or audience rather than a place.
- **What is allowed**, such as the seller's other, unrelated projects.
- **What happens on a breach**, which the agreement spells out.

It is often paired with a [non-disclosure agreement](/glossary/non-disclosure-agreement/) and with a promise not to take customers or staff.

## Example

A seller sells a newsletter about home budgeting for $150,000. The agreement says the seller will not start or run another newsletter about personal budgeting for 24 months after closing, but may keep writing about travel. The figures are an illustration, not a market term.

If the seller launched a new budgeting newsletter after eight months and invited the old readers, the buyer would have grounds to raise a breach under the agreement. Whether it holds up, and what the buyer can claim, depends on the contract and on the law that applies.

## Why it matters in a valuation

A buyer is paying for earnings that should continue. A non-compete protects them from the seller's competition, which matters most when the seller is the face of the brand or holds the audience. For a seller, the same clause limits future plans, so read it before agreeing. A very broad clause, such as one that covers every online business for many years, is a reason to ask questions.

Rules on whether a non-compete is valid differ between countries and, in some places, between regions, so the agreement should be written or checked by a lawyer. This page is not legal advice.
