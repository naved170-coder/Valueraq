## How it works

A data room is a shared folder, often online, that the seller sets up after a buyer has signed a [non-disclosure agreement](/glossary/non-disclosure-agreement/) and usually after a [letter of intent](/glossary/letter-of-intent/). Instead of answering the same questions one email at a time, the seller puts the records in one place with a clear folder structure, and the buyer works through them during [due diligence](/glossary/due-diligence/).

For a small online business the folders often look like this:

- **Financials:** monthly profit and loss, bank and payment-processor statements, tax filings.
- **Traffic and sales:** analytics exports, search reports, order or subscriber data.
- **Assets and accounts:** the domain, code, hosting, app-store or store accounts, and a list of who holds each login.
- **Contracts:** supplier, advertiser, affiliate and customer agreements, and software subscriptions.
- **Operations:** how the business runs week to week, and the owner's tasks.

Access is normally limited to named people, and a seller may open more folders as the buyer moves closer to closing.

## Example

A seller lists a newsletter for $90,000 and a buyer signs an NDA and a letter of intent. The seller creates a shared folder with 24 months of monthly revenue, sponsor invoices, subscriber exports and the email platform's billing history. The buyer compares the invoices with the bank statements, finds the numbers agree, and asks two follow-up questions inside a week. Without a data room, the same checks might have taken several rounds of email.

## Why it matters in a valuation

A buyer prices a business on the figures they can verify. A tidy data room makes verification quick and shows that the seller keeps good records, which supports the asking price. A messy or incomplete one makes a buyer cautious and often leads to a lower offer or a longer delay.

It also protects the seller. Sharing records in stages, only with people who have signed an NDA, limits how much sensitive information leaves their hands before a sale is likely.

## Take advice

Which records you may share, and how, can depend on privacy rules and on contracts with your customers. Ask a lawyer before sharing personal data about customers or staff.
