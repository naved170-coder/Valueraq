## How it works

**Customer retention cost = total retention spend in a period ÷ customers kept in that period**

Retention spend is whatever you pay to keep people who already buy. Typical items are customer support, customer success staff, renewal reminders, loyalty discounts, and the software used for those jobs. Money spent to win new customers does not belong here. That is [customer acquisition cost](/glossary/cac/) (CAC).

Pick one period and keep it the same each time, usually a month, so that the figure can be compared over time.

## Example

A subscription site has 500 paying customers. In one month it spends:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Support and contractor time | $3,000 |
| Customer success and onboarding emails | $2,000 |
| Loyalty discounts given to at-risk customers | $500 |
| Retention and help-desk software | $500 |
| **Total retention spend** | **$6,000** |

**CRC = $6,000 ÷ 500 = $12 per customer per month.** If each customer pays $40 a month, retention takes 30% of revenue ($12 ÷ $40). The figures are invented to show the arithmetic.

## CRC vs CAC

| | Retention cost (CRC) | Acquisition cost (CAC) |
|---|---|---|
| Who it is spent on | Existing customers | New customers |
| Typical spend | Support, success, loyalty offers | Ads, sales time, promotions |
| Divided by | Customers kept | New customers won |

## Why it matters in a valuation

A buyer of a subscription business reads CRC next to [churn](/glossary/churn/) and [lifetime value](/glossary/ltv/). Two things follow.

First, retention cost is a real running cost, so it lowers profit and therefore the value. Cutting it can look attractive: removing $1,000 a month of spend adds $12,000 a year to earnings, and at a 3.2x multiple that is $38,400 of value on paper. But a buyer will ask whether churn rose when the spend fell. If it did, the extra profit is not sustainable.

Second, a business that keeps customers cheaply is easier to predict. Low churn at a modest CRC suggests the product holds people by itself, which supports a stronger multiple than one that must keep paying to stop people leaving. See [what buyers check in SaaS metrics](/guides/saas-metrics-buyers-check/).

The 3.2x multiple is VALUERAQ's default midpoint for profit-based businesses, explained in the [methodology](/methodology/), not market data.
