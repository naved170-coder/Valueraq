## How it works

At closing, the buyer pays most of the price. A smaller share stays with a neutral party, often an [escrow](/glossary/escrow/) provider, for an agreed period. If no valid claim is made in that period, the money is released to the seller. If the buyer has a claim the agreement allows, such as a statement about revenue that proves false, the claim can be paid from the held amount.

The [purchase agreement](/glossary/purchase-agreement/) should state:

- **The amount**, as a figure or a share of the price.
- **The period**, for example 90 days.
- **What counts as a valid claim**, and how the buyer must give notice.
- **Who decides** if the two sides disagree.
- **When and how** the money is released.

## Example

A buyer agrees to pay $120,000 for a website, with 10% held back for 90 days.

- Paid at closing: $120,000 x 90% = **$108,000**.
- Held back: $120,000 - $108,000 = **$12,000**.

If no claim is made in 90 days, the seller receives the $12,000. If the buyer proves a $4,000 claim that the agreement covers, the seller receives $12,000 - $4,000 = **$8,000**. The percentage and period here are made-up figures to show the arithmetic.

## Why it matters in a valuation

A holdback means the price you see is not all certain on closing day. A seller should compare offers on the amount received at closing, and treat the held-back share as paid later and only if no claim is made. A buyer gets a practical remedy, which is easier than chasing a seller for money after it has been paid out.

A holdback is not the same as an [earnout](/glossary/earnout/). An earnout depends on the business hitting a future target. A holdback depends only on whether something the seller said was untrue. It also differs from [seller financing](/glossary/seller-financing/), which is a debt owed in instalments whatever happens.

See [deal structures for online business sales](/guides/deal-structures-for-online-business-sales/) for the four structures compared on one price.

Contract and tax rules vary by country and by deal, so take legal advice before you sign.

## Related terms

- [Escrow](/glossary/escrow/): a neutral party holding money until conditions are met.
- [Earnout](/glossary/earnout/): price paid later, tied to results.
- [Purchase agreement](/glossary/purchase-agreement/): the contract that sets out the holdback.
