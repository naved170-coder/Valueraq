## How it works

Many digital businesses rent their audience. A website may get most of its visits from one search engine, a store may make most of its sales on one marketplace, an app may sit in two app stores, and an AI product may run on one provider's model. The owner can do everything right and still lose income if the platform changes how it ranks, charges, approves or pays.

Platform risk shows up in three ways:

- **Rules and policy.** The platform bans a product type, a tactic or an account.
- **Price.** The platform raises fees, commission rates or usage charges.
- **Visibility.** The platform changes how it ranks or recommends, and traffic or sales fall.

It is a close cousin of [customer concentration](/glossary/customer-concentration/). In both, one outside party holds a large share of the income, and the owner cannot negotiate with it.

## Example

A hypothetical store makes $30,000 of sales in a typical month. $24,000 comes from one marketplace and $6,000 from its own website.

**Largest channel share = $24,000 ÷ $30,000 = 80%.**

If the marketplace suspended the account, 80% of sales would stop at once. The figures are an illustration, not market data.

## Why it matters in a valuation

A buyer pays for profit that will continue. If one platform can switch it off, the buyer is taking on a risk the seller never priced.

{{BRAND}}'s default assumptions reflect this. For every business type, the share of traffic or sales from the largest channel adjusts the multiple: no change at 60% or less, 6% off above 60% up to 80%, and 12% off above 80%. In the example above, 80% falls in the middle band, so the multiple would be cut by 6%. Other defaults follow the same idea: affiliate revenue takes 3% off because programs can change commission rates, and an AI business that relies on one model provider takes 10% off. The full rules are in the [methodology](/methodology/).

You can lower the risk, and the price discount that goes with it, by building owned channels such as an email list, adding a second sales channel, and keeping records that show how stable each channel has been. Buyers will ask for sales or traffic by channel for at least a year. See [due diligence](/guides/digital-business-due-diligence/).
