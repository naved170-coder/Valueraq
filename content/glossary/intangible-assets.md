## How it works

A tangible asset is something you can touch: equipment, a computer, stock on a shelf. An intangible asset cannot be touched, but it still produces income or lowers costs. The U.S. Small Business Administration, in its guide to selling a business, says the property tied to a business "can include intangible assets like brand presence, intellectual property, customer information, and projection of future revenue."

Common examples in an online business:

- Domain names and the website itself.
- Brand name, logo and trademarks.
- Source code, apps and software.
- Written content, videos and images.
- Email and customer lists.
- Search rankings, accounts and audience.

Some intangibles can be listed and valued one by one. Others, such as reputation, cannot, and show up as [goodwill](/glossary/goodwill/) in a sale.

## Example

A seller lists the assets of a small content site with a $60,000 asking price. The figures are made up.

| Asset | Type | Share of price |
|---|---|---|
| Laptop and camera | Tangible | $2,000 |
| Domain and website | Intangible | $30,000 |
| Content library | Intangible | $18,000 |
| Email list | Intangible | $7,000 |
| Brand and social accounts | Intangible | $3,000 |
| **Total** | | **$60,000** |

Intangible assets are $30,000 + $18,000 + $7,000 + $3,000 = $58,000, or $58,000 ÷ $60,000 = 96.7% of the price. For most digital businesses, almost everything a buyer pays for has no physical form.

## Why intangible assets matter in a valuation

- **They are the product.** A buyer of a website, app or newsletter is buying audience, content and rankings, not equipment.
- **They need proof of ownership.** A domain registered to a past partner or a logo made by a contractor with no written transfer can undermine the sale. [Due diligence](/glossary/due-diligence/) should check each one.
- **They are hard to price on their own.** That is why most small online businesses are valued on earnings, not on a sum of parts. See [asset-based valuation](/glossary/asset-based-valuation/) and [liquidation value](/glossary/liquidation-value/) for the asset view.
- **They can be written off over time.** Accounting often spreads the cost of some intangibles over their useful life through [amortization](/glossary/amortization/). Ask an accountant how it applies.
- **They fall into the price split.** In an [asset sale](/glossary/asset-sale/), the contract divides the price across the assets, which can affect each side's tax.
