## How it works

The word has two uses, and it helps to know which one you are reading.

**1. Amortizing an intangible asset.** When a business pays for something it cannot touch, such as a purchased software license, a trademark or a domain name, accountants spread the cost over the years it is expected to be useful. Each year's share is the amortization expense. The simplest method is straight-line:

**Yearly amortization = cost ÷ years of useful life**

This is the non-physical twin of [depreciation](/glossary/depreciation/), which does the same for physical assets.

**2. Amortizing a loan.** A loan is amortized when it is repaid in regular installments that cover both interest and part of the amount borrowed. A loan schedule showing each payment is called an amortization schedule.

Accounting and tax rules decide which intangible assets can be amortized and over how many years, and the rules differ by country. Ask an accountant before you rely on a figure.

## Example

A business buys a software license for $6,000 and expects to use it for 3 years. The figures are made up.

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Amortization each year | $6,000 ÷ 3 | $2,000 |
| Remaining cost after year 1 | $6,000 − $2,000 | $4,000 |
| Remaining cost after year 3 | $6,000 − ($2,000 × 3) | $0 |

Reported profit is $2,000 lower in each of the three years, although the business paid the $6,000 up front.

## Why amortization matters in a valuation

Amortization lowers reported profit without a matching cash payment in that year, so buyers add it back when comparing businesses. [EBITDA](/glossary/ebitda/) stands for earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, and [SDE](/glossary/sde/) adds it back too. BizBuySell lists depreciation and amortization among the standard categories of [add-backs](/glossary/add-backs/) ([BizBuySell](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/article/add-backs-sde/)).

Two cautions. If a business keeps buying new licenses or tools, the spending is real even when the accounting spreads it out. And if a business was bought at a price above the value of its assets, the extra may be recorded as [goodwill](/glossary/goodwill/), which is treated differently. Check how the seller's accountant recorded it.
