## How it works

Expansion revenue is the increase in [MRR](/glossary/mrr/) from customers who were already paying at the start of a period. It comes from three places:

- **Upgrades** to a higher-priced plan.
- **More seats or usage** on the same plan.
- **Add-ons** sold on top of the base subscription.

Stripe defines MRR as "the total amount of money a company can expect to receive from its existing customers each month" ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/resources/more/essential-saas-metrics)). Expansion is the part of that figure that grows without signing anyone new.

**Expansion MRR = MRR added by existing customers during the period**

Revenue from customers who joined during the period is new revenue, not expansion.

## Example

A SaaS business starts the month with $30,000 of MRR. During the month:

- Existing customers upgrade plans and add seats worth $1,200.
- New customers add $2,000.
- Cancellations remove $900.

**Expansion MRR = $1,200.** The $2,000 is new-customer revenue and the $900 is churn.

Expansion as a share of starting MRR is $1,200 ÷ $30,000 = 4%.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Expansion offsets [churn](/glossary/churn/). If expansion is larger than the revenue lost to cancellations and downgrades, [net revenue retention](/glossary/net-revenue-retention/) rises above 100% and recurring revenue grows even with no new customers. In the example, expansion of $1,200 is more than the $900 lost, so existing customers add $300 net.

Buyers read that as revenue that is easier to keep and grow. VALUERAQ's [SaaS valuation tool](/tools/saas-valuation/) has no expansion input, but it adjusts the multiple for revenue growth and monthly churn, which expansion feeds into.

A word of care: expansion that comes from one large customer, or from a one-off price change, is less dependable than steady upgrades across many customers.

## Related terms

[NRR](/glossary/net-revenue-retention/) combines expansion, contraction and churn into one percentage. [ARR](/glossary/arr/) is MRR × 12. [Churn](/glossary/churn/) is the revenue lost to cancellations.
