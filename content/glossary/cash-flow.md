## How it works

**Cash flow = cash coming in − cash going out**, measured over a set period such as a month or a year. It counts money when it moves, not when a sale is agreed or a bill is dated.

That timing is the whole difference from [net profit](/glossary/net-profit/). Profit records a sale when it is made. Cash flow records it when the payment arrives. A business can show a profit and still run short of cash, or the reverse.

Cash flow is usually split into three parts: operating cash flow (the day-to-day business), investing cash flow (buying or selling equipment, software or other long-term items) and financing cash flow (loans, repayments and money paid out to owners). For a small online business, operating cash flow is the part buyers care about most.

## Example

An online store reports the following for a year.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Net profit | $40,000 |
| Extra inventory bought and not yet sold | −$15,000 |
| Customer payments still to arrive at year end | −$5,000 |
| Annual software plan paid in advance for next year | −$3,000 |
| **Operating cash flow** | **$17,000** |

Operating cash flow is $40,000 − $15,000 − $5,000 − $3,000 = $17,000. The business was profitable, yet only $17,000 of cash was generated, because money went into stock and was still owed by customers.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Small digital businesses are usually priced on profit measures such as [SDE](/glossary/sde/) and then multiplied. Cash flow is how a buyer checks that the profit is real:

- **Does the profit turn into cash?** Compare the profit figure with what actually reached the bank account. A large, repeated gap needs an explanation.
- **Is the business funding itself?** A store that must keep buying stock, or a service with slow-paying clients, needs spare cash to grow.
- **Is it steady?** Look at cash flow month by month across the [trailing twelve months](/glossary/trailing-twelve-months/), not one good month.

Larger deals sometimes use a [discounted cash flow](/glossary/discounted-cash-flow/) model, which values expected future cash flows. For most small online businesses a multiple of profit is the norm, with cash flow as the cross-check.

## Cash flow vs profit vs SDE

| Measure | What it counts | Timing | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash flow | Money that actually moved in and out | When payments happen | Checking the business funds itself |
| Net profit | Revenue less costs | When sales and bills are recorded | Showing results for a period |
| [SDE](/glossary/sde/) | Profit with the owner's pay and one-offs added back | Same as profit | Pricing the business |

## Why buyers ask for bank statements

Profit is an accounting figure and can be adjusted. Bank statements are harder to change, so buyers use them to confirm that reported sales arrived as cash. If monthly profit and monthly deposits do not line up, the seller should be able to explain why: stock bought, late customer payments or annual bills paid in advance.

## Common questions

**Can a profitable business have negative cash flow?** Yes, if it spends more cash than it receives in a period, for example while buying stock for a busy season.

**Is cash flow the same as revenue?** No. Revenue is sales; cash flow is what is left after cash costs and timing differences.

**Which should a seller show?** Both. Show profit for the price and cash flow to prove it is real.
