## How it works

Variable costs are the costs that rise and fall with each sale: the product itself, shipping, payment fees and often per-order advertising. Fixed costs stay the same whatever you sell, such as software subscriptions, a contractor's retainer or a warehouse lease.

**Contribution margin per unit = price − variable costs per unit**

**Contribution margin ratio = contribution margin ÷ price × 100**

It differs from [gross margin](/glossary/gross-margin/), which subtracts only the direct costs of making the product. Contribution margin also takes out other costs that move with each order, so it is usually the lower figure.

## Example

A store sells a product for $40. Invented figures per unit:

- Product cost: $14.00
- Shipping and packing: $5.00
- Payment fee, 3% of $40: $1.20

Variable costs = $14.00 + $5.00 + $1.20 = $20.20.

**Contribution margin = $40.00 − $20.20 = $19.80 per unit, or 49.5% of the price.**

The store has fixed costs of $3,000 a month. Break-even units = $3,000 ÷ $19.80 = 151.5, so it must sell 152 units a month before it earns a profit. Each unit after that adds $19.80 to monthly profit.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Contribution margin shows how much of each extra sale reaches profit, which tells a buyer how sensitive earnings are to a drop in sales. A business with a high ratio gains quickly when sales rise and loses quickly when they fall. One with a thin ratio needs steady volume just to cover its fixed costs.
