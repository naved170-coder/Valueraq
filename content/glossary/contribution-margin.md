## How it works

Variable costs are the costs that rise and fall with each sale: the product itself, shipping, payment fees and often per-order advertising. Fixed costs stay the same whatever you sell, such as software subscriptions, a contractor's retainer or a warehouse lease.

**Contribution margin per unit = price − variable costs per unit**

**Contribution margin ratio = contribution margin ÷ price × 100**

It differs from [gross margin](/glossary/gross-margin/), which subtracts only the direct costs of making the product. Contribution margin also takes out other costs that move with each order, so it is usually the lower figure.

## Example

A store sells a product for $40. Invented figures per unit:

- Product cost: $14.00
- Shipping and packing: $5.00
- Payment fee, 3% of $40: $1.20

Variable costs = $14.00 + $5.00 + $1.20 = $20.20.

**Contribution margin = $40.00 − $20.20 = $19.80 per unit, or 49.5% of the price.**

The store has fixed costs of $3,000 a month. Break-even units = $3,000 ÷ $19.80 = 151.5, so it must sell 152 units a month before it earns a profit. Each unit after that adds $19.80 to monthly profit.

## Contribution margin after ad spend

Many stores pay for advertising on each order. Buyers often look at contribution margin after that cost, because it shows what a sale really adds. Using the same $40 product, add $8.00 of advertising per order:

| Line | Per unit | Share of price |
|---|---|---|
| Price | $40.00 | 100% |
| Product cost | −$14.00 | |
| Gross profit | $26.00 | 65.0% (gross margin) |
| Shipping and packing | −$5.00 | |
| Payment fee | −$1.20 | |
| Contribution margin before ads | $19.80 | 49.5% |
| Advertising per order | −$8.00 | |
| **Contribution margin after ads** | **$11.80** | **29.5%** |

With $3,000 of fixed costs a month, break-even is $3,000 ÷ $11.80 = 254.2, so 255 units. That is much higher than the 152 units needed before ad spend, which is why buyers ask which version a seller is quoting.

## Contribution margin or gross margin?

| | Gross margin | Contribution margin |
|---|---|---|
| Costs removed | Direct product costs | All costs that move with each order |
| Includes shipping, fees, ads? | Usually not | Yes, if you count them as variable |
| Best for | Pricing and product cost | Break-even and growth decisions |

## Why it matters in a valuation

Contribution margin shows how much of each extra sale reaches profit, which tells a buyer how sensitive earnings are to a drop in sales. A business with a high ratio gains quickly when sales rise and loses quickly when they fall. One with a thin ratio needs steady volume just to cover its fixed costs.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a higher contribution margin always better?

A higher margin means more of each sale reaches fixed costs and profit. But a high margin on very few orders can still lose money, so look at volume too.

### Are ads a variable or a fixed cost?

It depends on how you spend. Spend that scales with each order is treated as variable. A fixed monthly budget that does not move with sales is closer to a fixed cost. Whichever you choose, tell a buyer.

### Can contribution margin be negative?

Yes. If variable costs per order exceed the price, every sale loses money. Fix pricing or costs before a sale.
