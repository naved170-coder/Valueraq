## Formula

**ARR = MRR × 12**

For businesses that sell annual contracts, ARR can also be calculated as the sum of the annualised value of all active contracts. Both approaches should give the same answer if MRR is calculated correctly. To turn ARR into a price, use the [ARR valuation calculator](/calculators/saas-valuation-calculator/).

## What to include and exclude

Include recurring subscription fees from active customers, including annual plans spread evenly across their months.

Exclude one-off setup or onboarding fees, consulting and custom development, usage charges that don't recur predictably, and revenue from customers who have already cancelled.

## Example

A SaaS business has 400 customers on a $50 monthly plan and 20 customers on a $1,200 annual plan.

- Monthly plans: 400 × $50 = $20,000 MRR
- Annual plans: 20 × $1,200 ÷ 12 = $2,000 MRR
- Total MRR: $22,000
- **ARR: $22,000 × 12 = $264,000**

## Why ARR matters for valuation

SaaS businesses are often valued as a multiple of ARR, because subscription revenue tends to repeat. A higher [ARR multiple](/guides/saas-valuation-multiples/) is justified when growth is strong, [churn](/glossary/churn/) is low and [gross margin](/glossary/gross-margin/) is high. {{BRAND}}'s [SaaS Valuation Tool](/tools/saas-valuation/) starts from ARR and adjusts the multiple for those factors.

## What counts as ARR: a quick table

| Item | Counts as ARR? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly subscription fees | Yes | Recurring and predictable |
| Annual plan, paid up front | Yes, spread over 12 months | Count the yearly value, not the cash received |
| Setup, onboarding or consulting fees | No | One-off |
| Usage charges that vary | Only a steady, predictable part | Otherwise unreliable |
| Discounts that apply to the plan | Count the discounted price | It is what the customer pays |
| Customers who have cancelled | No | Revenue has stopped |

## A second example: discounts and one-off fees

A business bills 100 customers $40 a month, but 20 of them get a 25% discount. It also earned $3,000 in setup fees this month.

- Full price: 80 × $40 = $3,200
- Discounted: 20 × $30 = $600
- MRR = $3,800. The $3,000 of setup fees is left out.
- **ARR = $3,800 × 12 = $45,600**

Counting the setup fees would overstate ARR by $36,000.

## ARR, MRR and run rate

[MRR](/glossary/mrr/) is the monthly figure. ARR is MRR × 12. A run rate is wider: Corporate Finance Institute describes it as taking "a company's current revenue in a certain period (a week, month, quarter, etc.) and converts it to an annual figure", and warns about seasonality and one-off events. ARR is a cleaner version of that idea, limited to recurring subscriptions.

## From ARR to a price

{{BRAND}}'s default range for SaaS is 2.0x to 5.0x ARR, with a midpoint of 3.5x, before adjustments for growth, churn, margin and other risks (see the [methodology](/methodology/)). On the $264,000 ARR in the example above, the unadjusted range is $528,000 to $1,320,000, with a midpoint of $924,000. These are default assumptions, not market data, and a real offer reflects the adjustments.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is ARR the same as annual revenue?

No. ARR is the yearly value of subscriptions active today. Annual revenue is what was earned over the past year. A growing business has ARR above last year's revenue.

### Can a small business use ARR?

Yes, if it has subscriptions. ARR is most useful for software and memberships. Businesses that sell one-off products are better described by profit.

### Should I include annual prepayments?

Yes, spread over 12 months. Counting the whole payment in one month would inflate that month's MRR.

### Does ARR include customers who gave notice to cancel?

Check the sales agreement. Many buyers remove customers who have already given notice, since the revenue is ending.

## ARR vs revenue

ARR is a snapshot of the current subscription base projected over a year. Annual revenue is what was actually earned over the past year. A fast-growing business will have ARR above last year's revenue; a shrinking one will have ARR below it. Buyers look at both.
