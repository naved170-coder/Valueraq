## Formula

A common simple formula for subscription businesses:

**LTV = (average revenue per customer per month × gross margin) ÷ monthly churn**

Dividing by monthly churn is the same as multiplying by the average customer lifetime in months (1 ÷ churn).

## Example

A SaaS product charges an average of $40 a month, has an 80% [gross margin](/glossary/gross-margin/) and 4% monthly [churn](/glossary/churn/).

- Monthly gross profit per customer: $40 × 0.80 = $32
- Average lifetime: 1 ÷ 0.04 = 25 months
- **LTV = $32 × 25 = $800**

## LTV:CAC ratio

Comparing LTV with [CAC](/glossary/cac/) shows whether acquiring customers is profitable. With an LTV of $800 and a CAC of $200, the ratio is 4:1: each customer returns four times what it cost to acquire. A ratio near or below 1:1 means the business loses money on growth.

## Limitations

- The simple formula assumes churn is constant. In reality, churn is often high in the first months and lower afterwards.
- It ignores expansion revenue from upgrades.
- Small changes in churn change LTV a lot: at 2% churn the example above gives $1,600, double the result at 4%.

Buyers often prefer actual cohort data, showing what customers who joined in a given month have really paid, over a formula.
