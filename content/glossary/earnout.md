## How it works

The buyer and seller agree a fixed amount paid at closing and a further amount that depends on results. The contract sets out:

- **The measure**: usually revenue, profit or a count such as paying subscribers.
- **The target and the period**: for example, revenue over the 12 months after closing.
- **The payment**: all or nothing, or a sliding scale, often with a maximum.
- **Who measures it**, from which records, and what happens if the two sides disagree.

## Example

A buyer and seller disagree on what a newsletter is worth. The seller wants $300,000 and the buyer will commit to $240,000. They agree:

- $240,000 paid at closing.
- A further $60,000 if revenue in the 12 months after closing reaches $200,000.

**Maximum price = $240,000 + $60,000 = $300,000.** If revenue falls short, the seller keeps $240,000 and the earnout is not paid. Here the earnout is 20% of the maximum price ($60,000 ÷ $300,000).

## Why earnouts matter when buying or selling

An earnout bridges a gap in expectations. It is common when recent growth is strong but unproven, when revenue depends on a few customers, or when the seller's own involvement drives results.

For a **buyer**, it lowers the amount at risk on day one. For a **seller**, it is the least certain part of the price, because after closing the buyer controls the business. Changes to pricing, marketing spend or product direction can all affect whether the target is met.

Revenue targets are harder to dispute than profit targets, since profit depends on what costs the new owner chooses to charge to the business.

## Earnout vs seller financing

With [seller financing](/glossary/seller-financing/), the deferred amount is a debt owed whatever happens to the business. With an earnout, it is owed only if the target is met. Sellers should compare offers on the amount that is certain, not on the headline price.

## Take advice

Earnout wording and tax treatment vary by country and by deal. Have a lawyer draft or review the terms, and ask an accountant how the payments will be taxed, before you sign.
