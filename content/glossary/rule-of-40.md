## How it works

The Rule of 40 balances growth against profit in one number.

**Rule of 40 score = revenue growth rate (%) + profit margin (%)**

The Corporate Finance Institute gives the formula as "Revenue Growth Rate (%) + Profit Margin (%)" and says a score of 40% or higher is the target. EBITDA margin is the most common profit measure used, though you can use another as long as you use the same one each time. The idea is that a business can grow fast while losing money, or grow slowly while earning well, and both can score the same.

## Example

Two subscription businesses, shown as an illustration and not as market figures:

- **Business A** grew revenue 30% over the last 12 months and has a 15% profit margin. **30 + 15 = 45**, above the 40 target.
- **Business B** grew revenue 60% but lost 30% of revenue after costs. **60 + (−30) = 30**, below the target even though growth is faster.

Business A has the stronger score because its growth does not rely on losing money.

## Why it matters in a valuation

The rule is a quick way to judge whether growth is being bought at too high a cost. For a small business sold on a marketplace, it is a sanity check, not a pricing method.

The same source warns that the rule "oversimplifies a company's financial prospects by focusing on just two metrics" and "doesn't distinguish between different types of growth". It is described as a back-of-the-envelope calculation that suits mature companies better than startups. A fast-growing app with high [churn](/glossary/churn/) can pass the test and still be a poor buy.

{{BRAND}} does not use the Rule of 40 as an input. Its [SaaS valuation tool](/tools/saas-valuation/) multiplies [ARR](/glossary/arr/) by a default range of 2.0 to 5.0 times and adjusts it for churn, growth, margin, customer concentration, age and owner workload, as set out in the [methodology](/methodology/). Read the score next to those factors, not instead of them. The [SaaS valuation guide](/guides/saas-valuation/) shows how they work together.

## Related terms

[ARR](/glossary/arr/) is the annual value of active subscriptions. [EBITDA](/glossary/ebitda/) is a common profit measure for the margin part of the formula. [Net revenue retention](/glossary/net-revenue-retention/) shows whether existing customers grow or shrink.
