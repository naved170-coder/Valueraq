## How it works

When a buyer and seller agree on a price in principle, they often sign a [letter of intent](/glossary/letter-of-intent/). It usually includes an exclusivity period: a set number of days in which the seller stops talking to other buyers while the chosen buyer carries out [due diligence](/glossary/due-diligence/) and the two sides write the [purchase agreement](/glossary/purchase-agreement/).

The clause normally states:

- **The length**, often counted in weeks.
- **What the seller may not do**, such as soliciting or accepting other offers.
- **What happens at the end**: the period expires, or the buyer may ask to extend it.

## Example

A seller and buyer agree on $120,000 and sign a letter of intent with a 30-day exclusivity period. The buyer uses that time to review the seller's [data room](/glossary/data-room/). On day 22 the buyer finds that revenue is $6,000 lower than stated and asks for a lower price. The seller can accept, negotiate or walk away, but cannot start talks with another buyer until day 30 has passed, unless the buyer agrees.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Exclusivity protects the buyer's time and costs, so it supports a firmer offer. For the seller it has a price: while the clock runs, no competing offer can push the price up. Sellers should keep the period short, say what the buyer must deliver by when, and make sure the buyer's price can only change for reasons found in due diligence.

## What to negotiate

Sellers and buyers usually settle three points. **Length:** a short period keeps pressure on the buyer to finish. **Milestones:** the seller can ask the buyer to deliver a draft agreement or proof of funds by set dates, or the period ends. **Extension:** any extra time should need the seller's written agreement, not happen automatically. Also check whether the clause stops only the seller from taking offers, or also stops the buyer from walking away without notice.

## Related terms

[Letter of intent](/glossary/letter-of-intent/), [due diligence](/glossary/due-diligence/) and [data room](/glossary/data-room/).

## Take advice

Whether an exclusivity clause is binding, and for how long, depends on its wording and on local law. Have an attorney review the letter of intent before you sign.
