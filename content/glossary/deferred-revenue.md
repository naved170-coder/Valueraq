## How it works

When a customer pays for a year of service up front, the business has the cash today but has only earned a small part of it. The unearned part is deferred revenue. It is a liability on the [balance sheet](/glossary/balance-sheet/), because the business owes the customer the remaining service or a refund.

Each month, one slice moves from deferred revenue to earned revenue. The formula for a simple annual plan is:

**Monthly earned revenue = amount paid ÷ months of service.**

Deferred revenue is common in subscription businesses, memberships, courses and software sold on annual plans. It is a different thing from [MRR](/glossary/mrr/), which measures the monthly value of subscriptions, and from cash received.

## Example

A SaaS business sells an annual plan for $1,200, paid in full on January 1. The figures are made up to show the arithmetic.

| Month end | Revenue earned that month | Deferred revenue left |
|---|---|---|
| January | $100 | $1,100 |
| June | $100 | $600 |
| December | $100 | $0 |

Each month earns $1,200 ÷ 12 = $100. After six months, $600 has been earned and $600 is still owed in service. If the business has 50 annual customers who all paid in January, it holds $60,000 in cash on day one, but only $5,000 of it is earned in the first month.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Prepayments can make a business look healthier than it is. A seller who collected $60,000 in January has cash, but the buyer inherits the duty to serve those customers for the rest of the year without receiving that money again. Buyers often ask how deferred revenue is treated in the price, whether it is counted as a debt-like item or adjusted in [working capital](/glossary/working-capital/). Settle it in the written agreement rather than leave it assumed.

It also affects the numbers a buyer reads. Cash received in a month is not the same as revenue earned, so a seller should show both, as covered in [SaaS metrics buyers check](/guides/saas-metrics-buyers-check/). High annual prepayment can be a good sign, because customers who pay for a year are usually committed, but a buyer will still want to see [churn](/glossary/churn/) at renewal time.
