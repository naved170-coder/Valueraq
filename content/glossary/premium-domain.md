## How it works

"Premium" is used for two different things, and the difference decides what you pay each year:

| | Registry premium | Aftermarket |
|---|---|---|
| Who sets the price | The registry that runs the ending, such as .com or .shop | The current owner |
| Where you buy | A registrar's search results | A resale marketplace, a broker or directly from the owner |
| Renewal | Often higher than a standard name, every year | Usually a standard renewal once it is yours |
| What to confirm | The renewal price, before you pay | Who the real owner is, and the name's history |

Always ask for the renewal price in writing. A low first-year price can hide a high yearly fee.

## Example

All figures are invented to show the arithmetic.

You compare two names for a new site:

- Name A, registry premium: $1,200 now, renewed at $300 a year.
- Name B, aftermarket: $2,500 now, renewed at $20 a year.

Cost over five years, counting the purchase and four renewals:

- Name A: $1,200 + 4 × $300 = **$2,400**
- Name B: $2,500 + 4 × $20 = **$2,580**

Name B costs more on day one and almost the same by year five, and the gap shrinks every year after. A cheap-looking price tells you little until the renewal is counted.

## Why it matters in a valuation

A domain is often part of what a buyer pays for in a website sale, so it matters whether the name is a one-off cost or a recurring one. To judge a price, see [domain appraisal explained](/guides/domain-appraisal-explained/). Before paying, check:

1. The name's history, such as past use and any trademark conflict.
2. That the seller controls the name and its registrar account.
3. The renewal price, for registry premium names.
4. That payment goes through [escrow](/glossary/escrow/), released when the transfer is complete.

The seller's number is an [asking price](/glossary/asking-price/), so negotiating is normal.
