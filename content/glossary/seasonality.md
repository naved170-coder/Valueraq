## How it works

Demand for many products is not the same every month. A gift store sells more before year-end holidays. A website about garden care peaks in spring. A tool for students is busiest when school starts. These swings repeat from year to year, which is what separates seasonality from a one-off spike or a long-term trend.

Seasonality affects more than sales. Stores buy stock before the busy period, so cash goes out months before it comes back in. Advertising costs and returns can swing as well. See [cash flow](/glossary/cash-flow/) and [inventory turnover](/glossary/inventory-turnover/).

## Example

A hypothetical online store earns $4,000 of net profit in each of the nine quiet months and $9,000 in each of the three peak months.

**Annual profit = (9 × $4,000) + (3 × $9,000) = $36,000 + $27,000 = $63,000.**

Now suppose a seller shows only the latest peak month and multiplies it by twelve.

**Peak-month run rate = $9,000 × 12 = $108,000.**

That is $45,000 above the real annual figure. A buyer who paid a multiple of $108,000 would pay for profit the store does not earn. The figures are an illustration, not market data.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Buyers value a business on profit that is likely to repeat, so they look at a full cycle. The usual tool is [trailing twelve months](/glossary/trailing-twelve-months/), which adds up the last twelve months of real results. The Corporate Finance Institute lists seasonality among the risks of the [run-rate](/glossary/run-rate/) method, because a single period projected forward assumes conditions stay unchanged.

Three habits keep seasonal figures honest:

- **Compare like with like.** Match each month with the same month a year earlier, not with the month before. {{BRAND}}'s growth input asks for the latest three months against the same three months a year earlier for this reason.
- **Check which months the sale spans.** A store listed after its peak has a strong trailing figure that may not last. A store listed before its peak may look weaker than it is.
- **Plan the cash.** If you buy a seasonal store, make sure you can fund stock for the next peak.

Ask for monthly records for at least a year, and for two years if you can get them. See [how to buy an ecommerce business](/guides/how-to-buy-an-ecommerce-business/) for the full checks, and [valuation mistakes](/guides/valuation-mistakes/) for common errors.
