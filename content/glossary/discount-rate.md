## How it works

Money received later is worth less than money received now. The discount rate says how much less. A buyer who needs a 25% yearly return divides each future year's cash by 1.25 for one year away, 1.25² for two years, and so on:

**Value today = cash flow ÷ (1 + discount rate) ^ years away**

The rate has two parts, in plain terms: what the buyer could earn elsewhere with little risk, plus extra for the risk of this business. The Corporate Finance Institute notes that in a DCF the discount rate is "typically the weighted average cost of capital." Small business buyers usually do not calculate that and instead choose a rate that reflects how risky the business feels.

## Example

A business is expected to earn $50,000 in cash one year from now. The figures are made up.

| Discount rate | Calculation | Value today |
|---|---|---|
| 15% | $50,000 ÷ 1.15 | $43,478 |
| 25% | $50,000 ÷ 1.25 | $40,000 |
| 35% | $50,000 ÷ 1.35 | $37,037 |

The same $50,000 is worth about $6,400 less at 35% than at 15%. A higher rate means a lower value. Over several years the gap grows, because the rate compounds.

## Why it matters in a valuation

The rate is the weakest part of a DCF. The cash forecast is a guess, and the rate is another opinion on top of it. That is why a seller and a buyer can look at the same business and disagree by a wide margin: they chose different rates.

Risks that push a buyer's rate up include [key person risk](/glossary/key-person-risk/), one channel supplying most of the traffic, [customer concentration](/glossary/customer-concentration/), and short or uneven records.

There is a link to multiples too. A [profit multiple](/glossary/profit-multiple/) of 3 means the buyer expects to earn the price back in about three years if profit holds. A buyer who wants a faster payback pays a lower multiple, which is the same as using a higher discount rate. See also [capitalization rate](/glossary/capitalization-rate/), the flat-income version of the same idea. {{BRAND}} uses published default multiple ranges instead of a discount rate, as the [methodology](/methodology/) explains.
