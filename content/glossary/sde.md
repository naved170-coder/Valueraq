## Formula

**SDE = net profit + owner's salary and benefits + genuine one-off or personal expenses + interest + depreciation and amortization**

In practice, for most small online businesses, SDE is simply revenue minus the real costs of running the business, before the owner pays themselves.

## Acceptable add-backs

Add-backs are expenses added back to profit because a buyer won't have them. Buyers accept add-backs that are documented and clearly non-recurring or personal:

- The owner's own salary or drawings.
- A one-off redesign, migration or legal cost.
- Personal expenses run through the business, such as a personal phone plan.

Buyers reject add-backs for costs the business needs to keep earning, such as content, advertising, contractors or software, however "discretionary" they seem.

## Example

A website business has $120,000 revenue and $70,000 of expenses, giving $50,000 net profit. Expenses include $24,000 paid to the owner and a one-off $3,000 redesign.

**SDE = $50,000 + $24,000 + $3,000 = $77,000.**

## Why SDE matters for valuation

Most small websites, newsletters, apps and stores are valued as a multiple of annual SDE. {{BRAND}}'s profit-based tools ask for monthly net profit before your own pay, which is monthly SDE. See [profit multiple](/glossary/profit-multiple/).

## SDE vs EBITDA

SDE includes the owner's pay; [EBITDA](/glossary/ebitda/) deducts a market-rate salary for everyone. For the same business, SDE is higher, and SDE multiples are usually lower than EBITDA multiples.
