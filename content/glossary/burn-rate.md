## How it works

There are two versions of the figure.

- **Gross burn** is everything the business spends in a month: salaries and contractors, software, hosting, advertising and other costs.
- **Net burn** is gross burn minus the cash that comes in that month. This is the number most people mean by "burn rate".

**Net burn = monthly cash spent − monthly cash received.** If a business earns more than it spends, it has no burn; it is generating cash instead.

Dividing the cash in the bank by the net burn gives the **runway**: how many months the business can last at the current pace.

## Example

A small software business has $120,000 in the bank. Each month it spends $30,000 and collects $18,000 in subscription payments.

- **Gross burn:** $30,000 a month.
- **Net burn:** $30,000 − $18,000 = $12,000 a month.
- **Runway:** $120,000 ÷ $12,000 = 10 months.

If the business cuts $4,000 of costs, net burn falls to $8,000 and runway rises to $120,000 ÷ $8,000 = 15 months.

### How runway changes with burn

Using the same $120,000 in the bank:

| Net burn per month | Runway |
|---|---|
| $12,000 | 10 months |
| $8,000 | 15 months |
| $6,000 | 20 months |
| $4,000 | 30 months |

Halving the burn doubles the runway, so small cost cuts can change a buyer's view of the risk.

### If you are buying or selling a loss-making SaaS

Buyers ask three questions: how much cash is needed to reach break-even, how fast revenue is growing, and which costs can be cut without losing customers. A seller can help by showing monthly net burn for the last 12 months, the cash balance and a plan with dated cuts. A buyer should rebuild burn from bank records rather than accept a summary.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Small online businesses sold to individual buyers are usually valued on profit, so a business that burns cash is hard to price that way. VALUERAQ's [SaaS tool](/tools/saas-valuation/) can still estimate a range from annual recurring revenue, but it flags loss-making businesses, because buyers of those often weigh how long the money lasts and how fast revenue is growing.

A buyer looking at a loss-making business will ask how much cash is needed to reach break-even and whether the seller's costs, such as paid advertising, can be cut without losing customers. A lower burn and a longer runway make the business easier to sell.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is burn rate the same as being unprofitable?

Mostly, but it is a cash figure. A business can lose money on paper while collecting cash up front, or spend cash on something that is not a cost, so check both.

### What is a good burn rate?

There is no single number. It depends on the cash in the bank and on how quickly revenue is growing. Runway is the more useful figure.

### Do small online businesses have a burn rate?

Many are profitable and have none. A business that spends more than it earns has one, and buyers will want the plan to fix it. To price a SaaS business, try the [SaaS Valuation Tool](/tools/saas-valuation/).
