## How it works

The U.S. Small Business Administration lists three approaches to valuing a business. One is the assets approach, which "subtracts total business liabilities from the total value of all assets."

**Value = total assets − total liabilities**

Each asset can be counted at its [book value](/glossary/book-value/), the figure in the accounts, or at its [fair market value](/glossary/fair-market-value/), what it would sell for today. Intangible assets, such as the brand, customer lists and intellectual property, can be counted too if they can be valued. The other two approaches the SBA names are the income approach, built on projected earnings (see [discounted cash flow](/glossary/discounted-cash-flow/)), and the market approach, which compares recent sales of similar businesses.

## Example

A small store owns inventory, equipment and a domain name, and owes a supplier and a loan. The figures are made up.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Inventory at current resale value | $30,000 |
| Equipment | $8,000 |
| Domain name and brand assets | $12,000 |
| Cash | $5,000 |
| **Total assets** | **$55,000** |
| Supplier balance owed | −$9,000 |
| Loan | −$16,000 |
| **Total liabilities** | **−$25,000** |
| **Asset-based value** | **$30,000** |

If the same store earns $24,000 a year in profit, a buyer using a multiple of 3 would offer $72,000. The gap is the part of the price that pays for the earning power the assets create, which accountants call [goodwill](/glossary/goodwill/).

## Why it matters in a valuation

Online businesses usually own few physical assets, so counting assets undervalues them. A website's worth lies in its traffic, audience and profit, not in its servers. That is why most are priced on earnings with a [valuation multiple](/glossary/valuation-multiple/).

Asset-based valuation is still useful in three cases: a business with little or no profit, where earnings give no useful number; a business being closed, where assets are sold one by one; and an [asset sale](/glossary/asset-sale/), where the buyer takes chosen assets rather than the company. It also gives a floor against which to test an earnings-based price. {{BRAND}}'s tools do not estimate asset value, and say so on the [methodology](/methodology/) page for loss-making businesses.
