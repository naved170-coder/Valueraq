## How it works

The sale agreement sets out how long the seller will stay available after closing, how many hours that covers, and what help is included. Handing over the assets is one task. Handing over the know-how is another, and the transition period is for the second.

Typical items written into the agreement:

- **Length and hours:** for example, a set number of weeks with a cap on weekly hours.
- **Scope:** walking through daily tasks, supplier and customer introductions, how content or code is published, and which tools run what.
- **Contact method:** a named email address or chat channel, and how fast the seller replies.
- **Cost:** included in the price, or paid at an agreed hourly rate once the included time is used.
- **What happens at the end:** after the period, questions may be answered at an agreed rate or not at all.

## Example

A seller sells a newsletter business for $90,000 and agrees to 6 weeks of support, capped at 5 hours a week.

**Included support = 6 weeks x 5 hours = 30 hours.** If the buyer needs more, the agreement says whether extra hours are paid at an agreed rate, for instance $50 an hour. Ten extra hours would then cost 10 x $50 = $500.

The figures are an illustration, not a norm.

## Why it matters in a valuation

A business that depends on its owner is harder to hand over, and buyers know it. {{BRAND}}'s default rules lower the multiple for high owner involvement and raise it slightly for low owner involvement (see the [methodology](/methodology/)). A clear transition period does not change that rule, but it reduces the buyer's practical risk and gives the seller a reason to document routines before listing.

For a seller, agree the hours and the end date in writing so support does not drift on without limit. For a buyer, ask for the transition period to cover the tasks you are least sure about, and ask the seller to write them down early. Where part of the price depends on later results, such as an [earnout](/glossary/earnout/), take extra care that the seller's help and the buyer's control are both clearly described.

Terms and tax treatment vary by country, so have a lawyer review the agreement.
