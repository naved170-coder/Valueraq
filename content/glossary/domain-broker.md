## How it works

A broker works for one side. A **buy-side** broker contacts the owner of a name you want, often without revealing who you are, and negotiates. A **sell-side** broker markets a name you own and looks for buyers. Payment is usually a commission on the final price, sometimes with a minimum fee. Terms vary, so get them in writing.

A broker may help when the owner is hard to find, when you want to stay anonymous so the price does not rise, or when you lack time to negotiate. Buying directly may be enough when the owner is easy to reach and the name is not costly.

## Example

All figures are invented. The 10% rate is an assumption for the arithmetic, not a typical or quoted rate.

| Step | Figure |
|---|---|
| Agreed price for the name | $20,000 |
| Broker commission, assumed at 10% | $20,000 × 10% = $2,000 |
| Your total cost, if you pay the fee | $22,000 |

The broker earns the fee only if a deal closes, if the agreement says so. Ask who pays it, and confirm that in writing.

## Why it matters in a valuation

A broker's fee is a real cost on top of the price. Count it when you work out whether a [premium domain](/glossary/premium-domain/) is worth buying, and compare the total with what the name could earn or what a buyer might later pay. See [domain appraisal explained](/guides/domain-appraisal-explained/).

Questions to ask before hiring one:

1. Do you work for the buyer or the seller, and can you act for both?
2. How is your fee calculated, and who pays it?
3. Is there a minimum fee, or a retainer paid before a deal closes?
4. How long does the agreement last, and can I end it?
5. Will payment go through [escrow](/glossary/escrow/)?
6. Can you show past deals I can check?
