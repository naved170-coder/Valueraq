## Common traffic metrics

| Metric | What it measures |
|---|---|
| Sessions (visits) | Separate visits to the site |
| Users | Distinct visitors, estimated by analytics tools |
| Pageviews | Pages loaded, including repeat views |
| Traffic by channel | Where visits come from |

Different analytics tools count these differently, so compare like with like and use the same tool over time.

## Traffic channels

- **Organic search:** unpaid visits from search engines.
- **Direct:** visitors typing the address or using bookmarks (also a catch-all for untracked visits).
- **Referral:** links from other websites.
- **Social:** visits from social networks.
- **Email:** clicks from newsletters and email campaigns.
- **Paid:** ads.
- **AI assistants and answer engines:** a growing but still uneven source of referrals.

## How traffic affects value

Buyers pay for profit, and traffic matters because it produces profit and signals risk. What they look at:

- **Concentration.** A site with most of its visits from one channel, often organic search, can lose much of its revenue after one algorithm change. {{BRAND}}'s [Website Valuation Tool](/tools/website-valuation/) lowers the multiple when more than 60% of traffic comes from one channel.
- **Trend.** Rising or stable traffic supports value; a recent decline lowers it, even if the 12-month average looks fine.
- **Quality.** Traffic that converts into revenue is worth more than large volumes of visitors who don't. See [conversion rate](/glossary/conversion-rate/).

## Example

Two sites each receive 100,000 monthly visits. Site A gets 90% from organic search; Site B gets 50% from search, 30% from email and 20% from direct visits. With the same profit, Site B would usually command the higher multiple, because a search-engine change would hit it less.
