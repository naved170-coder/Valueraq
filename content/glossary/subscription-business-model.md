## How it works

Customers pay on a schedule, usually every month or every year, and keep access for as long as they keep paying. Software ([SaaS](/glossary/saas/)), memberships, paid newsletters and content libraries commonly work this way.

The model changes what a business needs to watch. A one-off seller must find a new buyer for each sale. A subscription business earns from the same customer again and again, so it watches two things above all: how many new subscribers join, and how many leave.

Stripe's guide to SaaS metrics defines two of these. Monthly recurring revenue (MRR) is "the total amount of money a company can expect to receive from its existing customers each month", and churn is "the percentage of total customers who discontinue using a product or service during a given period".

## Example

A hypothetical paid newsletter starts the month with 400 subscribers paying $10 a month.

**MRR = 400 × $10 = $4,000. [ARR](/glossary/arr/) = $4,000 × 12 = $48,000.**

During the month, 20 subscribers cancel and 30 join.

- Churn = 20 ÷ 400 = 5%.
- New subscribers: 400 − 20 + 30 = 410.
- New MRR = 410 × $10 = $4,100.

A 5% monthly churn means that, without new sign-ups, about 5% of the base would be gone each month. The figures are an illustration, not market data.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Buyers like [recurring revenue](/glossary/recurring-revenue/) because it is easier to forecast than one-off sales. That is why VALUERAQ's SaaS and AI tools value a business on annual recurring revenue instead of profit alone (see the [methodology](/methodology/)). The advantage depends on customers staying. A business with high churn has to replace its customers constantly, so its revenue is less secure than it looks.

When checking a subscription business, buyers look at:

- **Churn and retention:** how many customers stay, month by month. See [churn](/glossary/churn/).
- **MRR trend:** whether recurring revenue is growing, flat or shrinking. See [MRR](/glossary/mrr/).
- **Plan mix:** whether revenue is monthly (easier to cancel) or annual.
- **Customer concentration:** whether a few customers pay most of the revenue.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a subscription the same as recurring revenue?

Close, but not identical. Subscription revenue is a common source of recurring revenue. Recurring revenue is the wider idea of income that repeats, whether or not it comes from a formal subscription.

### Why do buyers prefer subscription businesses?

Repeating payments make future income easier to predict. The advantage holds only while customers keep paying, so buyers look at churn closely.
