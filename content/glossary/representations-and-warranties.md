## How it works

In the [purchase agreement](/glossary/purchase-agreement/), the seller states facts about the business and promises they are true. Typical statements for an online business cover:

- **Ownership:** the seller owns the domain, accounts, code and content being sold.
- **Financials:** the figures given to the buyer are accurate and match the records.
- **Debts and claims:** there are no unpaid liabilities, disputes or legal claims, except those listed.
- **Compliance:** the business has not broken platform rules or relevant laws, as far as the seller knows.

If a statement turns out to be false, the buyer may have a claim against the seller. The agreement says how long the statements apply and how a claim is made.

## Example

A seller states that the store earned $60,000 of profit in the last 12 months. After closing, the buyer finds that $9,000 of that came from a one-off refund the seller had not disclosed. Because the figure was a warranty, the buyer may ask for compensation under the contract. At a multiple of 3.0 the difference in value is $27,000 ($9,000 × 3.0), which shows why buyers want these promises in writing.

## Why it matters in a valuation

A buyer prices a business on what the seller says it is. Representations and warranties turn those statements into promises with consequences, and they are often backed by an [escrow](/glossary/escrow/) or [holdback](/glossary/holdback/). For sellers, the safest approach is to disclose problems up front, so that a statement is true and any known issue is listed as an exception.

## Disclosure schedules

Sellers usually attach a list of exceptions to the statements, called a disclosure schedule. If a known issue is on the list, the matching statement is not breached. For example, a seller who says "there are no open disputes" should list the one chargeback case still being decided. Keeping a note of every issue found while preparing the [data room](/glossary/data-room/) makes this list easy to write, and it protects the seller as much as the buyer.
