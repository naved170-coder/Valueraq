## How it works

The ratio puts two numbers side by side. [LTV](/glossary/ltv/) is the gross profit an average customer is expected to bring in before they leave. [CAC](/glossary/cac/) is what the business spent on sales and marketing to win that customer.

**LTV to CAC ratio = LTV ÷ CAC**

A ratio of 4 (written 4:1) means each customer is expected to return four dollars of gross profit for every dollar spent winning them. A ratio of 1 means the customer only repays their own acquisition cost, before any other expense is paid.

## Example

A subscription app charges $40 a month, has an 80% [gross margin](/glossary/gross-margin/) and loses 4% of customers each month.

- Monthly gross profit per customer: $40 × 0.80 = $32
- Average lifetime: 1 ÷ 0.04 = 25 months
- **LTV = $32 × 25 = $800**

The business spends $7,500 a month on ads and tools and gains 300 new customers, so **CAC = $7,500 ÷ 300 = $25**.

**LTV to CAC = $800 ÷ $25 = 32.** That is unusually high, and a careful buyer would ask whether the ad spend is complete and whether 4% churn will hold.

Now suppose the same business counts founder time and a contractor, and its true CAC is $200. The ratio becomes $800 ÷ $200 = **4**. Same business, very different story, which is why the definition of CAC matters.

### How the ratio and payback read together

Using an LTV of $800 and a monthly gross profit of $32 per customer:

| CAC | LTV to CAC | Months to earn back CAC |
|---|---|---|
| $800 | 1.0 | 25 |
| $400 | 2.0 | 12.5 |
| $200 | 4.0 | 6.3 |
| $100 | 8.0 | 3.1 |

Months to earn back CAC is CAC ÷ monthly gross profit per customer. A high ratio with a long wait can still strain cash, so read both.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Buyers use the ratio to judge whether growth creates value or just spends money. A low ratio means each new customer costs about what they return, so the business only grows by spending more. A high ratio is attractive but invites questions about whether the inputs are complete.

The ratio is only as reliable as its two inputs. LTV depends on [churn](/glossary/churn/), and small changes in churn move it a lot. CAC depends on which costs are counted. Ask for the calculation, the period it covers and cohort data showing what customers have actually paid. There is no single right number: it depends on the business model, the margin and how fast customers leave.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a higher LTV to CAC ratio always better?

Not always. A very high ratio can mean the business is spending too little to grow, or that the figures leave out costs. Buyers look at the ratio alongside payback time and churn.

### Should LTV use revenue or gross profit?

Gross profit. Revenue ignores the cost of serving the customer, so it overstates what each customer is worth.
