## How it works

On a profit and loss statement, costs fall into two groups. The first is the direct cost of making or delivering the product, called [cost of goods sold](/glossary/cost-of-goods-sold/). Subtracting it from revenue gives [gross profit](/glossary/gross-profit/). The second group is operating expenses, the costs of keeping the business running that do not change much with each sale.

For an online business, typical operating expenses include:

- Software and hosting that is not part of delivering a product
- Advertising and marketing
- Contractors, freelancers and any staff pay
- Accounting, legal and insurance
- Payment and platform fees not counted as direct costs

Where a cost sits depends on the choices a business makes, so check how each line is labelled before comparing two businesses. The layout is covered in [how to read a profit and loss statement](/guides/how-to-read-a-profit-and-loss-statement/).

## Example

A hypothetical online store reports these figures for a year:

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Revenue | $200,000 |
| Cost of goods sold | $110,000 |
| **Gross profit** | **$90,000** |
| Operating expenses | $62,000 |
| **Profit before the owner's pay** | **$28,000** |

Gross profit = $200,000 − $110,000 = $90,000. Profit = $90,000 − $62,000 = $28,000. Operating expenses are 62,000 ÷ 200,000 = 31% of revenue.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Small online businesses are usually priced as a multiple of profit, so every dollar of operating expense that a buyer accepts reduces the price. A multiple of 3 turns each extra $1,000 of yearly expense into $3,000 less value.

Buyers therefore read this section of the accounts closely.

- **Personal costs.** A seller may run personal items through the business. Removing them is an [add-back](/glossary/add-backs/), and BizBuySell warns that "some add-backs can unrealistically overvalue the company", so each one should be backed by a record.
- **Missing costs.** A business run by its owner may show no wage for the work. [SDE](/glossary/sde/) leaves the owner's pay out on purpose, but a buyer who plans to hire help must put that cost back in.
- **Cuts that cannot last.** If advertising was pushed down just before a sale, profit looks higher than it will be.

Compare operating expenses with revenue over twelve months, not one, and ask what changed if the ratio moved. A change in [net profit](/glossary/net-profit/) is easier to trust when you can see which expense lines caused it.
