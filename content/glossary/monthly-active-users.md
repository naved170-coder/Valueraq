## How it works

MAU counts people, not visits. Someone who opens an app 40 times in a month adds one to MAU, the same as someone who opens it once. The count only means something once the business says what "active" is. Common choices are any app open, a logged-in session, or a specific action such as sending a message or finishing a lesson.

**MAU = distinct users who met the activity rule at least once in the month**

Two related counts are used alongside it. Daily active users (DAU) counts distinct users in a single day. The ratio **DAU ÷ MAU** is often called stickiness: it shows roughly what share of the month's users show up on a typical day.

## Example

An app has 12,000 distinct users who opened it at least once in September. On an average day, 1,800 distinct users open it.

- MAU: **12,000**
- Average DAU: 1,800
- Stickiness: 1,800 ÷ 12,000 = **15%**

If 3,000 of those 12,000 only opened the app to dismiss a notification, a stricter rule that requires a real action would give a lower MAU. The number is only comparable between businesses, or between months, when the rule is the same.

## Why it matters in a valuation

MAU shows how many people the product reaches, but it does not pay the bills on its own. Revenue per user does. Dividing monthly revenue by MAU gives a simple figure to compare: an app with $6,000 of monthly revenue and 12,000 MAU earns $0.50 per active user per month (6,000 ÷ 12,000).

VALUERAQ's [App Valuation Tool](/tools/app-valuation/) asks for MAU as an optional input. It does not change the estimate. The value is driven by profit, as described on the [methodology](/methodology/) page. Buyers still ask for MAU history because a shrinking user base often comes before falling revenue, and because it shows whether users stay; see [retention rate](/glossary/retention-rate/).

A buyer will usually want the count from the analytics tool or the app store console, not from a spreadsheet the seller built, along with the exact definition of "active".

## Related terms

[Traffic](/glossary/traffic/) is the website equivalent of reach. [Retention rate](/glossary/retention-rate/) shows how many users stay. [Churn](/glossary/churn/) is the share that leave, and [ARPU](/glossary/arpu/) is revenue per user.
