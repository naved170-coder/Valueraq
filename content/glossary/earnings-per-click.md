## How it works

Every time a visitor clicks a link that can earn you money, such as an affiliate link or an ad, that is one click. EPC spreads your earnings over those clicks.

**EPC = total earnings ÷ total clicks**

Affiliate programs often show EPC per 100 clicks instead, so check which one a dashboard is quoting. Also note that clicks do not equal sales. Most clicks earn nothing, and a few earn a commission, so EPC averages the lot.

## Example

An affiliate site sends 2,500 clicks to a merchant in a month and earns $1,750 in commissions. These figures are an illustration, not a market figure.

- **EPC** = $1,750 ÷ 2,500 = **$0.70**

If the site sends 3,000 clicks next month at the same EPC, expected earnings are 3,000 × $0.70 = $2,100. If a change to the page layout lifts EPC to $0.90 on 2,500 clicks, earnings become 2,500 × $0.90 = $2,250 without any extra traffic.

## Why it matters in a valuation

A buyer of an affiliate site wants to know what drives income. EPC splits it into two parts: how many clicks the site sends and how well each click earns. Both can be checked against the merchant's own reports.

- **Stable EPC across several merchants** points to income that does not depend on one program.
- **A high EPC from one program** is a risk. If the merchant cuts its commission rate, income falls at once, and a buyer will price that in.

EPC differs from [RPM](/glossary/rpm/), which measures earnings per 1,000 visits rather than per click. A site can have a modest RPM and a strong EPC if few visitors click but those who do buy. ## Related terms

[RPM](/glossary/rpm/) is revenue per 1,000 visits. [Conversion rate](/glossary/conversion-rate/) is the share of visitors who complete a purchase or sign-up. [Traffic](/glossary/traffic/) is the supply of visits that clicks come from.
