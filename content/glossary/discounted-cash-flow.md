## How it works

A dollar received next year is worth less than a dollar today, because today's dollar could be invested and because next year's dollar might not arrive. DCF puts a number on that.

The Corporate Finance Institute describes it as a method "used to value investments by discounting the estimated future cash flows." For each future year:

**Value today = cash flow ÷ (1 + discount rate) ^ number of years away**

Add the results for every year and you have the value of the business. The discount rate is the yearly return the buyer needs for taking the risk. A riskier business calls for a higher rate, which gives a lower value.

## Example

A buyer expects a small software business to produce $50,000 a year for three years and wants a 25% yearly return.

| Year | Cash flow | Calculation | Value today |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $50,000 | ÷ 1.25 | $40,000 |
| 2 | $50,000 | ÷ 1.25² (1.5625) | $32,000 |
| 3 | $50,000 | ÷ 1.25³ (1.953) | $25,600 |
| **Total** | | | **$97,600** |

Three years of cash adds up to $150,000, but it is worth $97,600 to this buyer today. A full DCF would also add a figure for the years after the third, called the terminal value.

## Why it matters in a valuation

DCF is the main form of what the U.S. Small Business Administration calls the income approach, which "looks at projected revenue and accounts for potential risks." It is the standard tool for large companies with forecasts that can be defended.

## Its limits for small online businesses

- **The forecast is a guess.** A small website or app rarely has earnings predictable enough to forecast five years ahead.
- **Small changes swing the result.** In the example, a 20% rate gives about $105,300 and a 30% rate gives about $90,800.
- **It looks precise when it isn't.** The output has decimals, but the inputs are opinions.

This is why small digital businesses are usually priced with a [valuation multiple](/glossary/valuation-multiple/). A multiple is a shortcut for the same idea: paying 3 times yearly profit means the buyer expects to earn the price back in about three years if profit holds. {{BRAND}}'s tools use multiples for that reason, as the [methodology](/methodology/) explains.
