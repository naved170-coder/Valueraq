## How it works

A seller has information they do not want to share freely: exact revenue, customer lists, supplier names, source code and traffic data. A serious buyer needs to see it. An NDA bridges the gap. The buyer signs first, agreeing to keep the information private and to use it only to decide whether to buy.

The U.S. Small Business Administration lists a confidentiality agreement among the documents that professionals can help a buyer or seller with ([SBA](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/plan-your-business/buy-existing-business-or-franchise)).

A typical NDA covers:

- **What counts as confidential**, such as financial records, customer data and trade secrets.
- **Allowed use**, usually only to evaluate the deal.
- **Who may see it**, for example the buyer's lawyer, accountant and lender.
- **How long it lasts**, and what must be returned or deleted if the deal falls through.
- **Exceptions**, such as information that is already public.

## Example

A seller lists a small SaaS business for sale. A buyer asks for customer-level subscription data. The seller sends an NDA, the buyer signs it, and the seller opens a read-only data room with the exports. The agreement says the buyer may share the files only with their accountant and must delete them within 30 days if no deal is signed. The terms are an illustration, not a standard.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Buyers cannot judge profit, churn or traffic without real records, and sellers will not share real records with strangers. A sensible NDA lets the checking happen. Some listing sites show only summary figures until a buyer signs, and release the detail afterward. See the [due diligence checklist](/guides/digital-business-due-diligence/) for what a buyer will want to see and [what documents buyers ask for](/guides/what-documents-do-buyers-ask-for/).

For sellers, share in stages: summary numbers first, then detail after the NDA, and the most sensitive items such as customer names late in the process. An NDA does not make a careless seller safe, so remove anything the buyer does not yet need.

An NDA is a legal document and its effect differs by country and by wording. Have a lawyer write or review one before you rely on it. This page is not legal advice.

## Related terms

[Due diligence](/glossary/due-diligence/) is the checking the NDA makes possible. A [letter of intent](/glossary/letter-of-intent/) sets out the main terms once the buyer is serious. A non-compete agreement limits what the seller can do after the sale, while an NDA limits what is said about the information.
