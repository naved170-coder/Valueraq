## How it works

You choose the unit that fits the business, then list what one unit earns and what it costs.

- **An ecommerce store** uses one order. Revenue is the [average order value](/glossary/average-order-value/). Costs are the [cost of goods sold](/glossary/cost-of-goods-sold/), shipping, payment fees and the advertising spend to win the order.
- **A subscription business** uses one customer. Revenue is what the customer pays over their time as a customer ([LTV](/glossary/ltv/)). The main cost is the [cost to win them](/glossary/cac/) plus the cost of serving them.
- **An app or a website** can use one user or one thousand visits, depending on how it earns.

**Profit per unit = revenue per unit − all costs that rise with each unit**

If the result is positive, each extra unit adds to profit. If it is negative, growth makes the business lose money faster.

## Example

An online store sells a product with these figures per order. They are invented to show the arithmetic.

| Item | Per order |
|---|---|
| Average order value | $50.00 |
| Cost of goods | −$20.00 |
| Shipping and packing | −$6.00 |
| Payment fee | −$2.00 |
| Advertising to win the order | −$12.00 |
| **Profit per order** | **$10.00** |

$50 − $20 − $6 − $2 − $12 = $10. If the store ships 400 orders a month, the units add up to $10 × 400 = $4,000 a month before rent, software and other overheads.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Buyers of online businesses pay for earnings that will continue, so they test whether each sale really makes money. Strong overall profit can hide weak unit economics: a store may look profitable while a rise in ad prices turns every new order into a loss. A buyer who finds that will lower the offer or ask for more evidence.

VALUERAQ's [valuation tools](/tools/) value a business on its net profit or recurring revenue, with adjustments for age, growth and risk, and they do not take unit economics as an input. Work out your own per-unit figures anyway. They explain why profit moves, and they are among the first things a buyer asks about during [due diligence](/glossary/due-diligence/).
