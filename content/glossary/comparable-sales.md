## How it works

The U.S. Small Business Administration describes the market approach to valuation as one that "compares your business to other similar businesses that have recently sold." Comparable sales are the raw material for it.

1. Find sales of businesses like yours: same type, similar size, similar way of earning.
2. Turn each price into a [multiple](/glossary/valuation-multiple/): price ÷ yearly profit, or price ÷ yearly revenue.
3. Apply the typical multiple from those sales to your own figures.

## Example

Three content websites sold recently:

| Site | Yearly profit | Price | Multiple |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | $40,000 | $120,000 | 3.0 |
| B | $25,000 | $85,000 | 3.4 |
| C | $60,000 | $168,000 | 2.8 |

The middle multiple is 3.0. A similar site with $50,000 of yearly profit would come out at about $50,000 × 3.0 = $150,000.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Comparable sales show what buyers actually paid, not what a model says they should pay. When good comparables exist, they are the most persuasive evidence in a negotiation.

## Why they are hard to use for small online businesses

- **Most sales are private.** Prices and profit figures are rarely published.
- **The figures behind a price differ.** One seller's "profit" includes their own pay, another's doesn't.
- **Terms change the price.** A sale paid partly later is not comparable with one paid in full at closing.
- **Few businesses are truly alike.** Traffic sources, age and owner workload all move the price.

This is why {{BRAND}} doesn't present its default multiples as market data. They are published assumptions, set out in the [methodology](/methodology/), and the tools show how each adjustment changes the result.

## How to use comparables well

Use them as a check, not the only answer. Collect as many as you can, look at the spread rather than one sale, and compare like with like: profit before or after the owner's pay, and price paid in full or over time. When the spread is wide, the business's own figures matter more than any single comparison.
