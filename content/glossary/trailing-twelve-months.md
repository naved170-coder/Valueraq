## How it works

Add up the last 12 full months. If today is in October, the period runs from last October to this September. Next month, the oldest month drops off and the newest is added, so the figure rolls forward.

When you only have yearly and year-to-date accounts, the Corporate Finance Institute gives this formula:

**TTM = latest full year + current year to date − the same period last year**

TTM is also written LTM, for last twelve months.

## Example

A newsletter is being valued at the end of September. Its monthly profit was:

| Period | Profit |
|---|---|
| October to December last year | $9,000 |
| January to September this year | $36,000 |
| **TTM profit** | **$45,000** |

Last calendar year's profit was $30,000. Valuing on that figure would understate what the newsletter earns now by a third.

Using the formula: last full year $30,000 + this year to date $36,000 − January to September last year $21,000 = $45,000.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Small businesses are usually valued as a [multiple](/glossary/valuation-multiple/) of 12 months of profit. TTM is the standard period for three reasons:

- **It is current.** It reflects the business as it is today, not as it was at the last year end.
- **It covers every season.** A full year includes the strong months and the weak ones.
- **It is hard to flatter.** One good month moves a 12-month total far less than it moves a three-month average.

{{BRAND}}'s tools ask for the average of the last 12 months for the same reasons.

## What to watch for

- **A falling trend inside the 12 months.** If the last three months are well below the first three, TTM overstates what a buyer will receive. Compare the two.
- **One-off spikes.** A single viral month or a large one-time sale should be explained or taken out.
- **Switching periods.** A seller who quotes TTM revenue next to last year's costs is mixing periods. Both must cover the same months.

For a business less than a year old, TTM doesn't exist yet. Sellers then quote a run rate, one recent month multiplied by 12, which carries more risk.
