## Formula

**Gross margin = (revenue − cost of revenue) ÷ revenue × 100**

## What counts as cost of revenue

| Business | Typical direct costs |
|---|---|
| SaaS | Hosting and infrastructure, third-party services used by the product, payment fees, often customer support |
| AI products | Model/API usage or GPU costs, hosting, data costs, payment fees |
| Ecommerce | Cost of goods, inbound shipping, fulfillment, payment fees |
| Apps | App-store commissions, backend services |

Be consistent from month to month, and state what you include.

## Example

An AI writing tool earns $12,000 in a month. It pays $3,600 for model usage, $600 for hosting and $360 in payment fees.

**Gross margin = ($12,000 − $4,560) ÷ $12,000 = 62%.**

### Same revenue, three margins

Three businesses each earn $10,000 in a month. The costs are illustrative.

| | SaaS tool | AI tool | Online store |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $10,000 | $10,000 | $10,000 |
| Direct costs | $1,800 | $4,800 | $6,500 |
| Gross profit | $8,200 | $5,200 | $3,500 |
| **Gross margin** | **82%** | **52%** | **35%** |

Each extra $1,000 of sales leaves about $820, $520 or $350 to pay for everything else. A buyer reads the gap as a difference in how fast profit can grow, which is why the same revenue can be priced very differently.

## Why gross margin matters for valuation

Gross margin sets how much of each new dollar of revenue can turn into profit. A software business with high gross margin can grow profit quickly as revenue grows; a business with low gross margin needs far more revenue to earn the same profit.

For AI businesses, gross margin is especially important, because model costs can take a large and changing share of revenue. {{BRAND}}'s [SaaS](/tools/saas-valuation/) and [AI](/tools/ai-business-valuation/) tools raise the multiple slightly for margins above 80% and lower it for margins below 70%, more steeply below 50%. See the [methodology](/methodology/#adjustments).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between gross margin and gross profit?

[Gross profit](/glossary/gross-profit/) is a dollar amount: revenue minus direct costs. Gross margin is that amount as a percentage of revenue. In the example above, the SaaS tool has gross profit of $8,200 and a gross margin of 82%.

### What is a good gross margin?

It depends on the type of business, so compare yours with your own past months and with businesses like yours rather than with a single number. A falling margin on steady revenue usually means a direct cost went up.

### Is gross margin the same as net margin?

No. Gross margin counts only direct costs. Net margin also subtracts overheads such as advertising, software and salaries, as covered under [net profit](/glossary/net-profit/).
