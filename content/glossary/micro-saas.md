## How it works

A micro-SaaS is a [SaaS](/glossary/saas/) product reduced to one job. It might clean up a spreadsheet export, post to a single social network, or track one kind of report. Customers pay a monthly or yearly subscription, and the founder often handles building, support and marketing alone.

There is no official size line. The word describes a way of running software, small and narrow, not a fixed revenue figure. What makes it different from a larger SaaS company is that the business and the founder are closely tied together, and the customer base is small enough that a few cancellations show up in the monthly numbers.

Stripe defines monthly recurring revenue as "the total amount of money a company can expect to receive from its existing customers each month". For a micro-SaaS, [MRR](/glossary/mrr/) is the starting figure for almost every conversation about value.

## Example

A hypothetical micro-SaaS has 120 customers paying $50 a month.

**MRR = 120 × $50 = $6,000. ARR = $6,000 × 12 = $72,000.**

{{BRAND}}'s default range for a SaaS business is 2.0x to 5.0x [ARR](/glossary/arr/), with a midpoint of 3.5x, before adjustments. These are {{BRAND}} editorial assumptions, not market data.

- Low: $72,000 × 2.0 = $144,000
- Midpoint: $72,000 × 3.5 = $252,000
- High: $72,000 × 5.0 = $360,000

The figures are an illustration. A real result depends on [churn](/glossary/churn/), margins and the other inputs in the [methodology](/methodology/).

## Why it matters in a valuation

Small size changes how a buyer reads the numbers.

- **Churn is noisy.** With 120 customers, six cancellations in a month is 5%. One month tells little, so buyers look at a longer run.
- **The founder is the business.** If one person writes the code and answers every email, the buyer is partly paying for that person's time. See [key-person risk](/glossary/key-person-risk/).
- **One customer can matter.** If a single account pays a large share of MRR, losing it changes the whole picture.

A buyer will often compare the ARR figure with annual profit, since a very small software business may be bought for the income it produces. The full approach is in [micro-SaaS valuation](/guides/micro-saas-valuation/).
