## How it works

Year-over-year growth sets one period against the same period twelve months earlier: this March against last March, or the latest three months against the same three months last year. Using the same period removes most of the effect of [seasonality](/glossary/seasonality/), which is why buyers prefer it to a month-on-month comparison.

**YoY growth (%) = (this period − same period last year) ÷ same period last year × 100**

A negative result means the figure fell. The same method works for revenue, profit, traffic or paying customers, as long as both periods are measured the same way.

## Example

A software product earned $24,000 in revenue in the three months to September last year and $27,600 in the same three months this year.

- Change = $27,600 − $24,000 = $3,600
- **YoY growth = $3,600 ÷ $24,000 = 15%**

If revenue had fallen to $21,600 instead, growth would be ($21,600 − $24,000) ÷ $24,000 = **−10%**.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Growth tells a buyer which way the business is heading, so it moves the price. In {{BRAND}}'s published rules, the revenue trend adjusts the base multiple like this:

| Revenue change over 12 months | Effect on the multiple |
|---|---|
| Below −20% | −25% |
| −20% to below −5% | −12% |
| −5% to +5% | 0% (baseline) |
| Above +5% to +25% | +8% |
| Above +25% to +75% | +15% |
| Above +75% | +20% |

The 15% in the example falls in the "above +5% to +25%" band, so the multiple rises by 8%. A default midpoint of 3.5x becomes 3.5 × 1.08 = **3.78x**, before any other adjustment. These are the website's own assumptions, not market data. See the [methodology](/methodology/) for the full list.

Two cautions apply. A single growth figure can hide a recent change of direction, so look at the monthly trend as well. And fast growth from a small base is easy to achieve and hard to repeat. Pair it with [trailing twelve months](/glossary/trailing-twelve-months/) revenue and ask what caused it.

## Related terms

- [Trailing twelve months](/glossary/trailing-twelve-months/): the latest twelve months of results, a steadier base than one quarter.
- [Seasonality](/glossary/seasonality/): regular swings that YoY comparisons help to remove.
- [Run rate](/glossary/run-rate/): a recent period turned into an annual figure.
- [MRR](/glossary/mrr/): monthly recurring revenue, often tracked as a YoY percentage.
- [Revenue multiple](/glossary/revenue-multiple/): the price as a multiple of revenue, which growth can raise or lower.
