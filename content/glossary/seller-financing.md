## How it works

The buyer pays part of the price at closing and signs a written promise to pay the rest, often called a seller note. The note sets out:

- **The amount** being deferred.
- **The term**: how many months or years the buyer has to repay.
- **Interest**, if any, and how it is calculated.
- **The payment schedule**: usually equal monthly instalments.
- **Security**: what the seller can claim if the buyer stops paying, such as the return of the business's assets.

The debt is owed whatever happens to the business. That is the difference from an [earnout](/glossary/earnout/), which is paid only if targets are met.

## Example

A website sells for $200,000. The buyer pays 70% at closing and the seller finances 30% over 24 months.

- **Paid at closing** = $200,000 × 70% = $140,000.
- **Seller note** = $200,000 − $140,000 = $60,000.
- **Monthly instalment before interest** = $60,000 ÷ 24 = $2,500.

Any interest the two sides agree is paid on top of the $2,500. If the site earns $5,000 a month in profit, the instalments take half of it for two years, which a buyer should allow for when planning.

## Why it matters when buying or selling

For a **buyer**, seller financing reduces the cash needed up front. It also shows that the seller expects the business to keep performing, since the seller is only paid in full if it does well enough for the buyer to keep up the payments.

For a **seller**, it can widen the pool of buyers and support a higher price than an all-cash offer. In exchange, the seller takes on the risk that the buyer mismanages the business and defaults. Recovering a damaged website or store is rarely worth what was owed.

Points a seller should check:

- The buyer's experience and ability to pay from other income.
- How much is paid at closing. A larger down payment means the buyer has more to lose by walking away.
- What security the note gives, and how practical it would be to enforce.

When comparing offers, look at the amount paid at closing and the reliability of the rest, not only the headline price. A [letter of intent](/glossary/letter-of-intent/) should state these terms clearly.

## Take advice

Loan terms, security over business assets and the tax treatment of instalment payments differ by country. Have a lawyer draft the note, and ask an accountant how the payments will be taxed.
