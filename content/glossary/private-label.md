## How it works

A private label seller picks a product that a manufacturer already makes, or a variation of it, and sells it under the seller's own name and packaging. The manufacturer produces the goods. The seller handles the brand, the listing, the marketing and usually the customer service.

This differs from [dropshipping](/glossary/dropshipping/), where a supplier ships a standard product for the seller. In private label, the seller usually buys stock in advance, so there is inventory to fund and store. Many private label sellers use [Fulfillment by Amazon](/glossary/fulfillment-by-amazon/) or a similar service for storage and shipping.

In the accounts, the manufacturer's price plus freight and duties are the [cost of goods sold](/glossary/cost-of-goods-sold/). What is left from each sale is [gross margin](/glossary/gross-margin/).

## Example

A private label store sells a product for $30 that costs $12 to make and ship to the warehouse. Gross profit is $30 − $12 = $18, a gross margin of $18 ÷ $30 = 60%. Marketplace fees, advertising and software come out of that $18.

Suppose the store earns $4,000 a month in net profit, or $48,000 a year. Using {{BRAND}}'s default ecommerce range of 2.0 to 3.5 times yearly profit, the midpoint is 2.7, so the estimated midpoint value is 2.7 × $48,000 = $129,600, about $130,000. Inventory at cost is shown separately and is usually paid for on top. This is an illustration, not market data.

## Why it matters in a valuation

A private label brand is something a buyer can take over: the brand name, the listings, the reviews and the supplier relationship. That is usually more transferable than a store that sells other people's brands. But a buyer will still ask who the manufacturer is, whether there is more than one, and whether the store depends on a single marketplace. See the [ecommerce valuation guide](/guides/ecommerce-valuation/) and the [Amazon FBA valuation guide](/guides/amazon-fba-business-valuation/) for how {{BRAND}}'s default rules treat channel concentration and fulfillment.
