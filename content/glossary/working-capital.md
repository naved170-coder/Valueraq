## How it works

**Working capital = current assets − current liabilities.** "Current" means due or usable within about twelve months.

Current assets include cash, inventory and money customers owe the business. Current liabilities include supplier bills, unpaid taxes, customer refunds still to pay and any short-term loan. If the result is positive, the business can cover its near-term bills from what it holds. If it is negative, it cannot without new money.

Working capital is not the same as profit or [cash flow](/glossary/cash-flow/). It is a snapshot of one day, while the other two measure a period.

## Example

An online store has these balances on the day of sale.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Cash | $12,000 |
| Inventory | $30,000 |
| Money owed by customers and platforms | $3,000 |
| **Current assets** | **$45,000** |
| Supplier bills due | −$14,000 |
| Refunds and taxes due | −$4,000 |
| **Current liabilities** | **−$18,000** |

Working capital is $45,000 − $18,000 = $27,000. Most of it is stock, so the store needs the inventory to keep selling for the number to be useful.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Many small online businesses are sold as an [asset sale](/glossary/asset-sale/), and the agreed price often covers the site, brand and accounts but not the cash in the bank. Stock is different, and the two sides must say clearly whether it is included. Working capital is where those questions come from:

- **Is inventory included in the price, or paid for separately?** An ecommerce buyer needs enough stock to keep selling from day one.
- **Who pays the bills that arrive after closing?** Supplier bills for goods ordered before the sale need an owner in the agreement.
- **Is the business starved of cash?** A buyer who must immediately fund stock or pay down supplier debt has less left for growth.

Write the treatment of stock, cash and unpaid bills into the [letter of intent](/glossary/letter-of-intent/) and check the figures during [due diligence](/glossary/due-diligence/). Services businesses and most content sites hold little inventory, so working capital is often small for them.
