## How it works

The Corporate Finance Institute defines enterprise value as "the measure of a company's total value". The formula is:

**Enterprise value = equity value + debt − cash**

For a company listed on a stock exchange, equity value is its market capitalization. For a private business, it is the price paid for the owner's shares.

Debt is added because a buyer who takes over the company takes over what it owes. Cash is subtracted because the buyer gets it back on day one. The result is the price of the business itself, whatever mix of loans and savings the current owner happens to have.

## Example

Two software companies each sell for $600,000 in equity value.

| | Company A | Company B |
|---|---|---|
| Equity value | $600,000 | $600,000 |
| Debt | $0 | $150,000 |
| Cash | $50,000 | $10,000 |
| **Enterprise value** | **$550,000** | **$740,000** |

Company A: $600,000 + $0 − $50,000 = $550,000. Company B: $600,000 + $150,000 − $10,000 = $740,000. The headline price is the same, but the buyer of Company B is really paying $190,000 more for the business.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Enterprise value is the top half of several common ratios, such as EV to revenue and EV to [EBITDA](/glossary/ebitda/). Using it lets you compare businesses with different amounts of debt on equal terms. A [valuation multiple](/glossary/valuation-multiple/) built on equity value alone would make a heavily indebted company look cheap.

## How it applies to small online businesses

Most small websites, apps and stores change hands as an [asset sale](/glossary/asset-sale/). The buyer takes the domain, code, content and customer accounts. The seller keeps the company, its bank balance and its debts. In that case the agreed price already is the value of the business with no debt and no cash, so enterprise value and price are the same figure.

The difference starts to matter when a buyer purchases the company itself, or when the deal includes stock, customer deposits or loans. Then both sides need to agree what is included before they compare the price with a multiple.
