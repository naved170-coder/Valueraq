## How it works

**Capitalization rate = annual earnings ÷ price × 100**

Rearranged, the same relationship gives a value from earnings:

**Value = annual earnings ÷ capitalization rate**

The term comes mostly from property investing, where earnings are the yearly income after running costs. It can be used for any asset that produces steady earnings, including a small online business. In that case the earnings figure is usually [SDE](/glossary/sde/) or net profit.

The key link is with the multiple. A [profit multiple](/glossary/profit-multiple/) is price divided by earnings, and a capitalization rate is earnings divided by price. Each is 1 divided by the other.

## Example

A hypothetical website earns $36,000 of net profit a year and is priced at $108,000.

**Capitalization rate = $36,000 ÷ $108,000 × 100 = 33.3%.**

**Profit multiple = $108,000 ÷ $36,000 = 3.0x.**

Check the link: 1 ÷ 3.0 = 0.333, which is 33.3%. If the buyer believes a fair rate is 25% instead, the value is $36,000 ÷ 0.25 = $144,000, the same as applying a 4.0x multiple (1 ÷ 0.25 = 4). The figures are an illustration only and do not predict what any business will earn.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Sellers and brokers usually quote a multiple, while buyers who compare investments often think in rates of return. Moving between the two takes one division, so you can compare a website with other uses of the same money.

Some points to keep in mind.

- **A higher rate means a lower price for the same earnings.** A buyer who sees more risk asks for a higher rate, which pushes the value down.
- **The rate is only as good as the earnings.** If the earnings figure includes add-backs that do not hold up, the rate looks better than the real return.
- **It assumes the earnings continue.** Unlike a forecast model, the simple rate takes one year's earnings as steady. The [discounted cash flow](/glossary/discounted-cash-flow/) method is the usual alternative when earnings are expected to change.
- **It is a close cousin of ROI.** For a purchase with no extra costs, the rate and the first-year [return on investment](/glossary/return-on-investment/) are the same number.

VALUERAQ's default website range is 2.5x to 4.0x annual profit, which works out to rates of 40% down to 25%. These are VALUERAQ's own assumptions, explained in the [methodology](/methodology/), not market data.
