## How it works

Retention rate is the mirror image of [churn](/glossary/churn/). Churn counts who left. Retention counts who stayed. Stripe describes churn as "the percentage of total customers who discontinue using a product or service during a given period" ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/resources/more/essential-saas-metrics)), so retention is what remains after that share is removed.

**Customer retention rate = (customers at the end of the period − customers added during the period) ÷ customers at the start of the period**

Customers who joined during the period are taken out of the numerator. Otherwise a business that signs many new customers would look as if it kept everyone.

For revenue, Stripe defines net revenue retention as "the percentage of revenue that is retained from existing customers over a given period of time". That figure can pass 100% when upgrades outweigh cancellations; see [net revenue retention](/glossary/net-revenue-retention/).

## Example

A subscription business starts the quarter with 400 customers. It adds 60 new ones and ends with 430.

- Customers kept from the start: 430 − 60 = 370
- Customer retention rate: 370 ÷ 400 = **92.5%**
- Customers lost: 400 − 370 = 30, so churn is 7.5% for the quarter

Retention measured over a longer period is always lower than over a shorter one. A business that keeps 97% of customers each month keeps only about 0.97¹² = 69.4% over a year, because each month's loss builds on the last.

## Why it matters in a valuation

A buyer is paying for revenue that continues after the sale. Retention is the most direct evidence of that. Two businesses with the same [MRR](/glossary/mrr/) can be worth very different amounts if one loses 2% of customers a month and the other loses 8%.

VALUERAQ's [SaaS valuation tool](/tools/saas-valuation/) does not ask for retention directly. It asks for monthly churn and moves the multiple up or down according to published bands, listed on the [methodology](/methodology/) page. Retention figures are only as good as the way they are counted, so a buyer will usually ask to see customer-level records.

## Related terms

[Churn](/glossary/churn/) is the share lost. [Net revenue retention](/glossary/net-revenue-retention/) includes upgrades and downgrades. [Expansion revenue](/glossary/expansion-revenue/) is the extra revenue existing customers add.
