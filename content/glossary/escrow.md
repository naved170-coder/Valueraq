## How it works

In the sale of a digital business, each side fears going first. The buyer doesn't want to pay before receiving the business, and the seller doesn't want to hand over domains and accounts before being paid. Escrow removes that problem.

A typical sequence:

1. Buyer and seller agree the price, what is being transferred and an inspection period, and record this with the escrow provider.
2. The buyer pays the funds to the escrow provider, which confirms receipt to both sides.
3. The seller transfers the assets: domain, hosting, code, store or app accounts, email list and so on.
4. The buyer checks that everything has arrived and works as described, within the inspection period.
5. The buyer confirms acceptance, and the escrow provider releases the funds to the seller.

If something is missing, the funds stay in escrow until the two sides resolve it under the provider's dispute process.

## Example

A buyer agrees to pay $80,000 for a content website, with a seven-day inspection period. The buyer pays $80,000 into escrow. The seller transfers the domain and site files. On day four the buyer confirms that the site, analytics and advertising accounts are all in their control, and the provider releases $80,000 to the seller, less any fee the two sides agreed the seller would bear.

## Holdbacks

Sometimes part of the price stays in escrow for longer, for example for a few months after closing. This is called a holdback. It gives the buyer a source of recovery if a statement the seller made turns out to be untrue. It differs from an [earnout](/glossary/earnout/), which depends on future performance.

## Why escrow matters when buying or selling

- It protects both sides from the most common fraud in online sales: paying and receiving nothing, or transferring and not being paid.
- It sets a clear point at which the deal is complete.
- It does not replace [due diligence](/glossary/due-diligence/). Escrow confirms that assets were delivered, not that the revenue claims were true.

Check who the provider is, whether it is licensed where it operates, what it charges and who pays. Beware of escrow sites suggested by a counterparty that you cannot verify independently.

## Take advice

Escrow terms and the rules that govern escrow providers differ by country. For any sizeable deal, have a lawyer review the escrow instructions together with the sale agreement.
