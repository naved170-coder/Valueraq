## How it works

**Net income = revenue − cost of goods sold − operating expenses − interest − income taxes**

It is the last line of a profit and loss statement, which is why it is called the bottom line. "Net income" and [net profit](/glossary/net-profit/) mean the same thing in practice. Read how each line is built in [how to read a profit and loss statement](/guides/how-to-read-a-profit-and-loss-statement/).

Net income can be negative. Then the business made a loss for the period.

## Example

A hypothetical small online business had these results over twelve months.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Revenue | $200,000 |
| Cost of goods sold | $80,000 |
| Operating expenses (ads, tools, contractors, owner's pay) | $70,000 |
| Operating income | $50,000 |
| Interest and income taxes | $12,000 |
| **Net income** | **$38,000** |

**Net income = $200,000 − $80,000 − $70,000 − $12,000 = $38,000.** As a share of revenue that is 19%.

## Net income vs SDE vs EBITDA

Buyers of small businesses rarely price on net income as it stands. They adjust it.

| Measure | Starts from net income, then | Example, using the figures above |
|---|---|---|
| Net income | nothing | $38,000 |
| [EBITDA](/glossary/ebitda/) | add back interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization | $38,000 + $12,000 = $50,000 (no depreciation in this example) |
| [SDE](/glossary/sde/) | add back interest, taxes, the owner's pay and personal or one-off costs | $50,000 + $30,000 owner's pay = $80,000 |

The owner's $30,000 pay sits inside the operating expenses, so it is added back to reach SDE. The result is the profit a new owner-operator could expect before paying themselves.

## Why it matters in a valuation

A multiple is applied to a profit figure, so the profit figure must be defined. A seller quoting $38,000 and a buyer expecting $80,000 are not disagreeing about the business; they are using different measures. Always ask which one is on the table.

Net income is also affected by choices that have nothing to do with how the business performs: how assets are written off, what tax the owner pays, how the business is financed. That is why small online businesses are usually valued on SDE. See [add-backs](/glossary/add-backs/); a buyer will ask for proof of each one.

VALUERAQ's profit-based tools ask for monthly net profit before the owner's pay (SDE) and multiply it by 12. They do not use net income after tax.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is net income the same as profit?

Usually yes, when "profit" means the bottom line. Gross profit and operating income are earlier lines on the statement and are higher than net income.

### Is net income the same as cash in the bank?

No. Net income is an accounting result. A business can have net income and still be short of cash, for example when it pays suppliers before customers pay it.
