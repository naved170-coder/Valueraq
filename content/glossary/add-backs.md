## How it works

A small business's accounts are usually kept to suit the owner and the tax return, so reported profit is often lower than what the business really produces. Add-backs correct for that. Each one takes a cost out of the accounts and puts the money back into profit.

Common types:

- **The owner's pay and benefits.** Added back when calculating [SDE](/glossary/sde/), because SDE is profit before the owner is paid.
- **Personal costs run through the business.** A family phone plan or a personal subscription.
- **One-off costs.** A site redesign, a legal dispute or a move to a new host that won't happen again.
- **Costs unrelated to running the business.** Interest on the owner's own loan, for example.
- **Depreciation and amortization.** Accounting entries, not cash leaving the bank.

## Example

A content website reports a net profit of $40,000 for the year.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Reported net profit | $40,000 |
| Owner's salary | + $30,000 |
| One-off redesign | + $6,000 |
| Personal software subscriptions | + $1,200 |
| **SDE** | **$77,200** |

At 3.2 times yearly profit, the midpoint of {{BRAND}}'s default range for websites, the add-backs lift the estimate from $128,000 ($40,000 × 3.2) to $247,040 ($77,200 × 3.2). That is why buyers look at each one closely.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Every dollar added back is multiplied. On the example above, a $1,000 add-back is worth $3,200 of price. A seller has a reason to find as many as possible, and a buyer has a reason to question each of them.

## Add-backs buyers reject

- **Costs that will come back.** If the "one-off" redesign happens every two years, it is a running cost.
- **Costs the business needs.** Advertising, freelancers and tools that keep revenue coming in stay in the accounts.
- **Anything without a receipt.** An add-back you can't show on a bank statement or invoice will be struck out.
- **The owner's unpaid work, counted twice.** SDE already leaves out the owner's pay. A buyer who must hire a manager will take that cost off again. See [SDE vs EBITDA](/guides/sde-vs-ebitda/).

A short, well-documented list is more convincing than a long one. Checking it is a standard part of [due diligence](/guides/digital-business-due-diligence/).
