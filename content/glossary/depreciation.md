## How it works

When a business buys equipment that will last several years, accountants do not treat the whole price as a cost of the year it was bought. They spread it across the years the asset is useful. Each year's share is the depreciation expense, and it appears on the profit and loss statement as a cost even though no cash leaves the business that year.

The simplest method is straight-line depreciation:

**Yearly depreciation = (cost − expected resale value at the end) ÷ years of useful life**

Depreciation applies to physical assets such as computers, cameras, vehicles and furniture. The same idea for non-physical assets, such as a purchased software license, is called amortization. Which method a business uses, and how many years it assumes, are accounting choices. Tax rules in your country may set their own schedules, so ask an accountant which applies to you.

## Example

An online store buys a $3,000 laptop and photo equipment. It expects to use it for 5 years and to resell it for $500. The figures are made up.

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Cost to spread | $3,000 − $500 | $2,500 |
| Depreciation each year | $2,500 ÷ 5 | $500 |
| Book value after year 2 | $3,000 − ($500 × 2) | $2,000 |

The store's profit is $500 lower each year because of this expense, although it paid the $3,000 in one go. Its [book value](/glossary/book-value/) for the equipment falls by $500 each year.

## Why depreciation matters in a valuation

Depreciation lowers reported profit without costing any cash that year, so two identical businesses can show different profit depending on when they bought their equipment. To compare them fairly, buyers add it back. [EBITDA](/glossary/ebitda/) is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, and [SDE](/glossary/sde/) starts from profit and adds depreciation back along with the owner's pay. BizBuySell lists depreciation and amortization as one of the standard categories of [add-backs](/glossary/add-backs/) ([BizBuySell](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/article/add-backs-sde/)).

Adding it back is fair only if the assets rarely need replacing. A business that must buy new equipment every few years has a real cost that depreciation was approximating. For most online businesses, which own little equipment, the amount is small, but check it.
