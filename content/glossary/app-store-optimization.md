## How it works

ASO is search engine optimization for an app store. It has two jobs: help people **find** the app, and persuade them to **install** it once they see it.

- **Finding:** the app's name, subtitle or short description, and keywords tell the store what the app is about, so it can show the app for matching searches. Category choice and update history can also play a part.
- **Installing:** the icon, screenshots, preview video, description, ratings and reviews decide whether a visitor taps install. This is a [conversion rate](/glossary/conversion-rate/) problem: of the people who view the listing, how many install?

Each store has its own fields and rules, so owners test small changes one at a time and watch the results in the store's own reports.

## Example

An app's listing is viewed 10,000 times a month, and 30% of viewers install it.

- **Installs now:** 10,000 × 30% = 3,000 a month.
- After better screenshots and a clearer first line, the install rate rises to 35%: 10,000 × 35% = 3,500 a month.

That is 500 extra installs a month with no advertising spend. If better keywords also lift views to 12,000, installs become 12,000 × 35% = 4,200 a month. These figures are made up to show the arithmetic.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Buyers of apps ask where users come from. An app that gets most of its installs from store search is cheaper to keep growing than one that needs paid ads for every download, and that kind of [organic traffic](/glossary/organic-traffic/) is easier to defend in a sale. Strong, honest ratings and steady listing views also make the user numbers more believable.

The same facts show a risk. Store ranking depends on the store's own rules, and a change there can move installs overnight, which is a form of [platform risk](/glossary/platform-risk/). A buyer will ask for the store's search and install reports for at least 12 months, alongside [monthly active users](/glossary/monthly-active-users/) and revenue records.

If you are selling, keep a record of what you changed in the listing and when, and the effect it had. Read [how to sell an app](/guides/how-to-sell-an-app/) and the [app valuation guide](/guides/app-valuation/) for how these numbers feed the price.
