## How it works

GMV adds up the sticker value of everything customers buy through a business during a period, usually a month or a year. It is a measure of how much trade flows through the business, not of how much the business keeps.

**GMV = number of orders × average order value**

You can also add up the price of every item sold. GMV is normally counted at the time of sale, before refunds, discounts a seller funds, shipping, payment fees and taxes are taken out. Companies define it slightly differently, so when you see a GMV figure, ask what it includes.

## Example

A hypothetical online marketplace connects independent sellers with buyers. In one month:

- Buyers place 2,000 orders.
- The [average order value](/glossary/average-order-value/) is $60.
- The marketplace charges sellers a 10% fee on each sale.

**GMV = 2,000 × $60 = $120,000.**

The marketplace does not keep $120,000. Its revenue is the fee: $120,000 × 10% = $12,000. The sellers receive the rest. The percentage of GMV that the platform keeps as revenue ($12,000 ÷ $120,000 = 10%) is often called its take rate. The figures are an illustration only, not market data.

## GMV vs revenue vs profit

| Measure | Example | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| GMV | $120,000 | Total value of goods sold through the business |
| Revenue | $12,000 | What the business earns (here, the fees) |
| Profit | less than $12,000 | What is left after the business's own costs |

For a store that buys products and sells them itself, revenue and GMV are close, except for returns and discounts. For a [marketplace](/glossary/marketplace/), revenue can be a small fraction of GMV.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Buyers of small online businesses pay for what the business earns, not for the money that passes through it. A marketplace with $1,200,000 of yearly GMV and a 10% fee has $120,000 of revenue. A buyer applying a revenue or [profit multiple](/glossary/profit-multiple/) uses that $120,000, or the profit left after costs, never the GMV.

GMV is still useful as a sign of activity and growth, and as the base for take rate. Treat it with care in three cases:

- **A seller who quotes GMV as if it were revenue.** Ask for the revenue line in the books and bank records.
- **Returns and cancellations.** A high GMV with many refunds overstates real sales.
- **Sales on another platform.** If sellers use your marketplace only to take payment, the trade may not need you.

See [ecommerce valuation](/guides/ecommerce-valuation/) for how stores are valued, and [revenue multiple](/glossary/revenue-multiple/) and [net profit](/glossary/net-profit/) for the measures buyers apply.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is GMV the same as revenue?

No. GMV is the total value sold. Revenue is what the business earns from those sales. For a marketplace that takes a fee, revenue is only a share of GMV.

### What is a good GMV?

There is no single good number. GMV matters less than the take rate, the profit and the trend over time. A growing GMV with a falling take rate may not mean growing earnings.
