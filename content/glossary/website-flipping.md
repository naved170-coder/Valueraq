## How it works

A flipper looks for a site priced low for what it could earn, buys it, raises its [profit](/glossary/profit-multiple/) or fixes what holds its price down, then sells. The price at each end is usually profit times a multiple, so a flip can make money in two ways: profit rises, or the multiple improves because the site is less risky.

Typical improvements are cleaner records, a second traffic source, documented processes and fixing slow pages. See [increase website value before selling](/guides/increase-website-value-before-selling/).

## Example

All figures are invented to show the arithmetic.

| Step | Figure |
|---|---|
| Profit when bought | $2,000 a month, $24,000 a year |
| Purchase price at 2.5x | $24,000 × 2.5 = $60,000 |
| Improvement spend | $6,000 |
| Profit when sold | $3,000 a month, $36,000 a year |
| Sale price at 3.0x | $36,000 × 3.0 = $108,000 |
| Selling costs, assumed at 10% | $10,800 |
| **Net gain** | $108,000 − $60,000 − $6,000 − $10,800 = **$31,200** |

The gain is $31,200 on $66,000 invested, about 47%. It ignores tax, income earned while holding the site, and the owner's own time.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Flipping works only when the buying price is right, so valuing before you buy is the main skill. Compare the asking price with the profit multiple you expect, and check the claims in [due diligence](/glossary/due-diligence/). VALUERAQ's default profit range is 2.5x to 4.0x, which is its own assumption (see the [methodology](/methodology/)), not market data.

Check each risk before buying:

| Risk | How to check |
|---|---|
| Traffic drops after purchase | Review a full year of data, and the main traffic sources |
| Overpaying | Test the price at cautious and confident profit figures |
| Improvements do not lift profit | Start with cheap, reversible changes |
| Unexpected platform or program changes | Note which single source supplies most income |
| Selling takes longer than planned | Allow time and cash to wait |

Read [red flags when buying a website](/guides/red-flags-when-buying-a-website/) first.
