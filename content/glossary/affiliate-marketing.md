## How it works

A website recommends a product and includes a special link. When a visitor clicks it and buys, the merchant's tracking records the sale against the site and pays a commission. The merchant sets the terms: the percentage or fixed amount, which actions count, how long a click stays valid, and when payouts are made.

Many merchants run their programs through an affiliate network, which handles tracking and payments for many sites. Others run their own. Either way, the site owner is paid by someone else's program, under someone else's rules.

## Example

A review site gets 10,000 visits in a month. The figures are an illustration, not a market figure.

- 3% of visitors click through to the merchant: 10,000 × 3% = **300 clicks**
- 4% of those clicks end in an order: 300 × 4% = **12 orders**
- Average order of $80 with a 6% commission: $80 × 6% = **$4.80 per order**

**Commission income = 12 × $4.80 = $57.60 for the month.** Dividing by clicks gives $57.60 ÷ 300 = $0.19 [earnings per click](/glossary/earnings-per-click/). Change any one step, such as the click rate or the commission, and the income changes in proportion.

## Why it matters in a valuation

Affiliate income is earned from a third party's program, so buyers look at how safe it is. They ask:

- **How concentrated is it?** If most income comes from one merchant, a cut in the commission rate or the end of the program removes it.
- **Do the terms carry over?** Some programs need the account holder's approval to change owner, so check the program's rules before a sale.
- **Is it proven?** Payout reports from the network or merchant, matched to bank deposits, show the income is real. See [how to verify website revenue](/guides/how-to-verify-website-revenue/).
- **Where do the visitors come from?** Income that depends on one search ranking is less steady. See [organic traffic](/glossary/organic-traffic/).
