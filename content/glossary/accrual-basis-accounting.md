## How it works

Under accrual accounting, the books follow the work, not the bank account. A sale counts in the month the customer receives what they bought, even if they pay later. A bill counts in the month the business uses the service, even if it pays later.

Two things follow:

- **Money owed to you** is recorded as [accounts receivable](/glossary/accounts-receivable/).
- **Money you owe** is recorded as [accounts payable](/glossary/accounts-payable/).

Cash a customer pays before you have delivered is not yet revenue. It is [deferred revenue](/glossary/deferred-revenue/) and becomes revenue as you deliver.

## Example

A newsletter business sells a $1,200 annual sponsorship in December. The sponsor pays the full $1,200 up front. The sponsorship runs for 12 months, one issue a month. All figures are made up.

| Month | Cash received | Revenue recorded (accrual) |
|---|---|---|
| December | $1,200 | $100 |
| Each of the next 11 months | $0 | $100 |
| **Total for 12 months** | **$1,200** | **$1,200** |

Check: $1,200 ÷ 12 months = $100 a month.

On an accrual basis, the business shows $100 of revenue every month. On a cash basis, December looks like a $1,200 month and the next 11 months show nothing from this sponsor. The total is the same. The monthly picture is not.

## Why accrual accounting matters in a valuation

- **It matches income to the period it belongs to.** Monthly profit is steadier and easier to compare, which helps when a buyer builds a [trailing twelve months](/glossary/trailing-twelve-months/) figure.
- **It shows what the business owes.** Unpaid bills and customer prepayments appear on the [balance sheet](/glossary/balance-sheet/) instead of staying hidden until cash moves.
- **It exposes timing tricks.** A seller who delays paying suppliers, or collects annual fees early, can make a cash-basis month look strong. Buyers who see accrual books can spot it.
- **It may need converting.** Many small online businesses keep cash-basis books. A buyer may ask the seller, or an accountant, to restate them on an accrual basis before agreeing a price. See [normalized earnings](/glossary/normalized-earnings/).

Which method a business may or must use depends on the country and on the size and type of business. Ask an accountant before changing methods.
